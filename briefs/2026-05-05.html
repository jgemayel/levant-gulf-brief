Syria

May 4, 2026 · Banking High impact

Central Bank of Syria opens the banking perimeter to Visa and Mastercard, sets end-May go-live for global card rails

Central Bank Governor Abdulkader Husrieh issued a binding decision authorizing licensed Syrian banks and electronic payment companies to operate with international card networks including Visa and Mastercard, with connection to the global rails expected to go live by end-May. Husrieh framed the move as a strategic step toward a more advanced digital economy and an end to years of reliance on traditional and limited tools, building on the December 2025 Visa roadmap and the September 2025 Mastercard MoU and pulling the QNB-Mastercard expansion track into operational reach.

May 4, 2026 · Society

Damascus walks back the Sunni-areas alcohol ban after a tourism-industry pushback led by Social Affairs Minister Hind Kabawat

Damascus authorities partially reversed a city-wide ban that had restricted alcohol sales in restaurants and clubs to the Christian-majority Bab Touma, Qassaa and Bab Sharqi neighbourhoods, allowing tourism-grade hotels and select restaurants to keep selling. Social Affairs Minister Hind Kabawat, the only woman and the only Christian in the Sharaa cabinet, openly criticized the original measure as a step that undermines the social and cultural diversity Syria has historically been known for, putting the cabinet's social-conservatism flank into open contestation against the reconstruction-track imperative to keep tourism investment coming in.

May 4, 2026 · Security High impact

Iran's missile and drone wave on the UAE breaks the April ceasefire, ADNOC tanker Barakah hit and Brent jumps 6 percent to USD 114

The UAE Defence Ministry confirmed it engaged 12 ballistic missiles, three cruise missiles and four drones launched from Iran on Monday, leaving three Indian nationals moderately injured at a Fujairah oil-industrial-zone fire and forcing the country's airspace and online-school posture as Iranian drones also struck the ADNOC-affiliated tanker Barakah off Oman with no cargo on board. Brent jumped 5.8 percent to settle above USD 114 a barrel and WTI gained over 4 percent to USD 106.42 as the most serious escalation since the April ceasefire ran in parallel with Project Freedom's first day, putting Iraq's federal oil-revenue line, currency reserves and post-war Hormuz-reopening trajectory directly back inside an active-conflict pricing regime.

May 4, 2026 · Energy

Kuwait crude exports stay at zero barrels in the May print, the first such halt in 35 years as the KPC force majeure runs into a fresh war cycle

Tracking data for early May confirms Kuwait's crude exports holding at zero barrels under the KPC force majeure first declared March 7 and renewed April 17, the first such complete export halt since 1991 and a structural break from the country's roughly 2.5 million barrel-per-day pre-war book. The new May 4 escalation cycle pushes any near-term lifting of the force majeure further out, with KPC having already advised customers that even a Hormuz reopening will not allow immediate full delivery against contractual obligations.

The KRG issued a directive prohibiting the export of rice, cooking oil, tea and other components of the monthly food-ration system to Iran and other regional countries, citing domestic price stabilization and food-security concerns tied to the broader regional instability following the February 28 conflict cycle.
