Syria

August 7, 2026 · Energy

Syrian electricity firm signs a 1 billion dollar Saudi solar package

Syria's state electricity company signed deals worth up to 1 billion dollars with Saudi Arabia's Al-Harafi group to build solar plants adding roughly 760 megawatts across Wadiyan al-Rabi and rural Damascus, and a deputy energy minister met a Saudi delegation on August 7 to widen technical cooperation on renewables. The push targets a power sector still delivering only a few hours a day in much of the country and leans on Gulf capital now that most sanctions have lifted.

Women's Protection Units to fold into the Interior Ministry as a final SDF hurdle clears

A member of the presidential team said the SDF integration deal has reached its final stages, with the Women's Protection Units to be absorbed into the Interior Ministry as a counterterrorism force, one of the thorniest sticking points in the January 29 accord. President Ahmad al-Sharaa cautioned that implementation is still moving slowly but reaffirmed the agreement as the only route to end the split in the northeast.

Iraq

August 8, 2026 · Energy High impact

Iran says a Hormuz reopening now hinges on the US lifting its naval blockade

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said an Oman-brokered deal on a single Strait of Hormuz corridor is very close but will not translate into an immediate reopening, adding that Iran will not clear the waterway until the US lifts its naval blockade of Iranian ports and honors the June memorandum it says Washington violated. The conditions leave Iraq, which routes about 95 percent of its crude and 90 percent of its budget through the strait, still waiting for relief even as the framework nears completion.

Bloomberg
August 8, 2026 · Energy

Iraqi crude exports fall 75 percent as Baghdad races to open overland routes

Oil Minister Hayan Abdul-Ghani said crude exports have dropped about 75 percent from a pre-war pace near 3.5 million barrels a day, with Iraq now shipping only a quarter of its usual volumes and the revenue collapse feeding a liquidity squeeze that has left public salaries unpaid. He said the ministry is accelerating overland pipeline corridors from the southern fields to the Mediterranean via Syria and Turkey, with longer-term planning toward the Red Sea through Jordan. Kuwait

August 8, 2026 · Sovereign

Fitch keeps Kuwait at AA- even as the war blows out the deficit

Fitch affirmed Kuwait at AA- with a stable outlook, citing sovereign net foreign assets near 668 percent of GDP, the highest among rated states, while warning the budget deficit could widen about 4 points to roughly 19 percent of GDP in the fiscal year ending March 2027 as the war on Iran drags on revenue. The agency assumed oil near 81.40 dollars and flagged damage to Kuwaiti energy infrastructure as a lingering risk.

The National
August 8, 2026 · Energy

Kuwaiti output climbs back toward 2 million barrels a day

Kuwaiti crude production averaged about 1.97 million barrels a day in July, up from roughly 1.65 million in June, as force majeure lifted in mid-June let more barrels move through the Gulf. The recovery comes as eight OPEC+ producers, Kuwait among them, agreed on August 2 to a further supply adjustment for September, though a full return to pre-war output still hangs on the Hormuz corridor actually opening.

Kurdistan

August 9, 2026 · Politics

Masrour Barzani presses for a new cabinet and warns against targeting the Region

Prime Minister Masrour Barzani called on the KDP and PUK to speed formation of the tenth KRG cabinet and reopen parliament, stressing that the Kurdistan Region poses no threat and should not be made a target amid regional instability. He made the remarks at the inauguration of the Balinda-Barzan water project, more than 20 months after the last parliamentary vote left power-sharing unsettled.

August 8, 2026 · Aviation

Qatar Airways restores Erbil service as Gulf air links reopen

Qatar Airways resumed flights to Erbil on August 8, alongside Kuwait and Bahrain, after suspensions tied to Iranian attacks and airspace restrictions earlier in the year. The restart is a tangible de-escalation signal for the KRG capital, which has weathered repeated drone interceptions over its skies, and reconnects Erbil to a regional hub as normal commercial traffic returns.
