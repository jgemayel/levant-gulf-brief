Syria

June 26, 2026 · Security

Islamic State attacks on Syrian government forces climb to 52 in the first half of 2026, more than triple last year's full-year tally

Islamic State attacks on Syrian government forces have risen to 52 in the first six months of 2026 from 16 in all of last year, the monitor ACLED told The National, as Damascus stretched its security apparatus thin after taking over former SDF territory in the northeast in January. The warning came days after a June 15 assault on Raqqa's main police headquarters killed two officers, and as US Central Command keeps up periodic strikes, having killed the group's Syria leader Ali Husayn al-Ulaywi on June 19.

The National
June 28, 2026 · Security

Israeli forces raid Daraa's Jamla and push again into southern Quneitra, detaining a resident in near-daily ground operations

Israeli troops raided the village of Jamla in the Daraa countryside on June 28 and carried out fresh incursions into southern Quneitra, searching homes and detaining a local resident, the latest in near-daily operations along the Golan line. Syrian state media said Israeli forces have detained 47 civilians since the December 2024 liberation and that about 80 percent of Quneitra's residents now depend on humanitarian aid, even as the wider region was consumed by a US-Iran flare-up that Damascus stayed clear of.

Iraq

June 28, 2026 · Politics High impact

Iraq detains 47 officials in an overnight Green Zone sweep, including the leader of the main Sunni bloc, in al-Zaidi's biggest anti-corruption push

Counter-Terrorism Service units raided Baghdad's fortified Green Zone overnight into June 28, detaining 47 current and former officials in the largest anti-corruption operation of Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi's two-month-old government, the state news agency INA said. Those held include Muthanna al-Samarrai, leader of the Azm Alliance and Iraq's main Sunni bloc, four Azm lawmakers, and deputy oil minister Ali Maarej, with warrants from the Central Anti-Corruption Criminal Court resting partly on confessions by a detained oil-ministry undersecretary whose case has already surfaced about 86 million dollars in seized cash.

Al Jazeera
June 28, 2026 · Energy

OPEC begins gradually restoring Iraq's quota as Baghdad denies it ever weighed leaving the group

Iraq's oil ministry said OPEC and its allies have started gradually restoring the country's production quota, days after Baghdad publicly threatened to quit the bloc, with spokesman Salim al-Rikabi denying that al-Zaidi had discussed withdrawal. As OPEC's second-largest producer, Iraq is pressing for a ceiling that matches a capacity it puts above 3 million barrels a day, arguing that postwar reconstruction needs justify a bigger share after the UAE's departure from the group.

Argus Media
June 27, 2026 · Energy

Baghdad races to triple northern pipeline exports and build a new Basra line as the weekend strikes revive the Hormuz risk

With the weekend US-Iran exchange renewing the threat to Strait of Hormuz shipping, Iraq's cabinet approved a plan to more than triple crude flows through the Kurdistan-Turkey pipeline to 770,000 barrels a day within roughly ten weeks and began work on a 700 km, 2.5 million barrel-a-day line from Basra to Haditha feeding export outlets at Baniyas, Ceyhan, and Aqaba. The drive is meant to loosen a dependence on southern Gulf terminals that still carry more than 90 percent of federal budget revenue, a vulnerability the renewed flare-up laid bare.

Kuwait

June 28, 2026 · Security High impact

Iran fires missiles at the US Ali Al Salem airbase in Kuwait, and Kuwaiti air defenses intercept two as the Gulf ceasefire wobbles

Iran fired ballistic missiles and drones at the US Ali Al Salem airbase in Kuwait on June 28 in retaliation for US strikes a day earlier on 10 Iranian military sites, and Kuwait said its air defenses intercepted two ballistic missiles with no casualties or damage. Kuwait's foreign ministry and the GCC Ministerial Council condemned the attacks, which also targeted Bahrain and Jordan, as a violation of sovereignty, and Iran threatened to halt ceasefire talks before agreeing later in the day to pause its strikes with high-stakes nuclear negotiations looming. The exchange was the sharpest escalation since the US-Iran memorandum signed earlier in June.

Al Jazeera
June 28, 2026 · Energy

Weekend escalation jolts oil markets just as Kuwait finished restoring wartime output

The strikes, alongside a US Navy move to widen a Hormuz shipping lane without Iran's oversight, injected fresh war premium into a market that had pushed Brent down toward 69 dollars, its lowest since late February, on hopes the four-month conflict was ending. The renewed risk tests Kuwait's recovery weeks after Kuwait Petroleum Corporation lifted wartime force majeure and raced crude output back toward 2.6 million barrels a day, leaving the just-reopened Gulf export channel exposed again.

Kurdistan

June 27, 2026 · Energy

Norway's DNO restarts Kurdish oilfields as the region's output recovers, even as the weekend strikes revive the drone threat

Norway's DNO will resume production at its Tawke and Peshkabur fields in Duhok as Kurdish operators bring crude back online after the wartime drone campaign, the KRG said, with the region's producers together projected to reach roughly 170,000 barrels a day as fields restart one by one. The recovery lands as the weekend US-Iran exchange reopens the threat to Kurdistan's energy sites, which absorbed hundreds of drone and missile strikes during the war and remain dependent on counter-drone defenses Erbil is still seeking from Baghdad.

Kurdistan24
June 2026 · Economy

Al-Zaidi orders regular payroll funding for Kurdistan as Erbil presses its budget-share grievance

Prime Minister al-Zaidi instructed the federal Finance Ministry to release Kurdistan Region payroll funding regularly and without discrimination after talks with KRG premier Masrour Barzani, as Erbil argued it received only about 41 percent of its entitlement, 24.3 trillion of 58.3 trillion dinars, under the 2023-25 budget framework. The region also offered to supply as much as 2,000 megawatts of electricity to the federal grid, a bargaining chip in a revenue standoff that has left more than a million civil servants short for months.
