Syria

August 27, 2026 · Finance

Visa and Mastercard go live in Syria, reconnecting the country to global card payments

Visa and Mastercard confirmed their networks can now process international card payments inside Syria for the first time in about 15 years, days after Washington removed the country from its terrorism list. President Ahmed al-Sharaa took part in the first live Visa transaction himself, paying at a restaurant in the Old City of Damascus, while Qatar's QNB and Mastercard ran what they called the first end to end international card payment and Visa settled its test through Lebanon's Fransabank. It follows the central bank's August 18 approval of a national electronic payment framework under Decision 1124 and marks one of the clearest signs yet of Syria's return to the global financial system.

The Damascus fair wraps its business days and opens to the public with Europe back in the hall

The 63rd Damascus International Fair closed its investor and delegation segment on Friday and opened to the general public on Saturday, drawing roughly 1,000 entities from about 60 countries in the largest edition since sanctions relief. European exhibitors returned in force, with Germany fielding 13 companies pitching cooperation on energy, infrastructure, transport, and medical technology, a reopening that widens the reconstruction race beyond its early Gulf energy core. The turnout tracks a broader commercial thaw, with Syria logging more than 10,500 new company registrations in the first half of 2026. Translated from SANA.

August 27, 2026 · Security

Turkey pledges to keep building the Syrian army as SDF integration is declared complete

Turkey's Defense Ministry said it will keep developing the new Syrian army's capabilities, especially in counterterrorism, with spokesman Rear Admiral Zeki Akturk vowing to sustain the momentum toward lasting stability. The pledge lands as Mazloum Abdi declares the military, security, and administrative integration of the former Syrian Democratic Forces into state institutions finished, shifting the Kurdish file to a political fight over rights in the constitution. Ankara's continued training role signals it intends to shape the force that now absorbs the northeast rather than watch from the border.

Iraq

August 28, 2026 · Security

A month from the September 30 deadline, the militia disarmament drive stalls

With five weeks left before the September 30 deadline, Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi is doubling down on his push to bring all weapons under state control, but hardline factions including Kataib Hezbollah and Harakat al-Nujaba are still refusing to hand anything over. Several groups floated freezing their activity for two years instead of disarming, an offer Washington has rejected, and the leaders insist any move follows a full withdrawal of US forces. Zaidi has tied the disarmament effort to reviving oil exports and steadying the economy, framing the arms question as the price of Iraq staying out of the regional war.

Al-Monitor
August 28, 2026 · Energy

Baghdad opens a three-month scramble to move crude around Hormuz as exports hold near 2 million barrels

Iraq's Council of Ministers approved a three-month mechanism, running from September 1, that lets specialist local and international firms market Iraqi crude through multiple outlets, a bid to keep barrels moving while the Strait of Hormuz stays throttled. The plan leans on the Kirkuk to Ceyhan line to the Mediterranean and looks ahead to the Basra to Haditha project that would feed Baniyas in Syria and Aqaba in Jordan, with crude exports averaging about 2 million barrels a day through August. It is Baghdad's near-term workaround while the bigger pipeline builds that would bypass the strait remain years off.

Kuwait

August 28, 2026 · Economy

Kuwait courts foreign investors even as the war carves a record hole in the budget

Kuwait's drive to pull in foreign capital is gaining traction, with Blackstone set to open a Kuwait office in the third quarter, KKR and partners signing a 16 billion dollar infrastructure deal with the Kuwait Oil Company, and the sovereign Kuwait Investment Authority raising a 4.5 billion dollar syndicated loan. The push runs against a brutal fiscal backdrop, with the IMF projecting the economy to contract 0.6 percent in 2026 and the government leaning on fresh borrowing and spending curbs after oil revenue cratered. Converting investor interest into sustained, job-creating projects under the Vision 2035 agenda is now the test.

Kuwait Times
August 28, 2026 · Energy

Gulf oil stays pinned between two chokepoints as Brent slips toward 88 dollars

Six months into the war, Kuwaiti crude is squeezed between the throttled Strait of Hormuz on one side and Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb risk on the other, leaving the country reliant on a shuttle trade and a partly darkened tanker fleet to keep cargoes moving. Brent eased to around 88 dollars on Friday as traders judged the standoff more an economic and sanctions fight than an imminent supply cut, helped by Gulf exports recovering to roughly two-thirds of pre-war levels and a new Iran and Oman revenue-sharing understanding over the strait. The calmer price masks how narrow Kuwait's remaining export margin still is.

Kurdistan

August 28, 2026 · Fiscal

The Erbil-Baghdad oil and salary deal stalls again despite talk of a breakthrough

Days after federal and KRG delegations opened a six-file agenda in Baghdad, the underlying oil and salary bargain has stalled, with the core standoff unchanged as the federal government presses for control of Kurdish oil revenue while Erbil insists on keeping a slice of crude for domestic use. Baghdad has told the Region to hold output near 46,000 barrels a day, with any extra volumes to be paid for and enforcement running through the same budget transfers that fund public salaries. That leaves the KRG's payroll hostage to a dispute that repeated rounds of talks have failed to close.

August 28, 2026 · Energy

Barzani orders fuel-price directives as pumps stay short across the Region

Prime Minister Masrour Barzani has ordered immediate steps to stabilize gasoline prices and shore up supply, with the KRG issuing new pricing directives to curb station-level markups as super grade petrol pushes past 2,000 dinars a liter in parts of the Region. Erbil says it is ready to negotiate a fuel arrangement with Baghdad and repeats that the 50,000 barrel daily allocation for domestic use should more than double to 126,700 barrels, its 12.67 percent population share. Until a larger allocation lands, the directives are a stopgap over a shortage the Region cannot refine its way out of.
