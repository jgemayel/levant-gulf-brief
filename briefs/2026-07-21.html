Syria

July 19, 2026 · Politics

Al-Sharaa consolidates Syria's security services under Interior Minister Khattab

President Ahmad al-Sharaa reshuffled the top of Syria's security apparatus, handing Interior Minister Anas Khattab the added post of head of the National Security Bureau that oversees the country's intelligence agencies, with Abdul Qader Tahan named to run the General Intelligence Service under Khattab's authority. Damascus framed the move as an effort to formalize command structures carried over from Idlib and to strengthen stability, concentrating oversight of the fragmented security services under one of the most powerful figures in the government.

Al Jazeera
July 20, 2026 · Politics

A year after the Suwayda bloodshed, the Druze province slips further from Damascus

One year after clashes between government-aligned Bedouin fighters and Druze factions killed more than 1,700 people, Suwayda functions largely outside state control, with Druze militias enforcing security, state symbols gone from the provincial entrance and spiritual leader Hikmat al-Hijri openly pursuing autonomy. Israel's continued military presence in the south and its support for the Druze constrain Damascus from retaking the province by force even as Washington presses Israel to withdraw as part of a wider security deal.

Foreign Policy
July 20, 2026 · Economy

Damascus sets its International Fair for late August in a bid to court investment

Syria will hold the 63rd Damascus International Fair from August 26 to September 4 under al-Sharaa's patronage, with Economy and Industry Minister Nidal al-Shaar calling it a marker of a new economic phase that will need a genuine state and private sector partnership. The revived fair is a piece of the government's drive to draw Gulf and Western capital toward reconstruction a year and a half after Assad's fall.

Iraq

July 20, 2026 · Security High impact

Houthis declare a Saudi naval blockade and tankers explode in Hormuz, sealing off Iraq's bypass routes

Yemen's Houthis declared an immediate naval blockade on Saudi Arabia on July 20, targeting the Red Sea approaches and the Yanbu terminal, hours after two tankers exploded in the Strait of Hormuz that Iran's IRGC said had used an unsafe southern corridor, on the ninth night of US strikes. Closing the Bab al-Mandeb would strip roughly 7 percent of global oil supply on top of the Hormuz shutdown and knocks out the very bypass routes Baghdad has been leaning on, deepening the collapse of Iraqi exports that normally move about 90 percent of the country's crude through the southern Gulf.

CNBC
July 20, 2026 · Energy

Iraq's brief July export rebound reverses as the war shuts the Gulf again

Oil Minister Bassem Mohammed Khudair said combined shipments through Hormuz and the Ceyhan pipeline had recovered to about 1.5 million barrels a day in early July, the largest month-on-month gain among Gulf producers, before the latest escalation cut the flow off again. That rebound sits against output that had crashed from roughly 4.2 million barrels a day in February to a low near 1.4 million in May, leaving Baghdad chasing overland and pipeline outlets to Syria and Turkey to keep barrels moving.

Arab News
July 19, 2026 · Energy

Baghdad opens talks with Tehran on routing crude through Hormuz

Iraq is in talks with Iran on ways to keep its crude moving through the Strait of Hormuz after the output drop, a pragmatic turn toward Tehran even as Baghdad publicly holds to neutrality in the war and pushes American majors deeper into its fields. The overtures underline how few options Iraq has while US strikes and the blockade throttle the southern terminals that carry the bulk of its exports.

Kuwait

July 19, 2026 · Security High impact

Iran sets a Kuwaiti power plant ablaze in a fresh wave on US Gulf allies

A Kuwaiti power plant was left ablaze on July 19 after Iran launched what it called the 17th phase of Operation Saeqeh, striking the US logistics hub at Camp Arifjan and radar and Patriot systems at Ali Al Salem Air Base, with Kuwait's military reporting damage to a second water and power plant. Tehran widened the same night's barrage to Bahrain and Jordan, hammering the Gulf states that host US bases and all but burying the memorandum meant to end the war last month.

Al Jazeera
July 19, 2026 · Energy

A drone hits a Kuwaiti offshore oil platform, wounding a worker

Kuwait's defense ministry reported a drone strike on an offshore drilling platform operated by state-owned Kuwait Petroleum Company, causing substantial damage to the rig and injuring one worker in one of the first direct hits on Gulf energy infrastructure since the ceasefire collapsed. The attack pushes the campaign past US military targets and into the oil facilities that underwrite Kuwait's budget, already strained by a projected 9.8 billion dinar deficit.

Kurdistan

July 20, 2026 · Energy

International oil firms shut in Kurdistan output as drone threats close in

Norway's DNO, the UK's Gulf Keystone and US-based HKN Energy suspended production across the Kurdistan Region as a precaution against the widening drone campaign, pulling roughly 125,000 barrels a day off line, about 45 percent of the Region's pre-war output. The shut-ins land as the northern Ceyhan route had become one of the few working arteries for Iraqi crude, stripping Erbil of hard-won leverage just as Baghdad's southern exports crater.

Al-Monitor
July 20, 2026 · Economy

Household gas prices double across the Region after the Khor Mor shutdown

The price of cooking gas across Kurdistan has doubled to about 800 dinars a liter from 400 before July 15 after Dana Gas halted the Khor Mor field over security threats, cutting some 2,500 megawatts of power generation. The KRG is trucking cylinders across the Region and reshuffling electricity distribution to head off a shortage, a direct hit to households already squeezed by delayed salaries and the wider export freeze.
