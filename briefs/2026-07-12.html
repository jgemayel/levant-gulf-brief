Syria

July 11, 2026 · Energy

Syria cuts power supply hours further as the renewed Gulf war chokes off its gas

The Ministry of Energy said it reduced electricity supply hours again after natural gas reaching power stations fell sharply, blaming the decline and intermittent pumping halts, including on the line running up through Jordan, on the emergency regional developments that have returned open war to the Gulf. Damascus has kept most of its airspace shut since late February and now leans harder on Azeri, Jordanian and Qatari gas to keep a threadbare grid running.

July 10, 2026 · Economy

As Hormuz shuts, Iraq eyes Syria as an overland route to the Mediterranean

With the Strait of Hormuz closed, Iraq is weighing ways to move more crude overland and Syria is re-emerging as a potential transit corridor, reviving talk of the long-idle Kirkuk to Baniyas line and a wider plan to route Iraqi and Gulf oil to the Mediterranean through Syrian territory. For Damascus the shift promises transit revenue and deeper integration with an Arab, Turkish and Western energy network, though the infrastructure would need years and heavy investment to carry real volumes. Iraq

July 12, 2026 · Security High impact

Iran declares the Strait of Hormuz closed and the US strikes back, severing Iraq's main oil artery

Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz closed until further notice after the Revolutionary Guard struck the Cyprus-flagged container ship GFS Galaxy for using an unapproved route, leaving one crew member missing and the vessel's engine room ablaze, and US Central Command answered with a fresh round of strikes on Iran that began late Saturday. The shutdown severs the waterway that carries most of Iraq's roughly 3.3 million barrels a day of exports, and pro-Iran factions kept up pressure on US positions around Baghdad as the country was pulled deeper into a war it has tried to sit out.

The Washington Post
July 11, 2026 · Economy

SOMO slashes Basrah prices but buyers stay away with tankers shunning the Gulf

State marketer SOMO cut official selling prices for its July Basrah cargoes, trimming Basrah Medium by 14 to 16 dollars a barrel and Basrah Heavy by as much as 18.80 dollars, yet a tender to sell July-loading crude drew little interest because traders could not secure tankers willing to enter the Gulf. Iraqi seaborne exports had already collapsed to about 260,000 barrels a day in May, a small fraction of the 2025 average, and Baghdad is now weighing northern and overland routes to bypass the strait.

Al-Zaidi carries a neutrality pitch into his July 15 Washington talks

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi heads to Washington on July 15 for talks with President Trump on turning the security relationship into an economic partnership, and he has ordered the armed forces to keep Iraqi soil from being used as a launchpad against neighbouring states as he pushes militias toward the September 30 disarmament deadline. Al-Zaidi plans to follow the trip with visits to Turkey, Iran and Saudi Arabia, casting Baghdad as a neutral channel even as the renewed war tests that line.

Kuwait

July 12, 2026 · Security High impact

Kuwait's air defences confront a fresh missile and drone wave as the Gulf is hit again

Kuwait's army said its air defences were confronting incoming missile and drone attacks early Sunday and urged residents to shelter as a fresh barrage swept the Gulf, with Qatar, the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain reporting responses to strikes of their own. The renewed wave follows the interception of two ballistic missiles and 13 drones on July 8 and a larger salvo on July 9, keeping Kuwait firmly inside the widening arc of the US-Iran war.

Gulf News
July 11, 2026 · Economy

The Hormuz shutdown puts Kuwait's hard-won oil recovery back at risk

The closure threatens to unwind Kuwait's oil rebound, just weeks after Kuwait Petroleum Corporation lifted all wartime force majeure notices on June 19 and drove output back to about 2 million barrels a day once the strait briefly reopened. During the spring blockade Kuwaiti crude exports had fallen to near zero and output slid to levels last seen after the 1990 invasion, and a prolonged shutdown would force fresh cargo deferrals and another round of production cuts.

Kurdistan

July 12, 2026 · Energy

Kurdistan's oil output climbs toward 170,000 barrels a day as HKN restarts Atrush

Kurdistan's oil output is climbing back toward 170,000 barrels a day as HKN Energy restarted the Atrush field at about 7,000 barrels a day, the latest step in a phased return that has already brought DNO's Tawke and Peshkabir, Gulf Keystone's Shaikan and Hunt Oil back on line. The recovery closes out more than two years of halted exports and hands the KRG a steadier revenue base to keep meeting public-sector salaries.

Kurdistan24
July 9, 2026 · Energy

The KRG's Ceyhan route gains weight as a Hormuz bypass while Iraq and Turkey near a pipeline deal

The Region's export lifeline runs north to Turkey's Ceyhan on the Mediterranean rather than through Hormuz, and that route gained strategic weight as the strait shut, with Iraq and Turkey close to a 12-month deal to keep crude flowing through the Kirkuk to Ceyhan pipeline. Holding the northern line open lets Baghdad and Erbil move barrels the Gulf blockade would otherwise strand, a rare piece of insulation from the war for the KRG economy.
