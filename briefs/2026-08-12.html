Syria

August 11, 2026 · Justice High impact

A Damascus court sentences Assad and eight others to death

The Fourth Criminal Court in Damascus sentenced ousted president Bashar al-Assad and eight other former regime figures to death on August 11 for premeditated murder, torture, arbitrary detention, and crimes against humanity across a nearly 14-year war that killed roughly half a million people. It is the first verdict against Assad or his inner circle since the family's five decades in power ended in December 2024, and only his cousin Atef Najib, who oversaw the 2011 Daraa crackdown that lit the uprising, stood trial in person. Assad and his brother Maher were tried in absentia from Russia, which granted them asylum and has so far rejected Damascus's extradition request, leaving the sentences largely symbolic for now.

Iraq

August 11, 2026 · Security High impact

Qaani makes a quiet Baghdad visit to steady the militias before the disarmament deadline

Quds Force commander Esmail Qaani held unannounced meetings in Baghdad with roughly 50 days left before the September 30 deadline Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi set for Iran-aligned factions to surrender their weapons or face terrorism charges. According to Shafaq News, Qaani told the groups to reject any handover at this stage while avoiding an armed clash with the state and to seek a political settlement instead, a message that underscores how far Baghdad still is from bringing the arsenals under state control. Only three armed groups have folded into the security forces so far, and Kataib Hezbollah has flatly refused to disarm as the US-led coalition prepares to end its mission.

Baghdad weighs deeper southern export cuts and fast-tracks overland routes

With talks on reopening the Strait of Hormuz stalled, Iraq's Oil Ministry is looking at trimming crude flows from the Basra fields that account for about 80 percent of national output and is accelerating overland export corridors, prioritizing a Mediterranean route through Syria and Turkey and longer-term planning toward the Red Sea via Jordan. The pivot leans hard on the northern Kirkuk-Ceyhan line as the country's most secure outlet while the southern terminals stay throttled by the closure. See the Kurdistan section.

Kuwait

August 11, 2026 · Security

Kuwait's cabinet approves a drone control law after the Iranian strikes

The Council of Ministers approved a draft decree-law on August 11 regulating drones, unmanned aircraft, and light sport aircraft, tightening the ground rules for airspace after waves of Iranian drones struck Kuwaiti infrastructure, including the Abdali crossing with Iraq, power stations, and desalination plants earlier in the war. The same session declared Thursday, August 27 an official holiday and reviewed the security situation, part of a steady hardening of civil-defense measures as the regional conflict drags on.

Kurdistan

August 11, 2026 · Energy

Khor Mor's suspension strips roughly 2,500 megawatts from Kurdistan's grid

Dana Gas suspended operations at all major production facilities in the Khor Mor field, citing credible security threats and the deteriorating regional picture, knocking about 2,500 megawatts off power generation and deepening the blackout across the Kurdistan Region. Iraq's Oil Ministry began trucking daily liquefied petroleum gas into the region to keep household cooking gas flowing, while Baghdad warned the shutdown could open a 1,400 megawatt deficit in federal grids that feed on Kirkuk. The stoppage follows the KRG's dispute with the operators over gas the companies had begun routing to Baghdad, which Erbil called a contractual breach.

August 11, 2026 · Politics

Turkey's parliament clears a conditional pardon for PKK fighters

Turkey's parliament voted overwhelmingly to approve a conditional pardon for thousands of Kurdistan Workers' Party members, a milestone in the peace track that opened after the PKK announced last year it would lay down its weapons. The move carries direct weight for the Kurdistan Region, where the group's Qandil strongholds and recurring Turkish operations have long shaped the border, and where Erbil has pushed for the fighting near its territory to wind down. Kurdish communities greeted the vote with a mix of relief and doubt over how far the amnesty will reach.
