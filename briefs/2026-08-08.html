Syria

August 6, 2026 · Security

SDF integration passes 70 percent as education and judicial files stall

Syria's presidential team said folding the Kurdish-led SDF into state institutions is now roughly 70 percent complete at the military and security levels, after President Ahmad al-Sharaa's August 4 talks with commander Mazloum Abdi and senior Rojava official Elham Ahmed. The unfinished work sits in education and the judiciary, where curricula, recognition of Autonomous Administration certificates and the future of teaching staff remain unsettled.

Enab Baladi
August 6, 2026 · Economy

Damascus pitches a Four Seas plan to wire itself back into regional trade

Syria is promoting a Four Seas initiative to rebuild its role as a transit hub, linking the Gulf, Iraq and Central Asia to Turkey and Europe through the Mediterranean via an integrated network of railways, roads, ports, power and telecoms. Officials cast it as a spine of reconstruction, arriving as the IMF projects double-digit 2026 growth and the World Bank signs off on the reform program.

August 6, 2026 · Finance

Central bank extends the window to swap out Assad-era pounds

The Central Bank of Syria pushed its deadline for exchanging withdrawn Assad-era banknotes to August 6, giving holders more time to convert legacy cash as the overhaul that stripped the old notes of legal tender is finalized. The extension eases the squeeze on Syrians still sitting on old paper while the new currency beds in.

Iraq

August 6, 2026 · Energy High impact

Iran and Oman strike a Hormuz shipping deal, and up to 7 percent transit fees enrage shipowners

Iran said it reached an agreement with Oman on a single commercial corridor through the Strait of Hormuz, with vessels entering along the Iranian coast and exiting past Oman and a joint statement now in final drafting. The framework would levy transit fees of up to 7 percent of cargo value and reportedly leaves the waterway under Iranian control, drawing sharp protest from global shipowners. For Iraq, which moves about 94 percent of its crude and close to 90 percent of its budget through the strait, a workable reopening is the fastest path back to normal flows.

Fortune
August 7, 2026 · Public Finance

Baghdad concedes a liquidity crisis as unpaid salaries spread across ministries

A government spokesman confirmed Iraq is in a liquidity crisis, citing a large gap between revenue and the monthly bill for wages and services after staff across five ministries and the three presidencies went without July pay. Analysts warn the arrears, driven by the Hormuz-led collapse in oil income, could tip into wider unrest, with many contract employees earning just 300,000 dinars a month.

The Jerusalem Post
August 6, 2026 · Energy

Only about 30 million barrels of Iraqi crude crossed Hormuz in a month

Roughly 30 million barrels of Iraqi crude passed through the Strait of Hormuz between July 4 and August 4, tanker-tracking data show, a fraction of the roughly 105 million barrels a month Iraq shipped before the war. The shortfall has cut monthly oil receipts from as much as 8 billion dollars to barely 1.5 billion, hollowing out the federal budget.

August 6, 2026 · Energy

Iraq and Kuwait face a tougher oil restart than their Gulf neighbors

Even with a Hormuz deal taking shape, Iraq and Kuwait will claw back full output more slowly than other Gulf producers, analysts say, held down by shut-in wells, aging equipment, difficult geology and war damage to storage and terminals. Kuwait, which has no pipeline to bypass the strait and saw its Mina al-Ahmadi and Mina Abdullah refineries hit during the war, is among the most exposed. See the Iraq section.

Kurdistan

August 6, 2026 · Security

A Turkish PKK disarmament bill heads to parliament, easing the threat of strikes on KRG soil

Turkey submitted a framework law to parliament setting out the legal machinery for the PKK's dissolution, including deferral of prosecutions for members, the most concrete step yet toward ending a four-decade conflict. For the Kurdistan Region, whose territory has absorbed repeated Turkish airstrikes on PKK positions, a durable deal would sharply cut cross-border military pressure. The draft carries 360 signatures but needs 400 votes or a referendum to pass.

Eurasia Review
August 6, 2026 · Economy

KRG warns its monthly revenue has collapsed more than 70 percent

The KRG cabinet under Prime Minister Masrour Barzani reviewed a finance ministry report showing the Region's own revenue for the first five months of 2026 fell more than 70 percent against the same period in 2025, blaming conflict, regional instability and the still-incomplete ASYCODA customs system at border crossings. Ministers tied the collapse to the stalled oil-export file and strained coordination with Baghdad over salaries and budget share.
