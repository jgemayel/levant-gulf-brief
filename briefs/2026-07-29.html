Syria

July 28, 2026 · Security

Israeli armor pushes a fresh incursion into Syria's Daraa countryside

Israeli troops advanced Tuesday from the Tal Abu al-Ghaithar area into the outskirts of Wadi al-Raqad in the western Daraa countryside, with two tanks and a military vehicle entering the zone and opening fire, though no casualties were reported. The move followed a day of raids across Quneitra and Daraa villages including Taranja, al-Aridah and Maariyah, part of near-daily violations of the 1974 disengagement line that Damascus keeps pressing the UN to halt.

Damascus and Cairo move to revive their joint business council

Economy and Industry Minister Nidal al-Shaar met Egyptian charge d'affaires Mohamed el-Feki in Damascus to discuss reactivating the Syrian-Egyptian Business Council and widening trade, investment and industrial cooperation between the two private sectors. The push tracks al-Sharaa's drive to reconnect Syria to regional markets as sanctions fall, with Cairo also preparing to restart direct flights to Damascus. Translated from SANA.

Syria stands up an IMF-backed fiscal unit to frame the 2027 budget

Finance Minister Mohammed Yisr Barnieh announced a new Macroeconomic and Public Finance Policy Unit built with IMF cooperation, tasked first with drafting a medium-term fiscal framework to guide the 2027 budget. The step signals Damascus is trying to rebuild credible economic institutions as it forecasts growth near 10 percent this year and courts reconstruction financing after the World Bank pegged rebuild needs around 216 billion dollars.

Iraq

July 29, 2026 · Energy High impact

Iraq and Turkey seal a "historic" energy alliance as Erdogan eyes 1 million barrels a day

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi and President Recep Tayyip Erdogan agreed in Ankara to build a broad energy alliance, with Erdogan saying Turkey could draw up to 1 million barrels a day of Iraqi crude and state firm TPAO set to take a 15 percent stake in BP's Kirkuk operating company. The two also vowed to fast-track the Development Road corridor linking the Grand Faw Port to Turkey and Europe, cementing Ankara as Baghdad's main export outlet while the Strait of Hormuz keeps southern shipments crippled.

Al Jazeera
July 29, 2026 · Oil & Gas

Iraq's May and June crude exports sink to 526,000 barrels a day as Hormuz bites

The oil ministry said Iraq shipped 32.1 million barrels over May and June for 2.33 billion dollars, averaging about 526,000 barrels a day against a normal 100 million barrels a month, with Basra moving 20.23 million barrels and the north sending 10.31 million of Kirkuk crude plus 1.56 million from Kurdistan through Ceyhan. The collapse underlines how the Hormuz shutdown has pushed nearly all of Iraq's working exports onto the northern Turkish route now being expanded.

Shafaq News
July 28, 2026 · Domestic

A war-driven fuel shortage grips Iraq as diesel lines lengthen in Baghdad

A diesel shortage worsened across Baghdad and several provinces as queues outside stations grew, driven by summer generator demand and reduced runs at several refineries. The squeeze is the federal face of a nationwide crisis that the Iran war has sharpened, with the north hit hardest as security threats cut gas feedstock to power plants.

Kuwait

July 28, 2026 · Security

Kuwait's cabinet condemns Iranian drone strikes on the Abdali crossing

The cabinet issued its strongest condemnation of what it called the aggression by Iran and its proxies that struck the Abdali border crossing with Iraq, calling it a violation of Kuwaiti sovereignty and UN Security Council Resolution 2817. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman phoned Kuwait's leadership in solidarity, and ministers also condemned a fresh wave of drone attacks on Jordan as the Gulf hardens its posture through the Iran war.

July 28, 2026 · Economy

Falling oil income drives Kuwait's deficit toward 9.8 billion dinars

Kuwait projects its 2026-27 fiscal deficit to widen sharply to about 9.8 billion dinars, roughly 32 billion dollars and up almost 55 percent on the prior year, as oil revenue slides with output capped by the Hormuz shutdown. Forecasters see the economy contracting anywhere from 4.2 percent on the IMF's read to double digits, with no pipeline to bypass the strait leaving Kuwait uniquely exposed among Gulf producers.

Kurdistan

July 28, 2026 · Energy

Khor Mor climbs back to 750 million cubic feet a day, restoring 24-hour power

Dana Gas has returned the Khor Mor field to its full 750 million cubic feet a day, clearing the way for the KRG to restore round-the-clock electricity under the Runaki program that serves more than 4.5 million people, about 70 percent of the Region. The recovery reverses a shutdown that had stripped up to 80 percent of Kurdistan's power and put the grid back on a stable footing within days of the gradual restart.

Channel 8
July 28, 2026 · Domestic

Overnight gas lines still grip Erbil as the fuel crisis lingers

Drivers in Erbil queued for hours, some overnight, to buy gasoline as most private stations stayed shut and only a handful of government pumps sold fuel, with families capped at one 40-liter fill twice a week. The shortage traces to the war-driven cut at Khor Mor and refinery strains, and is easing only slowly even as the power grid stabilizes.

July 28, 2026 · Fiscal

KRG approves electricity debt discounts as it moves to curb fuel prices

The Ministry of Electricity approved discounts of up to 50 percent for residential and agricultural customers, and 25 percent for industrial, commercial and public bodies, on electricity debts run up before the Runaki rollout, aiming to recover revenue for the sector. The cabinet paired the move with steps to rein in fuel prices as the Region works to end the gasoline crunch and hold its 24-hour supply.
