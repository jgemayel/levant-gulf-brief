Syria

August 23, 2026 · Diplomacy High impact

Syria says the door is open to resuming security talks with Israel

In a Reuters interview in Damascus, Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shibani said Syria expects security talks with Israel to resume soon and urged Israel to seize what he called a historic opportunity for diplomacy, while conceding it is very hard to bet on Israel. The overture lands days after Israeli jets cratered the runway at the Abu al-Duhur airbase in Idlib on August 18, a strike Israel justified by claiming Damascus was about to let Turkish forces deploy there, a charge both Syria and Turkey deny. Shibani said halting Israeli attacks and building confidence come first, with Washington still pressing a security deal between Israel and President Ahmed al-Sharaa's government.

Iraq

August 23, 2026 · Economy High impact

Iraq's finance minister heads to parliament as the deficit balloons and salaries wobble

Finance Minister Faleh al-Sari is set to appear before parliament to explain a fiscal crisis that has swung Iraq from surplus to a first-half deficit of roughly 21 trillion dinars, with revenue near 36 trillion against spending of almost 57 trillion, a gap that has tripled year on year to about 16 billion dollars. The shortfall traces to the months-long disruption of southern crude exports through the Strait of Hormuz, which has forced repeated public-sector salary delays, and officials warn pay may have to move to a 45-day cycle if exports do not recover. Parliament's finance committee met the central bank governor on August 23 to weigh payroll delays, inflation, and foreign reserves.

Shafaq News
August 22, 2026 · Energy

Buyers keep Basra crude moving through Hormuz, making Iran's permits redundant

Economist Ziyad al-Hashemi said international buyers have expanded liftings of Iraqi crude through the Strait of Hormuz on their own, arranging safe passage and shouldering the shipping risk days before Tehran publicized special transit permits, which he described as a late public-relations gesture rather than a logistical breakthrough. Abu Dhabi's ADNOC trading arm has been shuttling Basra crude through the strait in short runs with transponders switched off before transferring cargoes to other ships outside the Gulf, keeping barrels flowing despite the standoff that is starving the federal budget.

Kuwait

August 23, 2026 · Domestic

Kuwait forms nationwide teams to clear bachelors from family housing zones

Kuwait Municipality has set up field teams across all governorates to identify single male workers, so-called bachelors, living in private and model residential districts zoned for families, coordinating with the interior, electricity and water, manpower, and environment authorities to issue violations and evictions. The renewed drive reopens a long-running friction point in the housing market for the country's large expatriate workforce, which makes up most of the population.

Residency and traffic sweep nets dozens of violators

Interior Ministry patrols detained 27 expatriates for expired residencies and recorded some 1,300 traffic violations in a single enforcement sweep, part of a steady tempo of security campaigns in Farwaniya and Khaitan that has also rounded up dozens of wanted persons this week. The push runs alongside a civil-information drive that struck hundreds of stale addresses from the national registry and gave residents 30 days to re-register or face a 100 dinar fine.

Kurdistan

August 23, 2026 · Economy

Baghdad's cash crunch lands on Kurdistan's payroll for over a million workers

The federal fiscal crisis now squeezing Baghdad is landing directly on the Kurdistan Region, where the federal treasury funds monthly salaries for more than a million public servants. Even as the deficit balloons and Baghdad weighs stretching pay to a 45-day cycle, federal officials have committed to keep disbursing the Region's salaries through the end of the year, while again pressing Erbil over payroll transparency, duplicate names, and biometric registration. Erbil, which forwards a large share of its non-oil revenue to Baghdad under the oil-for-budget arrangement, is owed roughly 21 billion dollars in salary arrears built up over a decade of disputes.
