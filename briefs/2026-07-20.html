Syria

July 19, 2026 · Politics

Al-Sharaa overhauls Syria's security and intelligence command

President Ahmed al-Sharaa reshuffled Syria's security leadership on July 19, handing Interior Minister Anas Khattab the added post of head of the National Security Bureau that oversees the country's intelligence agencies, while naming Hussein al-Salamah his deputy, Mulham al-Shantout assistant interior minister for security affairs and Abdul Qader Tahan head of the General Intelligence Service. Damascus framed the move as an effort to tighten control of the services, steady the transition and clear obstacles to reconstruction, coming days after authorities said they had rounded up the cell behind the bombings that struck the capital during President Macron's visit.

July 18, 2026 · Economy

Iraqi oil rerouted overland turns Syria into the region's top fuel oil exporter

Iraq's scramble to move barrels around the blockaded Strait of Hormuz has made Syria the Middle East's biggest fuel oil exporter, with the country shipping about 720,000 tonnes in June, roughly 28 percent of regional volumes, according to analytics firm Vortexa. Thousands of tanker trucks, each carrying around 20 tonnes on a four to six day run from Iraqi fields to Mediterranean ports, are rebuilding Syria as a transit hub barely 18 months after Assad's fall. Iraq

July 18, 2026 · Energy

Iraq seals a 60 billion dollar US package and revives the Kirkuk to Baniyas pipeline

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi closed his Washington visit with about 50 agreements worth more than 60 billion dollars, headlined by memoranda to rebuild the long-defunct Kirkuk to Baniyas crude pipeline to Syria's Mediterranean coast, a line the State Department says a US-led consortium including Chevron would restore to an initial 2 million barrels a day. The package draws American majors deeper into Iraqi energy and hands Baghdad another way around the Strait of Hormuz, which normally carries about 90 percent of its 3.4 million barrels a day of exports. July 18, 2026 · Security

A drone strikes a militia position near Kirkuk as the war spills onto Iraqi soil

A drone hit a Popular Mobilization Forces position in the Al-Dibis area northwest of Kirkuk on July 18, Shafaq News reported, the latest strike to land on Iraqi ground as US and Iranian forces trade fire across the region. Iraqi air defenses have downed several drones along the Kirkuk to Sulaymaniyah corridor in recent days, and Baghdad, which insists it is neutral in the war, is struggling to keep its territory and its armed factions clear of the crossfire.

Kuwait

July 18, 2026 · Security High impact

Iran strikes a second Kuwaiti desalination plant, killing a worker

Iran hit a second Kuwaiti power and water desalination plant on July 18, a day after striking another such site, sparking fires, taking several generation units offline and killing one worker in the first death from the assault on the emirate's civilian infrastructure. With about 90 percent of Kuwait's drinking water drawn from desalination, the back to back hits came after a seventh straight night of US strikes on Iran and forced Kuwait to trigger emergency measures to hold its grid together.

July 18, 2026 · Energy

Repeated strikes hit a Kuwaiti oil facility and wound workers

The Kuwait Petroleum Corporation said on July 18 that repeated Iranian attacks struck a vital oil facility, wounding several workers and causing heavy material damage, with strikes reported to have set the North Pier crude export berth at the Mina al-Ahmadi hub ablaze. The hit on Kuwait's export machinery, with Brent holding near 88 dollars, threatens the oil income the emirate is already watching shrink under the war.

Kurdistan

July 18, 2026 · Energy

US firm HKN Energy halts all its Kurdistan operations

US energy firm HKN Energy has shut down all of its operations in the Kurdistan Region, an executive told Rudaw on July 18, citing the escalating US-Iran confrontation even after the company's recent deal with Baghdad to help develop a northern field. HKN operates the Sarsang block in Duhok, where it holds a 62 percent stake, and part of the Atrush block, and its exit deepens a foreign pullback that is draining the Region of both output and investor confidence.

July 18, 2026 · Economy

The Khor Mor shutdown strips most of the Region's power and doubles household gas prices

Household gas prices across the Kurdistan Region have doubled, with LPG reaching about 800 dinars a liter, after UAE-based Dana Gas suspended its Khor Mor field over credible security threats and pulled roughly 2,500 to 3,000 megawatts, up to 80 percent of the Region's electricity, off the grid. The KRG has rushed emergency fuel to blunt a household gas crisis across Erbil, Sulaymaniyah and Duhok as repeated drone attacks keep its main energy lifeline under threat.
