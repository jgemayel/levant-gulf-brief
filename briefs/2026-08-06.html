Al-Sharaa and SDF commander Abdi meet to drive integration into its final phase

Syrian President Ahmad al-Sharaa met SDF General Commander Mazloum Abdi at the People's Palace on Tuesday, with Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani present, to map the next phase of the January 29 integration accord. IMF projects Syria's economy will grow more than 10 percent in 2026

An IMF mission chief statement issued Tuesday projected the Syrian economy will expand by more than 10 percent in 2026 and keep growing strongly in 2027, citing recovery in agriculture, oil and gas output, electricity and trade after most sanctions were lifted. The assessment followed a July 19 to 23 visit to Damascus where the team met Finance Minister Mohammed Yisr Barnieh and Central Bank Governor Safwat Raslan.

Iraq

August 5, 2026 · Energy High impact

Iran and Oman agree on coordinates for a single Hormuz shipping corridor

Iran and Oman have agreed on the geographic coordinates of a single commercial shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz, Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said Wednesday, a corridor that would replace the temporary northern and southern lanes for an initial two to four months. Tehran cautioned the framework is not a full reopening and tied it to the United States lifting its naval blockade, even as President Trump said the strait would reopen soon. About 94 percent of Iraq's crude exports and roughly 90 percent of its budget ride on the waterway, making a deal that frees the strait the fastest path back to normal flows.

Gulf News
August 5, 2026 · Energy

Iraq's grid buckles at peak summer as Iranian gas deliveries collapse

Iraq is struggling to keep the power on at the height of summer as Iranian gas that feeds about a third of its plants falls to 15 to 20 million cubic meters a day, against the roughly 50 million it needs. Peak demand near 40 gigawatts is running well above the 29 gigawatts the grid can supply, and a Gulf interconnection meant to close the gap has slipped to at least the end of August.

Baghdad payroll strains as electricity staff protest unpaid July wages

Employees of Iraq's Electricity Ministry protested in Baghdad on Wednesday over unpaid July salaries, saying staff across five ministries, the three presidencies and several agencies had still not been paid. The delays show how the Hormuz-driven collapse in oil revenue, which supplies close to 90 percent of the federal budget, is now reaching into the state payroll.

Kuwait, most exposed to Hormuz, is poised to ramp crude once the corridor opens

Kuwait, the Gulf producer with no pipeline to bypass the Strait of Hormuz, stands to gain the most from the Iran-Oman corridor framework now taking shape.
