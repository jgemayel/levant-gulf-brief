Syria

August 14, 2026 · Justice

Damascus and Baghdad set up joint courts for thousands of transferred ISIS detainees

Syria and Iraq agreed to form joint judicial committees to review the cases of ISIS prisoners moved from northeast Syrian detention centers to Iraqi jails, after Iraqi Supreme Judicial Council chief Faiq Zaidan met Syrian Justice Minister Mazhar al-Wais in Damascus. About 5,700 detainees were transferred earlier this year, and both governments want them returned to Syria, with the main obstacle being the lack of secure prison space on the Syrian side. The technical teams will meet regularly to work through the trials, tying Damascus and Baghdad into a shared counterterrorism track. See the Iraq section.

The National
August 16, 2026 · Energy

State oil output creeps back as Damascus consolidates the recaptured northeast fields

With the al-Omar field, Syria's largest, and the Conoco gas complex now under government control through the SDF integration, the Syrian Petroleum Company says crude output has climbed back to around 26,000 barrels per day and could reach 45,000 within months. Officials project roughly 100,000 barrels per day within four months as maintenance proceeds, with ConocoPhillips, Dana Gas, and Novaterra among the firms holding memoranda to redevelop fields. The ramp-up is modest against pre-war levels but marks the first real recovery for a sector hollowed out by more than a decade of war and sanctions.

Iraq

August 17, 2026 · Security High impact

The 60-day US-Iran deadline lapses with no Hormuz deal and a Trump threat to bomb Oman

The 60-day deadline set by the June Versailles deal expired on August 17 with Washington and Tehran further apart than when they signed, no compromise on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and detailed nuclear talks never begun. President Trump warned the United States would strike Oman if it "gets in the way" in the strait, while Iran's foreign ministry ruled out extending the interim understanding. Traffic through the waterway sat near a standstill, with three vessels crossing on Sunday against a five-day average of 12, and Brent traded near 89 dollars a barrel, keeping Iraq's Gulf export lifeline throttled. The Washington Post
August 16, 2026 · Energy

Iraq claws August exports back to 2 million barrels a day, the highest since the war began

Oil Minister Bassem Mohammed Khudair said crude exports have averaged 2 million barrels per day since the start of August, the first time that rate has been reached since the February 28 crisis shut much of the strait. Basra crude shipments are seen near 2.93 million barrels per day for the month, and SOMO has lifted its September loading allocation to 3.017 million, as buyers increasingly shoulder the shipping risk themselves. Basra Oil Company's chief said Iraq could restore pre-war export levels within a week if Hormuz reopens.

Kuwait

August 17, 2026 · Energy

Gulf output rebounds but stays far below pre-war levels as the strait stays shut

The IEA's August report showed Gulf crude production rose 2.5 million barrels per day in July to 23.9 million, still 8.3 million below pre-war levels as producers route around the closed Strait of Hormuz. Kuwait, whose barrels have no outlet except the Gulf, remains among the states with capacity bottled up behind the blockade, and the lapse of the US-Iran deadline on August 17 leaves no near-term path to reopening. The squeeze lands even as Kuwait Petroleum Corporation booked a three-year-high profit for the fiscal year just closed. See the Iraq section.

Kurdistan

August 17, 2026 · Security High impact

Iranian drones strike Barzani's private office and the intelligence chief's home

Two explosive-laden Hadid-110 drones launched from Iran at about 12:28 a.m. on Monday, August 17 hit the private office of KRG Prime Minister Masrour Barzani in Pirmam and the residence of the Parastin intelligence chief, causing no casualties. Barzani called the attack "a dangerous escalation" and "illegitimate and unacceptable," Baghdad asked Tehran for clarification, and Iran denied responsibility and floated a "false flag." A direct strike on the head of the Region's government marks the sharpest turn yet in a campaign that has repeatedly hit Erbil and the Region's energy and border infrastructure. See the Iraq section.

The National
August 14, 2026 · Security

Coalition guns down three bomb-laden drones over Erbil as the tally passes 950 strikes

Coalition air defenses intercepted and destroyed three explosive drones over Erbil on August 14, days before the strike on the prime minister's office, with no casualties reported. The Kurdistan Region has now logged more than 950 drone and missile attacks launched from Iran or by Iran-aligned militias inside Iraq since the war began in late February, killing 32 people and wounding 154. The steady bombardment has battered oil fields, power plants, and border crossings and hardened Erbil's insistence that it will not be pulled into a war it did not choose.

August 16, 2026 · Economy

KRG presses Baghdad for August and September salaries while meeting its export quota

The Kurdistan Regional Government urged Baghdad to release the August and September public-sector salaries, arguing it has met its side of the tripartite oil deal by delivering an average of 230,000 barrels per day to SOMO and transferring 120 billion dinars a month in non-oil revenue. Baghdad sent only about 41.6 percent of the Region's entitlements over 2023 to 2025, and the payment squeeze has deepened as the war throttles trade and Khor Mor's shutdown keeps roughly 2,500 megawatts off the grid. The dispute keeps hundreds of thousands of salaries hostage to the monthly transfer fight. See the Iraq section.
