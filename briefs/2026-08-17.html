Syria

August 15, 2026 · Politics

SDF integration reaches its final military stages as brigades take shape

The presidential team overseeing the January 29 accord says the folding of the Syrian Democratic Forces into state institutions has reached its final stages on the military and security tracks, with a phased plan forming three new brigades in Hasakah and one in Ain al-Arab. Commander Mazloum Abdi and President Ahmed al-Sharaa met again at the People's Palace to push the remaining steps, though the civilian files covering courts, schools, and local administration still trail the armed merger. The northeast is now half-integrated, its military structures dissolving faster than the parallel administration that governed the Jazira.

Enab Baladi
August 16, 2026 · Economy

Al-Sharaa puts reconstruction as high as 900 billion dollars and presses for full sanctions relief

President Ahmed al-Sharaa put the cost of rebuilding Syria at up to 900 billion dollars and argued that the sanctions still on the books choke reconstruction and keep displaced Syrians from going home. Damascus says it drew 28 billion dollars of investment in 2025, including 14 billion in memoranda signed at an August 6 ceremony, while the UN estimates 16.5 million Syrians, nearly 70 percent of the population, still need aid. The World Bank has pegged core reconstruction needs closer to 216 billion dollars, a gap that frames the government's push for sanctions to come off in full.

Iraq

August 16, 2026 · Energy

Iraqi crude exports rebound to a wartime high as buyers take on the Hormuz risk

Iraq's crude exports have climbed back to around 2 million barrels per day this month, the highest level since the February 28 outbreak of the US-Iran war, as buyers increasingly shoulder the risk of shipping through the Strait of Hormuz themselves. SOMO chief Ali Nizar said the rebound followed deep discounts, with Basra Medium marked down 25 to 27 dollars a barrel and heavier grades cut close to 30 dollars below benchmark. Volumes remain well under the roughly 3.4 million barrels per day Iraq moved before Iran throttled the strait, leaving the budget still short.

The New Arab
August 15, 2026 · Politics

Baghdad lobbies Washington and Tehran for a clear pass through Hormuz

Baghdad is negotiating with both Washington and Tehran to secure an exemption that would let Iraqi crude move through the Strait of Hormuz unimpeded, with lawmakers pressing Iran-aligned factions to lobby Tehran directly. Iraq relies on oil for more than 90 percent of its budget and has seen revenue squeezed hard enough to delay state salaries across ministries. The diplomacy runs alongside a drive to build overland pipeline routes to the Mediterranean through Syria and Turkey, and a KRG offer to carry more southern crude north to Ceyhan. Kuwait

August 16, 2026 · Energy High impact

Kuwait eyes a Saudi bypass pipeline as Hormuz closure freezes its exports

Oil Minister Tariq al-Roumi said Kuwait is in talks with Saudi Arabia and other Arab states to build a new crude export pipeline that would bypass the Strait of Hormuz, after the waterway's effective closure brought Kuwaiti seaborne exports to a near standstill. Officials say rebuilding oil storage tanks damaged in Iranian strikes will take at least a year, and the military has intercepted Iranian drones entering Kuwaiti airspace. Kuwait also condemned the attack on two ADNOC tankers transiting the strait, one of three strikes on the company's vessels inside 48 hours.

Arab Times
August 15, 2026 · Markets

IEA warns global oil stocks are draining as Hormuz stays shut

The International Energy Agency warned that global oil inventories are draining fast as the Hormuz disruption drags on, cutting its supply outlook and noting regional exports have slipped toward 12 million barrels per day from about 15 million in July. Kuwait, the Gulf producer most dependent on the strait, has been among the hardest hit, with output far below its pre-war level near 2.5 million barrels per day. The agency flagged rising risk of a price spike if the passage stays closed into the autumn, even as Brent has drifted near 87 dollars a barrel.

Kurdistan

August 15, 2026 · Economy

A fresh Erbil-Baghdad round tackles frozen salaries and stalled oil exports

Erbil and Baghdad held a fresh round of talks on restarting Kurdistan's oil exports and releasing withheld public-sector salaries, with the KRG Council of Ministers reviewing export volumes, non-oil revenue, and payroll. The core dispute remains the volume Erbil must hand federal marketer SOMO, with Baghdad demanding 400,000 barrels per day and the KRG offering an initial 280,000, while roughly two months of pay for more than a million employees stays frozen. The federal finance ministry cut budget transfers in late May over the export and revenue gap, and security guarantees for the region's fields are now part of the file.

August 16, 2026 · Energy

KRG offers its Ceyhan route to lift Iraq's exports around the shut strait

Prime Minister Masrour Barzani said the Kurdistan Region is ready to raise Iraq's oil exports by sending more crude north through its pipeline to the Turkish port of Ceyhan, offering Baghdad a route that skirts the closed Strait of Hormuz. Regional production has held near 250,000 barrels per day even after exports to Iran stopped, with much of the flow now redirected toward Turkey.
