Syria

August 10, 2026 · Politics

Presidential team says the SDF merger is in its final stages while the sovereignty files stay stuck

Mustafa Abdi, a member of the presidential team implementing the January 29 integration deal, told state broadcaster al-Ikhbariya that the folding of the SDF into Syrian state institutions has reached its final stages at all levels, above all the military and security tracks, fueling talk that a formal dissolution of the force and the Autonomous Administration is near. The claim sits against a slower reality, with the judiciary, education, universities, the civil registry, and public-employee files all still snagged on questions of authority and local administration.

August 10, 2026 · Energy

Syria signs about a billion dollars in Saudi solar deals to rebuild its power sector

The Syrian Electricity Company signed three solar generation projects worth up to 1 billion dollars with Saudi Arabia's Al-Harbi Contracting, part of a widening Riyadh-Damascus energy push that also has Saudi teams studying grid rehabilitation and renewable capacity. The deals extend a run of Gulf-backed investment aimed at pulling Syria's crippled electricity network off a few hours of daily supply as sanctions relief opens the door to foreign money.

August 10, 2026 · Diplomacy

Al-Sharaa hosts the UN refugee chief in Damascus to push voluntary returns

President Ahmad al-Sharaa received the UN High Commissioner for Refugees and his delegation at the People's Palace on August 10, with the talks centered on deepening cooperation with UNHCR and supporting the voluntary, safe, and dignified return of Syrians displaced by the war. The meeting underlines how central refugee return has become to Damascus's recovery pitch as it courts reconstruction financing.

Iraq

August 7, 2026 · Energy

Iraq wins a US waiver to sell fuel oil stranded on a sanctioned tanker

State marketer SOMO issued a tender to sell 268,131 tonnes of straight-run high-sulfur fuel oil held aboard the US-sanctioned floater Hellstugutinden after securing an OFAC waiver clearing the sale, with cargoes to be lifted in the Khor al-Zubair lightering area and bids closing August 11. The scramble to monetize stranded barrels shows how far Baghdad is going to claw back revenue while the Hormuz closure keeps most of its crude bottled up.

August 8, 2026 · Energy

Iran weighs an interim Hormuz corridor but keeps a full reopening tied to Washington

Tehran is deliberating over an Oman-brokered arrangement that would run a roughly 60-day interim corridor through the strait, with inbound traffic in Iranian waters and outbound traffic in Omani waters, but Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi says any full reopening still hinges on US compliance with the June memorandum Iran says was violated. Until the terms firm up, Iraq keeps leaning on the northern pipeline to move barrels around the chokepoint. See the Kurdistan section.

Kuwait

August 10, 2026 · Government

Kuwait's cabinet moves to make its national airline a fully state-owned company

The cabinet approved a draft decree-law to turn Kuwait Airways into a fully state-owned shareholding company and referred it to Amir Sheikh Meshal for final approval, part of a push to overhaul loss-making state assets. In the same session the finance minister presented a first-half 2026 sovereign-rating review affirming Kuwait's investment-grade standing, days after Fitch held the country at AA- on its exceptional fiscal buffers.

Kurdistan

August 11, 2026 · Energy High impact

Erbil clears crude to flow north through its pipeline as Hormuz stays shut

Prime Minister Masrour Barzani approved Baghdad's request to export Iraqi crude through the Kurdistan Region's pipeline to Turkey's Ceyhan port, saying the region signed off in light of the extraordinary circumstances facing the country, with Oil Minister Hayan Abdul-Ghani telling Rudaw the deal takes effect Wednesday morning. Available volumes from both KRG fields and Kirkuk will move together up the line, the clearest workaround yet to a Hormuz closure that has cut southern shipments by about three quarters and drained the revenue funding close to 90 percent of the federal budget.
