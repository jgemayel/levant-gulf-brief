Syria

July 7, 2026 · Diplomacy High impact

Macron becomes the first Western leader in Damascus since Assad fell, restoring full ties and signing reconstruction deals

President Emmanuel Macron met Ahmad al-Sharaa at the People's Palace on July 7, the first visit by a French head of state to Syria in 18 years and the first by any Western leader since Assad was ousted in late 2024, with the two agreeing to reappoint ambassadors after roughly 14 years and signing more than a dozen accords spanning health, transport, banking and security cooperation. Paris will return more than 50 million dollars in assets seized from the Assad family, while TotalEnergies and shipping group CMA CGM, whose executives joined the delegation, advanced investments that include CMA CGM air cargo operations at Damascus International Airport.

SANA
July 7, 2026 · Security

Twin explosions near Macron's Damascus hotel wound 18 as the capital absorbs a second attack this month

Two blasts struck a busy district near the Four Seasons hotel and the Tourism Ministry in central Damascus during Macron's July 7 visit, wounding at least 18 people including four police officers, the Interior Ministry said, with the Elysee stating the French president did not hear the explosions and kept to his schedule. No group claimed responsibility, and the attack followed the July 2 bombing of a cafe near the Palace of Justice that killed 10 civilians.

Al Jazeera
July 6, 2026 · Governance

Syria's first post-Assad parliament holds its opening session

The new People's Assembly convened for the first time on July 6 under a transitional formula in which about a third of the members were appointed directly by al-Sharaa and the rest chosen through regional electoral bodies, seating Druze and Kurdish representatives for a term running roughly two and a half years. The southern Druze province of Sweida did not take part, its local leadership still rejecting rule from Damascus a year after last summer's bloodshed there.

Iraq

July 2026 · Diplomacy

Al-Zaidi heads to Washington on July 15 with a US-brokered Iraq-Syria economic pact in view

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi begins a four-day Washington visit on July 15, his first since taking office in May, with meetings set with President Trump, congressional leaders and US oil executives and energy investment at the center of the agenda. US envoy Tom Barrack is expected to sponsor an Iraq-Syria economic agreement during the trip, part of a push to draw Baghdad toward the US-aligned camp, alongside a proposed pipeline carrying crude from southern Iraq to Jordan's Aqaba port.

Shafaq News
July 2026 · Security

Baghdad's September 30 deadline for militias to disarm hangs over al-Zaidi's US trip

The government has set September 30, when the US-led anti-jihadist coalition mission ends, as the date for all armed factions to place their weapons under state control, a demand Washington is pressing hard as al-Zaidi prepares to meet Trump. Hardline Iran-aligned groups are resisting, leaving the militia file unresolved even as the prime minister leans on his anti-corruption sweep, which has seized roughly 86 million dollars, to build standing at home.

The Times of Israel
July 2026 · Energy

Iraq's grid buckles into peak summer as the Gulf power link slips to the end of August

With temperatures at their July peak, Iraq is running about 29 gigawatts of supply against demand that can top 40 to 55 gigawatts, and the long-delayed Gulf Cooperation Council electricity interconnection has been pushed to at least the end of August, widening the shortfall. The crunch follows wartime damage to power infrastructure and years of gas flaring and grid underinvestment that leave millions facing long daily outages through the hottest weeks of the year.

Kuwait

July 6, 2026 · Economy

Kuwait's bourse firms as the oil rebound and non-oil growth lift sentiment

The Kuwait All Share index closed near 8,739 on July 6, up about 0.5 percent on the day and roughly 3 percent higher than a year earlier, as the post-Hormuz recovery in crude output and steadier non-oil activity underpin the market. The IMF projects Kuwait's real GDP will expand about 3.8 percent in 2026 as OPEC+ cuts unwind, even with the budget deficit widening on higher spending and softer oil revenue.

Trading Economics
July 2026 · Energy

Kuwait pushes the Dorra gas field forward and holds its line against Iran's claim

Kuwait is advancing development of the offshore Dorra gas field it shares with Saudi Arabia, moving toward tenders for the associated gas plant while the two states reaffirm that the deposit sits wholly in their waters and that Iran has no legal basis for a stake. The field is central to Kuwait's drive to raise domestic gas output and trim costly imports as power demand climbs.

Kurdistan

July 2026 · Fiscal

Erbil transfers July's non-oil revenue to Baghdad and sends a delegation to settle the customs dispute

The KRG deposited about 120 billion dinars of July non-oil revenue into the federal Finance Ministry's account at the Central Bank branch in Erbil and dispatched a delegation to Baghdad for talks on customs collection, the mechanism that has repeatedly snagged the flow of public-sector salaries. The moves are meant to keep the oil-for-revenue arrangement intact so Baghdad releases the Region's payroll without another delay.

July 8, 2026 · Oil & Gas

Hunt Oil restarts on July 8 as Kurdistan's field recovery gathers pace

Hunt Oil resumed production on July 8, the latest international operator to bring Kurdish fields back after DNO, Gulf Keystone and HKN, as the Region works to rebuild output toward roughly 170,000 barrels a day channelled to SOMO for export through the northern pipeline to Ceyhan. The phased restart follows the spring war that knocked out much of the Region's production and the revived Erbil-Baghdad export deal that routes Kurdish crude to federal marketing.
