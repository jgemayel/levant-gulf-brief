Syria

August 12, 2026 · Politics

SDF merger advances on the military track while justice and schools stay stuck

More than six months after the January 29 accord, the folding of Syrian Democratic Forces units into the Defense Ministry is moving ahead while the civilian files lag, with the integration of the justice system and the recognition of Autonomous Administration schools, curricula, and teaching certificates still unresolved. Commander Mazloum Abdi is now reported to be weighing a shift from military command to civilian politics through a new party, a move Damascus says is open to any faction that fully disarms and cuts foreign ties. The uneven pace leaves the northeast half-merged, with the armed structures dissolving faster than the parallel administration that governed Hasakah and the Jazira.

August 11, 2026 · Energy

Syria positions itself as Iraq's overland bypass around the shut strait

With the Strait of Hormuz closed, Syria is emerging as a transit route for Iraqi crude to the Mediterranean, first by truck to the port of Baniyas and now through a plan to revive the mothballed Haditha-Baniyas pipeline. The roughly 891 kilometer line is envisioned at 1.5 to 2 million barrels per day, with a consortium that includes Chevron slated to carry out the restoration over about three years once talks close. The pivot hands Damascus transit revenue and a strategic role in the region's post-Hormuz oil map, tying its recovery to Baghdad's search for a route that avoids the Gulf. See the Iraq section.

Iraq

August 12, 2026 · Security High impact

Hormuz traffic sinks toward a three-month low as the US-Iran deal falters

Vessel traffic through the Strait of Hormuz fell to around six ships on August 11, near the lowest level since mid-May and roughly 90 percent below the pre-war average of about 130 a day, as hopes for a US-Iran agreement to reopen the waterway faded. Iran's Mohsen Rezaei said the strait will not fully open until Washington meets Tehran's terms, while President Trump again insisted the United States controls the passage and warned Iran would "get blown away" if it moved. The standoff, running since the February 28 strikes on Iran, keeps Iraq's Gulf export lifeline throttled, with Brent trading near 88 dollars a barrel.

The National
August 12, 2026 · Energy

A UAE dark-transit shuttle and deep SOMO discounts keep Basra crude moving

Abu Dhabi National Oil Company's trading arm is offering to shuttle Iraqi crude through the Strait of Hormuz using a dark-transit playbook, moving Basra and other grades to Asian refiners on short runs with transponders switched off before transferring cargoes to other ships outside the Gulf. To lure buyers willing to risk the passage, Iraq's SOMO has slashed prices by as much as 30 dollars a barrel below benchmark, with its flagship Basra Medium discounted 25 to 27 dollars, as buyers increasingly take on the shipping risk themselves. The workaround follows a run of attacks on ADNOC tankers in the strait over the past week.

Kuwait

August 12, 2026 · Economy

KPC posts a three-year-high profit despite the strikes on its facilities

Kuwait Petroleum Corporation recorded consolidated net profit of 2.155 billion dinars, about 6.9 billion dollars, for fiscal year 2025/26, its strongest result in three years, chief executive Sheikh Nawaf Saud Al-Nasser Al-Sabah announced on August 12 after the Supreme Petroleum Council signed off. He called the year "exceptional by all measures," noting it closed amid Iranian attacks on refineries, oil tankers, crude production sites, and KPC offices and airport facilities. KPC pointed to gains in offshore exploration and petrochemicals, groundwork on the Al-Saif project, and the launch of Al-Shaheen, described as the largest foreign investment in Kuwait's history.

Kurdistan

August 11, 2026 · Economy

Barzani says the war has cut Kurdistan's trade by more than 70 percent

Prime Minister Masrour Barzani said trade across the Kurdistan Region has fallen by more than 70 percent since the US-Iran war began and that "many of our infrastructure have been hit badly," pressing that the region will not be dragged into a conflict it never sought. The Region has been struck more than 1,000 times by missiles and drones launched from Iran or by Iran-aligned militias inside Iraq, hitting oil fields, power plants, and border crossings and deepening the economic squeeze on Erbil. His remarks land as the Khor Mor shutdown keeps roughly 2,500 megawatts off the grid and Baghdad-bound export routes carry the weight of the closed strait.
