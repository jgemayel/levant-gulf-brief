Al-Sharaa presses to finish the SDF merger and warns sanctions relief stays half-done

President Ahmed al-Sharaa acknowledged delays in carrying out the January 29 accord to fold the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces into state institutions and said Damascus still means to complete it. Japan and UN-Habitat sign a 9.5 million dollar Homs recovery deal

Japan and UN-Habitat signed a 9.5 million dollar agreement at Syria's foreign ministry in Damascus to rehabilitate Homs, restoring the Old Clock Square as a commercial hub and standing up sustainable solid waste management. The grant is the latest tranche of a Japanese-funded urban recovery push that officials say will benefit some 870,000 people across the country.

Iraq

July 30, 2026 · Security High impact

The US and Iran trade fresh strikes as the war reignites and spreads to Egypt

US forces hit dozens of Iranian military targets early Thursday to degrade Tehran's ability to threaten American troops, Gulf allies and shipping, after Iran fired a ballistic missile at the Muwaffaq Salti air base hosting US forces in Jordan and the IRGC pledged more retaliation. The renewed exchange, five months into the conflict, widened overnight to Egypt's Damietta port, where a drone set two gas tankers ablaze, and leaves Iraq exposed on every front through its Iran-aligned militias and its blocked southern export route.

Militias defy al-Zaidi's disarmament order as the September deadline nears

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi's order for pro-Iran factions to surrender their weapons to the state by September 30, when the US-led anti-ISIS coalition winds down, ran into open rejection from Kataib Hezbollah, Harakat al-Nujaba, Kataib Sayyid al-Shuhada and Ansar Allah al-Awfiya. July 30, 2026 · Energy

A first Hormuz cargo in three weeks slips through before the strikes reset the risk

A Qatari LNG vessel transited the Strait of Hormuz for the first time in nearly three weeks, a tentative sign of movement that the renewed US strikes on Iran hours later promptly undercut as monitors rated the waterway critical again. With southern traffic still throttled to a trickle, Iraq's crude keeps leaning almost entirely on the northern route to Ceyhan, the same lifeline that carries Basra and Kurdish barrels while Basra terminals stay all but idle.

Kuwait

July 30, 2026 · Public Finance

Kuwait borrows 7.3 billion dollars in a week and hits the brakes on spending

Kuwait raised more than 7.3 billion dollars in a single week, its heaviest issuance since the March 2025 debt law, pairing a 6 billion dollar sovereign bond that drew three times the bids with 150 million dinars sold to domestic banks, and ordered ministries to rein in outlays. The scramble for cash follows a revenue drop of about 25 percent as Hormuz keeps crude that once ran above 2 million barrels a day off the market and the budget gap balloons toward 7.1 billion dinars.

The country's airspace stayed limited to arrivals and departures with overflights banned, and the cabinet again drew a hard line against Tehran's strikes on its lifelines.

Kurdistan

July 30, 2026 · Security

Erbil downs six explosive drones over its oil fields as Sarsang halts again

Kurdish air defenses brought down six explosive-laden drones over Erbil, two of them near the Khurmala oil field, while a separate strike halted production at the Sarsang field in Duhok after overnight damage at another site. The barrage keeps the Region's energy sector under fire in a campaign that has thrown at least 900 drones and missiles at Kurdistan since late February, killing 29 people and wounding roughly 138.
