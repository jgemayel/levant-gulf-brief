Syria

July 16, 2026 · Security

Syria intercepts an Iranian arms convoy bound for Hezbollah at the Iraqi border

Syrian security forces seized a vehicle parked near the Iraqi border in Deir ez-Zor province carrying Iranian-made Almas anti-tank guided missiles, long-range rockets and a large batch of fiber-optic FPV attack drones the Interior Ministry said were headed for Hezbollah in Lebanon. The interception is the latest in a run of 2026 seizures by Damascus along a corridor Tehran long used to move weapons and cash to the group, underscoring how sharply post-Assad Syria has turned against the old Iran supply line even as the wider Gulf war rages. Al Jazeera
July 16, 2026 · Diplomacy

Syria and Germany launch a joint committee and sign an air transport deal

Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani received German Minister of State Geza Andreas von Geyr in Damascus and the two sides opened the inaugural session of a Syrian-German joint committee, signing an air transport agreement and a declaration framing cooperation on reconstruction, investment, energy, transport, transitional justice and refugee returns. The visit adds Berlin to the widening line of Western and Gulf capitals re-engaging Damascus a year and a half after Assad's fall.

Iraq

July 16, 2026 · Security High impact

The US reinstates its Hormuz blockade and disables a tanker as Iraq's southern exports crater

A US fighter jet struck the Curacao-flagged tanker M/T Belma in the smokestack with a Hellfire missile as it ran toward Iran's Kharg Island terminal, the first vessel crippled since President Trump reimposed the naval blockade of Iran on July 14 after declaring last month's ceasefire memorandum dead. CENTCOM said it rerouted two ships and disabled a third in the blockade's first 24 hours, and with the strait shut Iraq's exports from its main southern fields have fallen about 70 percent, choking the artery that normally carries roughly 90 percent of Baghdad's 3.4 million barrels a day.

The Hill
July 14, 2026 · Politics

Al-Zaidi returns from Washington with a September troop-exit date and a stack of energy deals

Standing beside Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi at the White House, Trump said the US no longer needs a military presence in Iraq, and both governments confirmed the roughly 23-year mission will end by September 30, with al-Zaidi framing it as US forces out and US companies in. The visit produced a stack of energy memoranda meant to pull American majors deeper into Iraqi oil and gas, recasting a security relationship built on the fight against ISIS as an investment partnership just as the Gulf war tests Baghdad's professed neutrality.

Al Jazeera
July 16, 2026 · Energy

Chevron edges toward West Qurna-2 and Nasiriyah as Baghdad hunts a Hormuz exit

Chevron is set to sign two non-binding memoranda moving it closer to developing the giant West Qurna-2 field and the Nasiriyah project, part of the Washington package that also drew in HKN, WesternZagros and Hunt under US security guarantees and a separate deal with TI Capital on the Kirkuk to Baniyas pipeline. Al-Zaidi wants output up from about 4.5 million barrels a day toward 7 million within three years and is building export routes that skirt the strait, giving the American return a clear Hormuz-exit logic.

Iraqi News
July 16, 2026 · Economy

Iraq and Jordan revive the Basra to Aqaba pipeline as a Hormuz bypass

Al-Zaidi and Jordanian Foreign Minister Ayman Safadi agreed to move forward on the long-dormant Basra to Aqaba oil pipeline, a two-section line that would carry up to 2.25 million barrels a day from the Rumaila field toward the Red Sea at an estimated cost of 18 billion dollars. Trump envoy Tom Barrack sat in on the talks as Washington presses regional producers to build alternatives to Hormuz, with Brent trading near 84 dollars as the blockade drags on.

Kuwait

July 15, 2026 · Security High impact

Kuwait weathers its heaviest barrage yet, a naval vessel hit and four wounded

Kuwait's armed forces said they intercepted one ballistic missile, five cruise missiles and 33 drones through the evening of July 15 in the largest single wave to hit the country since the war reignited, with defense spokesman Major General Saud al-Otaibi reporting a naval vessel struck and four servicemembers wounded. Falling missile and drone debris damaged several vital and civilian sites, marking the first Kuwaiti casualties and infrastructure hits of the campaign after weeks of intercepts that had caused no injuries.

Arab Times
July 15, 2026 · Economy

Kuwait projects a 9.8 billion dinar deficit as war saps oil income

Kuwait expects its fiscal shortfall to widen 54.7 percent to 9.8 billion dinars, about 31.9 billion dollars, as oil income slides to a projected 12.8 billion dinars on an average price assumption cut to 57 dollars a barrel, well below the roughly 90.5 dollar level the budget needs to balance. Salaries and subsidies still absorb 76 percent of the 26.1 billion dinars in planned spending, leaving little cushion as the Hormuz blockade keeps crude flows and prices unstable.

Kurdistan

July 16, 2026 · Security

Drones strike near Erbil's US consulate, the first such attack since April's ceasefire

Air defenses shot down a cluster of explosive-laden drones over Erbil late on July 15 near the US consulate and the coalition site at the international airport, the first drones aimed at the consulate since the April 7 ceasefire, with AFP reporters seeing the interceptions and smoke over the city and no casualties reported. No group claimed the attack, which landed as US and Iranian forces traded fire across the region and pulled the Kurdistan Region back toward the line of the spring bombardment.

Iraqi News
July 16, 2026 · Economy

KRG salaries hinge on oil flows as the northern route carries Iraq's working barrels

Kurdistan Region public workers are again waiting on their July pay, with the transfer from Baghdad pinned to the oil revenue moving north through the Kirkuk to Ceyhan pipeline rather than the blockaded Gulf. As US strikes shut Iraq's southern export terminals, the Region's Mediterranean-bound line has become one of the few working arteries for Iraqi crude, handing Erbil rare leverage even as the payroll for more than a million civil servants stays hostage to the pace of exports and the deal with the federal government.
