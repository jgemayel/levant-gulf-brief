Syria

July 24, 2026 · Diplomacy

Guterres arrives in Damascus in the first UN chief visit to Syria since 2009

UN Secretary General Antonio Guterres travelled to Damascus at the government's invitation, the first visit to Syria by a UN chief in 17 years, meeting President Ahmed al-Sharaa and Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani and holding talks with civil society and women's groups. Al-Sharaa pressed for faster UN recovery projects and a more active role for the disengagement force UNDOF, a diplomatic marker for a government that took power a year and a half after Assad's fall.

July 24, 2026 · Security

Israeli forces push again into Quneitra as Golan incursions continue

An Israeli force of five vehicles including two tanks advanced onto the al-Duraiyat hill near Al-Muallaqa village in Quneitra, a day after troops stormed Wadi al-Raqad in the Yarmouk basin of western Daraa, set up a checkpoint and withdrew. Damascus says the repeated raids, arrests and land clearing breach the 1974 disengagement line and has renewed its call for a full Israeli pullback from southern Syria.

July 23, 2026 · Diplomacy

Security Council urges backing for Syria's recovery as aid needs stay vast

The UN Security Council pressed member states to support Syria's reconstruction and stabilization ahead of the Guterres visit, with two thirds of the 23 million population still needing humanitarian aid and about 5.5 million people internally displaced. The push lines up international attention behind the transitional government as it seeks sanctions relief and a return to global financial institutions.

Iraq

July 24, 2026 · Economy

Iraq's southern exports crater as Hormuz tanker traffic falls to three ships a day

Southern output has dropped roughly 70 percent to about 1.3 million barrels a day as tanker crossings of the Strait of Hormuz thinned to three on a single day, the lowest since May, with buyers shunning the Gulf. Baghdad, which draws close to 90 percent of its budget from oil, has opened talks with Tehran on moving cargoes through the strait even as the wider war keeps its main southern artery all but shut.

Gulf News
July 24, 2026 · Energy High impact

Baghdad races a July 27 treaty deadline to keep Kirkuk to Ceyhan crude flowing

The 1973 crude pipeline treaty that governs the Kirkuk to Ceyhan line lapses on July 27, and Iraq and Turkey are finalizing a 12-month replacement to keep the Mediterranean route open as the strait stays closed. Baghdad is pushing to nearly triple northern pipeline flows from about 220,000 to 770,000 barrels a day within two and a half months, leaning on the Kurdistan-run line to shield revenue from the maritime blockade. July 23, 2026 · Economy

Iraq led Gulf export growth in early July before the latest escalation

Iraq was a main driver of a roughly 16 percent jump in Gulf crude exports in the first half of July, when combined shipments from Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Iraq, Kuwait and Iran reached about 12 million barrels a day, per Kpler and Vortexa data. The early-month surge, the strongest since the war began in late February, has since given way to the sharp tanker pullback now choking southern flows.

Kuwait

July 24, 2026 · Security

Iran strikes Kuwait's Ali al-Salem air base as defenses engage a fresh wave

Kuwait's military said air defenses engaged hostile missiles and drones on Thursday night, with explosions heard across parts of the country, after local reports of an Iranian strike on the Ali al-Salem air base used by Kuwaiti and US forces. Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed it destroyed a US early-warning radar and hit an MQ-9 drone hangar and a Patriot battery at the base, though Kuwait did not confirm damage or casualties.

July 24, 2026 · Economy

Banks warn a prolonged Hormuz shutdown could force Kuwait to curb output within days

JP Morgan warned that a sustained closure of the Strait of Hormuz could push Kuwait and Iraq to cut crude production within days, as storage fills and tankers stay away from the Gulf. For Kuwait, which ships almost all of its oil through the strait, a forced cutback would strike directly at an economy already projecting a wide deficit as the war drags on.

Kurdistan

July 24, 2026 · Energy High impact

Khor Mor shutdown strips up to 80 percent of Kurdistan's electricity

Dana Gas fully shut its Khor Mor field after a wave of drone strikes, pulling roughly 2,500 to 3,000 megawatts offline and cutting up to 80 percent of the Region's power supply, with household cooking gas prices doubling as a result. The federal Oil Ministry agreed to truck in daily LPG to the Kurdistan Region until production can restart, a rare Baghdad lifeline as the energy squeeze deepens.

July 24, 2026 · Economy

Erbil's fuel caps buckle as pumps run dry across the Region

The KRG's push to hold regular gasoline at 750 dinars a liter is failing as fuel vanishes from most stations and improved-grade petrol trades well above the cap. In Sulaimani the Ministry of Natural Resources is supplying about 144,000 liters a day against roughly 2 million liters of demand, meeting only around 7 percent of need as the halt in Iranian imports compounds the drone-driven outages.

July 23, 2026 · Energy

Baghdad turns to the Kurdish pipeline to move 250,000 barrels of Kirkuk crude

With Hormuz shut, Baghdad is seeking to route about 250,000 barrels a day of Kirkuk crude through the Kurdistan Region's line to Ceyhan, on top of the more than 200,000 barrels a day the KRG already ships north. The reliance hands Erbil unusual weight in export policy just as the July 27 treaty deadline over the Turkish pipeline forces a scramble to keep the corridor legally open.
