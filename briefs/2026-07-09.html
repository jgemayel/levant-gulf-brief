Syria

July 8, 2026 · Security

Damascus locks down with checkpoints and blast walls after a second bombing in a week

The Interior Ministry has fanned heavily armed patrols across Damascus, restored checkpoints at major intersections and thrown concrete blast walls and mandatory vehicle searches around government buildings, courts and foreign missions after the July 7 twin blasts near Macron's hotel that wounded 18 and the July 2 cafe bombing that killed at least nine. The clampdown lays bare the balancing act facing al-Sharaa's government, which is trying to project stability to visiting investors while a wave of unclaimed attacks tests its grip on the capital.

July 7, 2026 · Diplomacy

France returns 51 million euros in Assad-era assets and reopens a banking channel to Damascus

Alongside the restoration of full ties during Macron's visit, Paris agreed to return roughly 51 million euros in illicit assets tied to Rifaat Assad and to provide technical assistance to Syria's Central Bank as it undertakes financial reforms, part of a Framework Declaration for Comprehensive Cooperation. The accords also fund the rebuilding of destroyed water and electricity infrastructure in Homs and expand cargo operations at Damascus International Airport under a logistics deal with shipping group CMA CGM.

Al-Sharaa's government juggles old and new threats from the coast to the northeast

Seven months into its rule, the government is contending simultaneously with the stalled reintegration of Druze Sweida, a still-incomplete merger of the Kurdish-led SDF agreed in January, lingering Alawite grievances on the coast and the fresh urban bombings, even as it courts Gulf and Western capital to rebuild an economy wrecked by 14 years of war and sanctions. The overlapping pressures are stretching a security apparatus that Damascus is still rebuilding from the ground up.

Iraq

July 8, 2026 · Energy High impact

Iraq and Turkey move toward a one-year protocol to keep Ceyhan crude flowing past the July 27 expiry

An Iraqi delegation drawn from the foreign and oil ministries and the Kurdistan Region's Natural Resources Ministry held two days of talks in Ankara this week on a temporary protocol that would keep more than 200,000 barrels a day moving through the Kirkuk-Ceyhan line for a year after the 1973 pipeline treaty lapses on July 27. Turkey has refused a straight extension of the 52-year-old pact, citing arbitration disputes and pressing to lift the line toward its 1.5 million barrel a day capacity, leaving Baghdad, Ankara and Erbil racing to bridge the gap before northern exports are exposed.

Turkish Minute
July 8, 2026 · Energy

Baghdad's cabinet clears studies for new export pipelines to Turkey and Syria with a Qatar-US consortium

The Council of Ministers authorised the oil ministry to finalise a head of agreement and a non-disclosure pact with Qatar's UCC Holding and US firms Chevron and TI Capital to study building crude export pipelines toward Turkey and Syria. The move is part of Baghdad's push to diversify export routes after the Strait of Hormuz disruption and dovetails with the US-brokered Iraq-Syria economic track al-Zaidi will carry to Washington on July 15.

Al-Zaidi approves 11 decrees and pushes the one-million land plot housing drive forward

The prime minister signed off on 11 decrees at an executive session and reported concrete progress on the nationwide plan to distribute one million residential plots, allocating them across provinces by population density and verified poverty rates to steer land toward the neediest households. Kuwait

July 8, 2026 · Economy

Kuwait's cabinet signs off on a KD 7.14 billion final-accounts deficit for 2025/2026

The cabinet approved a draft decree adopting the state's final accounts for fiscal 2025/2026, which closed with a deficit of about KD 7.14 billion, roughly 23.3 billion dollars, as revenues of KD 16.45 billion fell short of KD 23.59 billion in spending. The entire shortfall will be covered by drawdowns from the General Reserve Fund, underlining how softer oil income continues to widen the gap even as non-oil activity picks up.

Cabinet speeds a cloud and AI drive built on Google and Microsoft data-centre deals

Ministers reviewed a progress report on Kuwait's multibillion-dollar cloud agreements with Google Cloud and Microsoft, citing gains in rolling out data centres, hardening cybersecurity and standing up digital government services, and backed AI-driven education and youth sports initiatives. The push is central to the government's drive to modernise state services and cut reliance on legacy systems.

Kuwait's PM and Amir court Brookfield's Bruce Flatt in an investment push

Prime Minister Sheikh Ahmad Al-Abdullah Al-Sabah and the Amir separately received Brookfield chief executive Bruce Flatt and his delegation, stressing the importance of a competitive business environment and deeper ties with global asset managers weighing Gulf commitments. The courtship fits Kuwait's wider drive to draw foreign capital as it diversifies away from oil-dependent revenue.

Kurdistan

July 8, 2026 · Fiscal High impact

A Baghdad cabinet no-show stalls the oil-for-salaries deal with 1.2 million KRG workers still unpaid

Iraq's Council of Ministers left the oil and salary file off its July 8 agenda, deflating hopes of an imminent breakthrough just a day after reports that talks had reached their final stages and that Baghdad was poised to authorise Kurdish exports through Ceyhan. More than 1.2 million Kurdistan Region public employees remain unpaid since May, with a KRG technical delegation still in Baghdad wrangling over a federal budget release in exchange for oil handovers as Erbil insists on keeping a share of crude for domestic use.

July 2026 · Energy

KRG says the Runaki drive now delivers 24-hour power to more than 90 percent of the Region

The Ministry of Electricity says more than 90 percent of the Kurdistan Region now receives round-the-clock electricity under the flagship Runaki programme, up from about 85 percent earlier in the year, with roughly 6,000 neighbourhood diesel generators decommissioned and around 80 percent of customers paying less than they did for generator power. The rollout stands in sharp contrast to federal Iraq, where the grid is buckling under peak summer demand.

July 2026 · Infrastructure

Erbil touts dozens of strategic water projects as a pillar of the ninth cabinet's reforms

The KRG says it has implemented dozens of strategic water schemes to shore up supply, led by the 720 billion dinar Erbil Emergency Water Project that delivers 480,000 cubic metres of drinking water a day, alongside large builds at Bardarash, Goptapa-Chamchamal and Dukan-Slemani. The government frames the works as part of more than 4.7 trillion dinars in service and investment projects completed since 2019 as it presses to lock in long-term water security.
