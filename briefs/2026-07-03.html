Syria

July 2, 2026 · Security High impact

A bomb rips through a crowded Damascus cafe near the Palace of Justice, killing nine and wounding twenty

An improvised device holding about one kilogram of explosives packed with metal fragments went off inside a cafe on al-Nasr Street in the al-Hijaz district on July 2, roughly 100 metres from the main gate of the Palace of Justice, where former Assad-era officials are being tried. The Interior Ministry put the toll at nine dead and twenty wounded, warned it could rise given severe injuries, and said a man appears to have left the device under a table before slipping out. No group claimed the attack, the deadliest in central Damascus since the government changed hands.

Al Jazeera
July 1, 2026 · Politics

Sharaa names the final third of the People's Assembly, sidelining the SDF and installing a divisive Druze figure for Suwayda

President Ahmad al-Sharaa appointed 70 lawmakers on July 1, one-third of the transitional chamber, completing a body whose other two-thirds were filled through indirect votes held between October 2025 and May 2026. The Kurdish figures selected come from factions opposed to the Kurdish-led SDF, such as the Kurdish National Council, leaving the group frozen out of the new legislature, while for Suwayda, scene of last year's violence that killed roughly 1,700 people, Sharaa named Laith al-Balous, a figure many Druze denounce for siding with Damascus against their factions.

Iraq

July 1, 2026 · Security High impact

Iraq gives pro-Iran factions until September 30 to disarm, and two armed groups signal they will comply

Government spokesman Haidar al-Aboudi said all armed factions outside the state have been formally notified to hand their weapons to state control by September 30, the date the US-led anti-ISIS coalition is due to wind down its mission, warning that after that "all weapons outside the state framework will be subject to legal redress." Asaib Ahl al-Haq and Kataeb Imam Ali have said they will transfer their brigades and arms to the state, while other groups have refused to engage, arguing the matter should not be settled under American pressure ahead of Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi's mid-July Washington visit.

Arab News
July 2, 2026 · Energy

Iraq pushes southern crude output back to 1.75 million barrels a day and targets two million as tanker loadings resume

Oil officials told Reuters that southern production rose by 250,000 barrels a day to around 1.75 million as more tankers loaded crude at Iraqi ports, freeing storage and clearing the way for a push to two million barrels a day within days. The rebound is the clearest sign yet that Gulf exporters are recovering from the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz during the US-Iran war, the same reopening driving Kuwait's return to market and pressing Baghdad to revive its northern route through Kurdistan.

Asharq Al-Awsat
July 2, 2026 · Economy

Washington restarts dollar shipments to Baghdad after a months-long freeze

Aides to al-Zaidi confirmed the US has resumed air shipments of dollars drawn from Iraqi oil revenues held at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, reversing an April halt of roughly 500 million dollars imposed to pressure Baghdad over Iran-backed militia attacks. Officials cast the move as a goodwill gesture recognizing the government's push on militias and corruption, though the parallel suspension of security cooperation with Iraq remains in place.

Kuwait

July 2, 2026 · Energy

Kuwait lifts force majeure and offers crude for July loading as the Strait of Hormuz reopens

Kuwait Petroleum Corp withdrew the force majeure it had declared on oil shipments during the war and tendered Kuwait Export Crude for July delivery in two-million-barrel cargoes, the formal commercial signal that standard sales are resuming as it moves output toward two million barrels a day. The turnaround is stark given exports had fallen to near zero by April, when the closure of the Strait left the pipeline-less emirate cutting production to little more than local needs.

Kurdistan

July 2, 2026 · Energy

Kurdistan's oil restart stalls on technical and financial snags as the Ceyhan pipeline treaty nears its July 27 expiry

Talks between the KRG, Baghdad, and the producers have yet to yield a firm timetable to restart exports, with unresolved technical damage from wartime drone strikes on the fields and open financial questions holding up a resumption that officials had cast just days ago as free of obstacles. The delay bites harder with the 970-kilometre Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline agreement set to lapse on July 27 and Ankara signaling it will not renew on the old terms, even as Baghdad leans on the northern route to move Kirkuk crude after the Hormuz squeeze on its southern exports.

July 1, 2026 · Security

Turkiye's intelligence chief tours the Kurdistan Region for talks with Kurdish leaders

Ibrahim Kalin, head of Turkiye's National Intelligence Organization, held talks with Kurdish officials in Sulaymaniyah and was received by Masoud Barzani in Erbil on July 1, exchanging views on regional developments as Ankara presses the PKK disarmament track and deepens security coordination with Baghdad and Erbil. Kalin's swing also took in Kirkuk, where he met Turkmen Front leaders, and the federal capital for talks with Iraq's president.
