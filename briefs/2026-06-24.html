Syria

June 23, 2026 · Energy

Al-Sharaa hosts Chevron at the People's Palace as Damascus pushes its offshore gas deal toward a binding contract and eyes the major for the Iraq-Baniyas crude line

President Ahmed al-Sharaa received a Chevron delegation led by corporate development head Frank Mount at the People's Palace on June 23 to map investment across Syria's energy sector, while Syrian Petroleum Company chief Youssef Qiblawy met the same team to turn the February memorandum on offshore Block 1 into an executive contract. The talks also weighed Chevron taking a strategic stake in the Kirkuk-Baniyas crude pipeline across Syrian territory, knitting the US firm into both Syria's first offshore development and the revived Iraq-to-Mediterranean export route. SANA
June 23, 2026 · Economy

Syria readies the Baniyas terminal as Iraq's Mediterranean outlet, adding two unloading areas and banking transit fees on the new crude corridor

Syria is expanding its Baniyas oil terminal to handle Iraqi crude and naphtha, opening two additional unloading areas and lifting throughput to roughly 900 tanker-trucks a day as Iraq's SOMO prepares to set up offices at the port. Damascus already collects transit fees on Iraqi fuel oil moving across the country, and the build-out positions postwar Syria as a standing energy corridor between Iraq and the Mediterranean, a point that drives the lead story in the Iraq section.

Iraq

June 23, 2026 · Energy High impact

Iraq readies its first crude and naphtha exports through Syria for early July and vows to keep the Mediterranean route even after Hormuz reopens

Iraq's State Oil Marketing Organization is preparing to start exporting crude and naphtha through Syria from early July, beginning at about 50,000 barrels a day by tanker truck to the port of Baniyas, an expansion of a fuel-oil scheme that has moved 650,000 tonnes a month since April. Officials in Baghdad and Damascus say the corridor will stay part of Iraq's long-term export strategy even after Strait of Hormuz shipping fully resumes, a structural hedge born of the Iran war that also feeds the Baniyas build-out flagged in the Syria section.

Zawya
June 22, 2026 · Energy

Iraq lifts Kirkuk-Ceyhan throughput toward 340,000 barrels a day as the northern route nears its July 27 legal cliff

Federal crude exports through the Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline have climbed to about 340,000 barrels a day as Baghdad pushes the northern line back toward its 770,000-barrel target, leaning on both Kirkuk fields and Kurdish volumes handed to SOMO. The ramp runs against the clock, with the 1973 transit treaty that governs the route set to lapse on July 27 and no replacement deal yet signed, a deadline that weighs most heavily on the Kurdish oil sector covered in the Kurdistan section.

Kuwait

June 22, 2026 · Energy

Kuwait's oil minister sees June crude output rebounding to 2.628 million barrels a day as the postwar recovery accelerates

Kuwait expects crude production to reach about 2.628 million barrels a day in June, the oil minister told KUNA, a sharp rebound after wartime output collapsed to roughly 490,000 barrels a day at the low point. The figure tracks the lifting of force majeure and the reopening of Hormuz shipping that has let KPC restart deliveries to Asian refiners.

Asharq Al-Awsat
June 22, 2026 · Energy

KPC says it can restore 80 percent of pre-war output within a month, with the last fifth taking three to four months

KPC chief executive Nawaf Al-Sabah said Kuwait can bring back 80 percent of the oil production halted by the Iran war in under a month, while the remaining 20 percent will need three to four months to return online. The timeline frames a faster recovery than markets had assumed for an industry that supplies roughly 7 percent of the world's crude.

Kurdistan

June 23, 2026 · Economy

KRG widens its e-Psule digital billing system to five more districts, adding about 280,000 subscribers

The Kurdistan Regional Government extended its e-Psule electronic billing platform to Garmian, Darbandikhan, Dukan, Chamchamal, and Said Sadiq, bringing roughly 280,000 new subscribers into the cashless system as the region pushes toward 1 million users by mid-2026. The rollout, run through the Ministry of Electricity, is part of a wider digital-governance drive that has already signed up more than 600,000 users across Erbil, Duhok, and Sulaimani.

Kurdistan24
June 22, 2026 · Energy

Barzani says the Kurdistan Region is ready to lift its share of Iraq's oil exports as the 16-dollar-a-barrel payment mechanism settles

Prime Minister Masrour Barzani said the Kurdistan Region stands ready to increase its contribution to Iraq's crude exports through Ceyhan, with producers paid an interim 16 dollars a barrel for oil handed to SOMO and the region keeping about 50,000 barrels a day for domestic use. The offer lands as the July 27 expiry of the Iraq-Turkey pipeline treaty hangs over the only major export route the Kurdish oil sector has, tying the region's finances to the federal throughput push in the Iraq section.
