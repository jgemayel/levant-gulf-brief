Syria

August 27, 2026 · Economy

Visa and Mastercard switch on international card payments in Damascus

Visa and Mastercard ran their first live international card transactions in Syria, reconnecting the country to global payment rails 15 years after they were cut off and days after Washington removed it from the state sponsors of terrorism list. President Ahmed al-Sharaa took part in the Visa test, paying by card at a restaurant in the Damascus Old City, while Qatar's QNB Group and Mastercard completed what they called the first end-to-end international card payment in the country and Visa ran its first live transaction with Lebanon's Fransabank. It is a concrete step toward the trade, tourism, and remittance flows the new government is counting on to fund reconstruction.

August 27, 2026 · Security

Israel keeps striking Syria as the US-brokered de-escalation track limps on

Israel carried out fresh strikes in southern Syria and Lebanon even as US envoy Tom Barrack pushed the de-escalation channel that produced a rare face-to-face Syrian and Israeli meeting in Amman a few days earlier. Damascus says the Amman talks addressed halting Israeli operations and keeping Syria out of the widening Israel and Turkey friction, but Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani has framed any progress around confidence-building rather than trust, and Israel still ties a deal to a demilitarized southwest and protection for the Druze. The gap between the diplomacy and the strikes on the ground is the story.

Iraq

August 28, 2026 · Energy High impact

Iraq offers Basra crude for loading outside Hormuz and takes over its own shuttle runs

State marketer SOMO offered Basrah Medium and Heavy for collection by ship-to-ship transfer near the Omani coast, the first time Iraq has sold its crude for loading outside the Strait of Hormuz since the war began, and the Oil Ministry says it is chartering its own tankers to run the shuttle rather than leave the risky crossing and the discounts to traders. Basra loadings have climbed back to about 2 million barrels a day, with August exports the highest since the conflict started, though still short of the roughly 3.3 million the port moved before the war. Spokesman Salim al-Rikabi says the aim is to route barrels through secure lanes and claw back the margins Iraq had been giving away, a direct hedge against the chokepoint that carries nearly all its oil money.

Iraqi News
August 28, 2026 · Security

The US rejects a two-year weapons freeze as Baghdad pins any handover on the PMF

Iran-aligned factions floated freezing their weapons for two years instead of disarming, and Washington rejected the offer, leaving negotiators to fall back on a compromise under which arms would be released to the Popular Mobilization Forces to register and store rather than surrendered outright. Three hardline groups, Kataib Hezbollah, Harakat al-Nujaba, and Kataib Sayyid al-Shuhada, still refuse even a symbolic handover and tie any deal to a full withdrawal of US forces, with September 30 now cast as the coalition's exit date rather than a firm disarmament cutoff. Analysts warn the factions are angling to run the clock past the deadline with the mechanics unresolved.

Kuwait

August 28, 2026 · Energy

Kuwait lifts its September output ceiling to 2.676 million barrels a day

Oil Minister Tariq al-Roumi said Kuwait will raise crude production to 2.676 million barrels a day in September, its share of a 188,000 barrel a day increase eight OPEC+ producers agreed for the month as the alliance keeps unwinding its voluntary cuts. The commitment holds even as the Hormuz disruption caps how much Kuwait can actually ship, since it depends almost entirely on the strait for exports. Kuwait framed the move as staying lockstep with OPEC+ coordination rather than a unilateral push for volume.

Times Kuwait
August 29, 2026 · Economy

A KD 9.8 billion deficit exposes how far Kuwait sits below its break-even oil price

Kuwait's 2026/2027 budget pencils in a deficit of about 9.8 billion dinars, up roughly 55 percent on the prior year, with spending near 26.1 billion dinars against 16.3 billion in revenue and salaries and subsidies still eating about three quarters of outlays. The finance ministry cut its oil price assumption to 57 dollars a barrel, far under the roughly 90 dollar level Kuwait needs to balance the books, a gap the war and the throttled Hormuz trade have only widened. With diversification stalled, the numbers sharpen the case for the tax and spending reforms Kuwait has long deferred.

Kurdistan

August 28, 2026 · Energy

The northern Ceyhan route sits idle as a Hormuz hedge Iraq is barely using

While Baghdad scrambles to route Basra crude around the Strait of Hormuz, the Kirkuk to Ceyhan pipeline that carries Kurdistan Region and Kirkuk oil to the Mediterranean is running at only about 170,000 to 200,000 barrels a day, far below the 750,000 barrel a day minimum guaranteed under the one-year Iraq and Turkey transit deal. The northern line is the one major export outlet that skips the strait entirely, yet ramp-up plans that were meant to push flows toward 770,000 barrels a day have slipped. For the KRG the underused pipeline is both leverage and a reminder that its crude still moves on Baghdad's timeline.

The Arab Weekly
August 28, 2026 · Fiscal

The arithmetic behind the payroll fight, only 41 percent of what Erbil was owed

A KRG accounting puts the shortfall driving the salary standoff in hard numbers, the Region was allocated 58.3 trillion dinars under Iraq's 2023 to 2025 budget framework but received about 24.3 trillion, roughly 41 percent of its entitlement, with the gaps carrying straight into unpaid 2026 payroll. The figure frames the six-file talks now running between Erbil and Baghdad, where the federal side wants Kurdish non-oil revenue transferred in full and Erbil wants salaries guaranteed on the same terms as the rest of Iraq. Until the transfers actually land on schedule, each side reads the same ledger as proof the other is not paying.
