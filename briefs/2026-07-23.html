Syria

July 19, 2026 · Security

Al-Sharaa overhauls Syria's security and intelligence leadership after a wave of Damascus bombings

President Ahmed al-Sharaa named Interior Minister Anas Khattab to also run the National Security Office, installed Hussein al-Salamah as his deputy, made Mulham al-Shantout assistant interior minister for security affairs, and appointed Abdul Qader Tahan head of the General Intelligence Service. The shake-up follows a July 2 cafe blast near the main Damascus courthouse that killed at least 10 and the July 7 explosions during Macron's visit, and it hands the interior minister sweeping new authority as Damascus tries to pull a fragmented security structure under central control.

Al Jazeera
July 22, 2026 · Diplomacy

Trump floats a Syrian role against Hezbollah as the Gulf war grinds on

President Trump said the Syrian president wants to move against Hezbollah and that such a step would be very effective, a striking public nod to Damascus as a counterweight to Iran's Lebanese ally amid the wider conflict. The remark lands weeks after Syrian forces seized an Iranian arms convoy on the Iraqi border that Damascus said was bound for Hezbollah, underscoring how far post-Assad Syria has turned against Tehran's old supply network.

The Times of Israel
July 22, 2026 · Aviation

Kuwait Airways lands in Damascus for the first time in 14 years

Kuwait Airways operated its first flight into Damascus International Airport since the carrier suspended the route more than a decade ago, resuming a daily service with return economy fares from about 75 dollars. The reopening adds another Gulf link to a widening list of routes back into Syria as capitals re-engage Damascus, even as Iranian strikes rattle the region's airspace.

Iraq

July 22, 2026 · Security High impact

Trump vows to destroy an Iranian bridge or power plant for every ship hit in Hormuz as US strikes reach an 11th night

Trump warned the US would knock out one bridge or power plant every time Iran fires on a vessel transiting the Strait of Hormuz, and Tehran answered that it would hit US-linked infrastructure and energy facilities across the region in return. Central Command struck Iranian military operations centers, drone storage sites and logistics hubs for an 11th consecutive night, keeping Iraq's export lanes through the strait effectively shut and its southern shipments a fraction of the 4.2 million barrels a day it was moving in February.

Al-Zaidi heads to Tehran for gas and de-escalation talks days after his Washington trip

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi opens a two-day visit to Tehran on July 23 aimed at easing US-Iran tensions and locking in uninterrupted Iranian gas supply as General Electric moves ahead with a nationwide electricity plan. Baghdad is pressing a sovereign guarantee to keep signed energy contracts valid through any change of government, a bid to reassure investors after al-Zaidi's Washington visit put Iraq in the middle of the two rivals.

Shafaq News
July 22, 2026 · Energy

Iraq puts its US energy deals at 200 billion dollars and targets 7 million barrels a day

The oil ministry valued the agreements signed with US firms during al-Zaidi's visit at roughly 200 billion dollars, spanning contracts with Halliburton, HKN Energy and Chevron across multiple fields. Al-Zaidi told US executives Baghdad wants to lift crude output to 7 million barrels a day within three years from a current capacity near 4.8 million, while reviving export pipelines through Turkey and Syria to cut its reliance on the Gulf.

The National
July 21, 2026 · Economy

The war has cost Iraq more than 300 million barrels of lost oil

Iraqi output slid more than 50 percent to about 1.9 million barrels a day in June from roughly 4.2 million in February as Hormuz disruption throttled tanker traffic, a shortfall now measured above 300 million barrels since the war began. Shipments had clawed back toward 1.5 million barrels a day before the latest escalation, leaving Baghdad chasing the Ceyhan and Syria overland routes to keep crude moving.

Kuwait

July 22, 2026 · Security

A fresh Iranian barrage hits a Kuwaiti power station and desalination plant

Kuwait's military said its air defenses engaged a new wave of Iranian drones and missiles, with several soldiers wounded by falling shrapnel and strikes that damaged a power station, a water desalination plant and other civilian infrastructure. No civilians were reported hurt, but the hit to water and power assets marks a sharper turn in a campaign Iran has run against US and Kuwaiti targets since late February.

The Jerusalem Post
July 22, 2026 · Finance

Kuwait raises 6 billion dollars in bonds despite the Iranian strikes

Kuwait pulled in about 14.8 billion dollars of orders for a three-part dollar bond sale, letting it raise 6 billion dollars and tighten pricing by 25 basis points across all tranches even as air-raid sirens sounded over the country. The strong book landed as Brent jumped 4.4 percent to 95.24 dollars a barrel, its highest since June 11, easing some of the strain the war has put on Kuwait's oil income.

Kurdistan

July 21, 2026 · Security

Iran's renewed drone campaign hammers Erbil, with 900-plus munitions fired since February

Erbil residents have heard drone intercepts nightly since July 15 as Iran resumed strikes on Kurdish Iranian opposition camps, with eight one-way drones hitting the capital in one night and Iraqi air defenses downing others. At least 904 drones and missiles have struck the Region between February 28 and July 17, killing 29 people and wounding 138, with most aimed at energy facilities.
