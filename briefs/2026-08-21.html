Damascus says Turkish officers visited the airbase Israel struck as Netanyahu warns Ankara off

Syrian Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani said on Thursday that Turkish military officers had visited the Abu al-Duhur airbase in Idlib days before Israel's eight-strike raid cratered its runway, while denying any plan to deploy Turkish troops. August 19, 2026 · Justice

Lebanon hands over an ex-Assad general to Syria in a first since the regime fell

Lebanon on August 19 extradited Major General Adel Issa, the former commander of the Syrian army's 17th Division and its ground forces in Deir ez-Zor until 2016, to face trial in Damascus on charges that include intentional killing, torture resulting in death, and inciting sectarian strife. It is the first handover of an Assad-era military officer by Beirut since the regime collapsed, carried out under a 1951 bilateral judicial agreement.

Trump declares an Economic D-Day on Iran and its enablers, a squeeze routed through Iraq

President Donald Trump announced what he called an Economic D-Day against Iran, warning that any country whose banks, businesses, airports, or government entities give Tehran any lifeline will face what he called tremendous economic consequences, and naming oil smuggling, swap lines, cash transfers, exchange houses, ship registries, and front companies as targets. Much of that shadow network runs through Iraq, and Washington layered fresh sanctions on Hezbollah on August 20 as Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi dismissed the campaign as a distraction. August 19, 2026 · Energy

Iraq's cabinet clears a three-month mechanism to market crude around the closed strait

Iraq's Council of Ministers approved a mechanism to market crude through specialist international and domestic firms across multiple export outlets under three-month contracts starting September 1, and authorized the oil minister to open new transport routes, renew deals, and import fuel to head off shortages. The decision formalizes Baghdad's drive to move oil around the closed Strait of Hormuz and cut its reliance on throttled southern Gulf terminals.

Iran's parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf arrived in Baghdad on August 19 for a three-day visit, pressing to keep Iraqi oil moving and backing factions resisting the government's push to bring all weapons under state control before a September 30 disarmament deadline and an October 1 foreign-troop withdrawal. Kuwait

August 20, 2026 · Energy

KPC chief calls the Hormuz shutdown Kuwait's worst oil crisis since 1990

Kuwait Petroleum Corporation chief executive Sheikh Nawaf Saud al-Sabah called the Strait of Hormuz closure the sector's worst crisis since Iraq's 1990 invasion, telling AFP the company is now storing 7 to 8 million barrels of crude closer to Asian buyers, roughly 3.5 million in Japan and about 4 million at South Korea's Ulsan, while enlarging its tanker fleet and negotiating bypass pipelines with Saudi Arabia and the UAE. He said Kuwait, which has no export route but the blocked Gulf, could restore output to pre-war levels quickly once the strait reopens but warned there is no substitute for guaranteed freedom of navigation. Al-Monitor
August 19, 2026 · Politics

Kuwait approves economic courts and tighter nationality rules in a reform package

Kuwait's cabinet approved a judicial package creating dedicated economic courts at the first-instance, appeal, and cassation levels to fast-track commercial disputes from October 2027, alongside amendments to the Nationality Law that bar naturalized citizens from voting, running for office, or sitting on parliamentary bodies and widen the grounds to revoke citizenship for fraud. Justice Minister Nasser al-Sumait framed the courts as a boost to investor confidence under Vision 2035.

Kurdistan

August 20, 2026 · Politics

The KDP and PUK inch toward a Kurdistan cabinet after a 19-month deadlock

A senior Kurdistan Democratic Party official, Ali Hussein, said behind-the-scenes talks with the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan have made solid progress this week toward forming the tenth KRG cabinet and reactivating a parliament dormant for roughly 19 months, with more meetings expected in the coming days. The two sides remain apart on power-sharing and the sequence for reviving parliament, and relations have been strained by disputes over messages relayed through a mediator, leaving any breakthrough unconfirmed.
