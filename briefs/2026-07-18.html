Syria

July 16, 2026 · Energy

Syria settles into a Mediterranean oil-transit role as Iraqi crude moves to Baniyas

With the Strait of Hormuz shut into a second week, Baghdad is running crude and naphtha overland to Syria's coastal ports of Baniyas and Tartus, a workaround approved by the Iraqi cabinet in June that officials now describe as permanent even if Gulf shipping recovers. SOMO is opening an office at Baniyas and the plan targets some 50,000 barrels a day and up to 650,000 tons of oil a month, handing post-Assad Damascus a transit corridor and hard currency at a moment when Washington has backed the wider Iraq to Syria route. Iraq

July 16, 2026 · Security

A drone hits a tanker at the Basra Oil Terminal, forcing a brief halt to southern loadings

Iraq suspended loading at the Basra Oil Terminal on July 16 after an unidentified drone struck a tanker in Iraqi territorial waters, towing the vessel and a second ship clear of the loading zone before operations resumed the same day. SOMO chief said the terminal was not the target, noting loading was at normal rates depending on vessel availability, but the incident showed how exposed Iraq's main southern outlet has become as the Gulf war grinds on.

Arab News
July 17, 2026 · Energy

Baghdad's southern exports stay down 70 percent as it leans harder on the northern pipeline

Southern production remains crippled by the blockade, with output at the Zubair field down to roughly 250,000 barrels a day from about 400,000 and overall southern exports off around 70 percent, forcing Baghdad to reroute barrels north through the Kirkuk to Ceyhan pipeline. Brent settled near 86 dollars on July 17 and was on course for a weekly gain above 10 percent, a squeeze felt sharply in a country that still draws about 90 percent of its budget from oil sales.

Kuwait

July 17, 2026 · Security High impact

An Iranian strike hits a Kuwaiti power and water desalination plant, wounding soldiers

Kuwait said a July 17 Iranian attack damaged one of its combined power generation and water desalination stations, sparking a fire and knocking out a large number of electricity generation units while drones targeting army camps wounded several Land Force personnel who were reported in stable condition. It is the first hit on Kuwait's critical water infrastructure since the war reignited, a serious escalation for a country that draws about 90 percent of its drinking water from desalination.

Al Arabiya
July 17, 2026 · Infrastructure

The strike exposes Kuwait's deep dependence on desalinated water

The hit on the desalination station laid bare a structural vulnerability in one of the world's driest countries, where nearly all fresh water is pulled from the sea by pushing it through ultrafine reverse-osmosis membranes. Firefighters brought the blaze under control and technical crews began restoring the affected generation units, but the attack signals that Iran is now reaching for the civilian lifelines that keep the Gulf states running, not just their military bases.

Kurdistan

July 17, 2026 · Politics

Baghdad and Erbil reach a deal to resume Kurdish oil exports and release salaries

The federal government approved a deal on July 17 to resume oil exports from the Kurdistan Region and clear public sector pay, with the KRG handing its full current output of about 80,000 barrels a day to SOMO in exchange for the release of salaries for more than a million civil servants. The agreement caps months of standoff over how much crude Erbil owes the treasury and how much Baghdad owes in wages, though production still sits far below the roughly 280,000 barrels a day the Region pumped before repeated drone strikes on its fields.

The Arab Weekly
July 17, 2026 · Energy

The Region's pipeline becomes Iraq's main working artery, now carrying blended Basra crude

As Hormuz stays restricted, southern Basra crude is being trucked north, blended with Kirkuk oil and pushed through the Kurdistan Region's pipeline to Turkey's Ceyhan port, lifting total flows on the northern route to around 230,000 barrels a day. The arrangement turns the KRG line into Iraq's principal working export outlet and hands Erbil rare leverage in the wider budget fight, even as Baghdad presses to widen the route further.
