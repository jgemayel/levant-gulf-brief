Syria

July 15, 2026 · Economy

Damascus and Beirut agree to deepen economic integration in fresh cross-border talks

Syria and Lebanon agreed to expand trade and investment ties during talks in Damascus between Syrian Economy Minister Nidal al-Shaar and his Lebanese counterpart Amer Bisat, pledging to ease business activity, strengthen institutional links and advance joint projects. The push builds on Syria's wider reopening since sanctions eased, with Gulf, Turkish, US and European investment pledges stacking up even as a severe domestic economic crisis presses on the Sharaa government.

SANA
July 15, 2026 · Energy

A US-backed pipeline plan would make Syria's Baniyas a Mediterranean outlet for Iraqi crude

The oil-export plan advanced during the Iraqi prime minister's Washington visit would run a line from Basra up to Haditha and on to Turkey's Ceyhan and Syria's Baniyas terminal, positioning the Syrian coast as a Mediterranean outlet for Iraqi and Gulf barrels that the shut Strait of Hormuz can no longer move. For Damascus the route promises transit revenue and deeper integration with an Arab, Turkish and Western energy network, though building it out would take years and heavy capital. Iraq

July 15, 2026 · Politics High impact

Al-Zaidi leaves the White House with a September 30 US troop-exit pledge and a raft of deals

President Trump hosted Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi at the White House and both men said the fewer than 2,000 US troops still in Iraq would withdraw fully by September 30, the same date al-Zaidi has set for armed factions to disarm, while Trump promised "a lot of deals" and praised his "tremendous chemistry" with the businessman-turned-premier. Al-Zaidi used the trip to press for a fairer OPEC production share and to cast Baghdad as neutral in the widening US-Iran war, with disarmament of Iran-backed militias a central thread through his talks with Trump and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

Al Jazeera
July 15, 2026 · Security

The US reimposes its blockade of Iran and strikes its coast, keeping Iraq in the crossfire

US forces reimposed a naval blockade of Iranian ports and ran a fresh multi-hour wave of strikes on dozens of coastal military assets near the Strait of Hormuz, killing seven Iranian personnel near Iranshahr and hitting Hengam Island, after the Revolutionary Guard said it struck two non-compliant supertankers in the strait. Iran answered with missiles and drones against US positions in Bahrain, Jordan and Kuwait, and Brent crude held above 85 dollars as Trump warned he could target Iranian power plants and bridges next unless Tehran returns to talks, leaving Iraq's oil arteries and US bases squarely inside the war.

CBS News
July 15, 2026 · Economy

Washington backs a Basra to Haditha pipeline to route Iraqi crude around Hormuz

An agreement tied to al-Zaidi's visit would see Iraq, US firms Chevron and TI Capital and Qatar's UCC build a pipeline linking southern Basra to Haditha in the west, and from there on to Turkey's Ceyhan and Syria's Baniyas, with a projected capacity near 2 million barrels a day. The plan is Baghdad's most ambitious bid yet to hedge against the Hormuz shutdown that has choked its seaborne exports, and it dovetails with Iraq's stated goal of lifting production capacity toward 7 million barrels a day by the end of the decade.

Kuwait

July 15, 2026 · Security High impact

Kuwait intercepts six missiles and 33 drones as a navy vessel is hit and four sailors wounded

Kuwait's air defences intercepted six hostile missiles and 33 weaponised drones aimed at energy and government sites overnight, with a navy vessel struck and four service members wounded and evacuated in stable condition, in what analysts called the heaviest assault on the country since the June airport attack. The barrage came as Iran stepped up strikes on US allies across the Gulf after the interim Tehran-Washington truce collapsed, scattering shrapnel across residential and industrial areas.

Sharjah24
July 15, 2026 · Economy

The reimposed blockade halts Kuwait's oil exports again and cuts output to domestic needs

With the US blockade back in force and Hormuz effectively shut, Kuwait's crude exports have halted once more and production has been pared to domestic needs, undoing the recovery that had followed KPC lifting all wartime force majeure on June 19 and rebuilding output toward 2 million barrels a day. Kuwait pumped about 2.6 million barrels a day before the war, and a prolonged shutdown revives the cargo deferrals and storage strain that defined the spring blockade.

Kurdistan

July 15, 2026 · Security

Three drones are shot down over the US consulate in Erbil, the first such strike since April

Air defences downed three explosive-laden drones near the US consulate in Erbil on Wednesday evening, with AFP journalists reporting drones over the city before interceptions sent up smoke, and no casualties among civilians or security personnel. No group claimed the attack, the first near the consulate since a fragile ceasefire took hold in April, as the renewed US-Iran war spills back onto Kurdish soil.

Shafaq News
July 15, 2026 · Economy

Al-Zaidi commits to equal salary funding and oil guarantees for the Kurdistan Region

Prime Minister al-Zaidi committed to funding Kurdistan Region public-sector salaries on an equal footing and to honouring oil guarantees to Erbil, as Baghdad transferred July wages worth about 957 billion dinars and the KRG deposited its 120 billion dinar non-oil share with the federal treasury. The moves lean on the US-brokered oil-for-salaries arrangement that Baghdad's cabinet has rolled over for another year, keeping Erbil's revenue base steady while the northern Ceyhan line carries the Region's crude clear of Hormuz.
