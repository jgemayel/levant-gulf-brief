August 3, 2026 · Diplomacy

Barzani makes the first visit to Damascus by an Iraqi Kurdish leader since Assad's fall

Kurdistan Region President Nechirvan Barzani met Syrian President Ahmad al-Sharaa at the People's Palace on Monday, the first trip to Damascus by an Iraqi Kurdish leader since Bashar al-Assad fell in December 2024, arriving with a delegation of Kurdish businessmen to open economic ties. Al-Sharaa's office said the talks centered on strengthening cooperation and on procedures for folding the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces into the state under the January 29 accord, giving Erbil a visible hand in a file it has long watched warily. Iraq

August 4, 2026 · Energy

Baghdad says an understanding with Iran on Hormuz tanker passage is days away

Oil Minister Bassem Mohammed Khudair al-Abadi said an Iraqi delegation is in talks with Iran on smooth tanker passage through the Strait of Hormuz and expects to reach an understanding within days, telling the state news agency the government is leaning on partnerships with neighbors to steady the economy. Southern exports through the Gulf have slowed to a trickle while the strait stays shut, making a deal that frees Iraqi crude the fastest route back to normal flows.

August 3, 2026 · Economy High impact

Iraq's first-half oil revenue more than halves to 18.67 billion dollars as Hormuz bites

SOMO figures show Iraq earned 18.67 billion dollars from crude exports in the first half of 2026 on roughly 268 million barrels shipped, less than half the 606.58 million barrels moved in the same period a year earlier, as the Hormuz closure and regional war throttle the country's main revenue line. Oil still funds around 90 percent of the federal budget, so the collapse in volumes is draining the treasury even with the northern Kirkuk to Ceyhan route reactivated.

Kuwait

August 2, 2026 · Governance

Kuwait passes a tough new law against commercial concealment

Kuwait issued Decree-Law No. 78 of 2026 to combat commercial concealment, a 14-article framework that bans unlicensed economic activity, carries prison terms of one to three years and fines of 10,000 to 100,000 dinars, and orders the confiscation of illicit proceeds, closure of offending businesses and deportation of foreign violators. Informants can claim up to 10 percent of collected fines, part of a drive to widen state oversight and pull hidden trade into the tax net.

Arab Times
August 2, 2026 · Public Finance

Kuwait leans on new taxes to rebuild non-oil income as Hormuz guts crude revenue

The concealment crackdown dovetails with a wider push to build non-oil income while the shut strait chokes oil revenue, with a 15 percent tax on multinationals set to raise about 250 million dinars a year and a planned sin tax on tobacco, sugary drinks and luxuries adding close to 200 million more. Kuwait is banking on those levies to lift non-oil revenue toward a record 3.5 billion dinars in the 2026-27 fiscal year, a partial hedge against a deficit penciled near 9.8 billion dinars.

August 3, 2026 · Fiscal

Erbil presses Baghdad for August and September salaries as back entitlements pile up

A KRG delegation urged Baghdad to release August and September public-sector salaries and to ease the fixed 120 billion dinar monthly deduction the federal government takes from the Region's transfers, arguing the cut is unsustainable as Erbil's internal revenue falls. The KRG says Baghdad has handed over only about 41 percent of the 58.3 trillion dinars it was owed for 2023 to 2025, roughly 24.3 trillion dinars, even as the Region keeps remitting its oil and non-oil income under the pay-for-oil deal.
