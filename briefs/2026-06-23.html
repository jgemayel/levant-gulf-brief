Syria

June 22, 2026 · Energy

US firm HKN Energy begins operating seven of Syria's largest northeastern oil fields under a 25-year production-sharing deal

Texas-based HKN Energy has started production at seven oil fields in northeastern Hasakah, including the large Suwaidiyah and Rmeilan fields along with Qarachokh, Sazaba, Alyan, Layla, and Hamza, under a 25-year agreement that splits output 60 percent to HKN, 32 percent to the state Syrian Petroleum Company, and 8 percent to al-Jazeera Oil Services. The cluster holds more than 1,300 wells and ranks among Syria's biggest oil assets, making the entry the first major international takeover of fields previously run by the Kurdish-led administration and the largest foreign energy commitment since the war.

Al-Sharaa says Syria has moved to seal and reinforce the Lebanon border against Hezbollah smuggling while ruling out any military role

In a June 22 interview, President Ahmed al-Sharaa said his forces have reinforced the Syria-Lebanon frontier to stop Hezbollah weapons smuggling through the Bekaa, while again rejecting any Syrian military intervention in Lebanon and insisting Damascus wants economic ties with Beirut, not military ones. He called Hezbollah's past role in Syria a mistake that left fresh scars but warned against any slide back to war, urging support for the Lebanese state and its institutions instead. June 21, 2026 · Aviation

Syria plans a July return of direct Damascus-Amsterdam flights, its first scheduled European route besides Moscow since the war

Syria's civil aviation authority said Syrian Airlines aims to resume nonstop service between Damascus and Amsterdam Schiphol in the first week of July, the first scheduled link to a European capital other than Moscow since flights were halted during the war. Authority chief Omar Hosari said Syrian and Dutch regulators are finishing technical and legal procedures and that talks with Germany on easing its own restrictions are expected to conclude in the first half of July, with more European destinations to follow.

Iraq

June 22, 2026 · Energy High impact

Turkey rejects Iraq's bid to extend the Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline treaty, leaving the country's only working export route exposed weeks from expiry

A senior Turkish official said Ankara has refused Baghdad's request to extend the Iraq-Turkey pipeline treaty past its July 27 expiry, arguing there is no value in prolonging an accord that has been tied up in international arbitration, with the two governments instead drafting a replacement. The rejection leaves Iraq's only functioning international crude outlet without a clear legal basis just weeks from the deadline, after the Hormuz disruption already gutted southern exports and forced Baghdad to lean on the northern line.

Parliament sets a July 5 vote to fill the nine vacant cabinet posts, including defense and interior, before al-Zaidi's Washington summit

The Hikma Movement said parliament has fixed July 5 to vote on the nine portfolios still empty in Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi's government, among them the security-critical defense and interior ministries, completing the cabinet ahead of his planned summit in Washington. Lawmakers confirmed only 14 of 23 ministers when al-Zaidi was sworn in on May 14, leaving the arms-monopoly file and the most sensitive posts hostage to disputes over candidates and bloc shares.

Iraqi News
June 21, 2026 · Energy

Iraq's May crude output sinks to a multi-decade low near 1.41 million barrels a day as Basra Gulf loadings falter

Iraqi oil production fell to roughly 1.41 million barrels a day in May, a multi-decade low down from 1.85 million in March and far beneath last year's 4.79 million average, as faltering Basra Gulf loadings during the Hormuz disruption strangled southern exports. The slump is the engine behind Baghdad's scramble for backup outlets, from tripling northern pipeline volumes toward 770,000 barrels a day to overland trucking, including a planned route carrying Basra crude to Syria's Mediterranean ports.

Kuwait

June 19, 2026 · Energy

Kuwait courts Saudi and Emirati pipelines to move its crude around Hormuz even as it races output back toward 2 million barrels a day

KPC chief executive Sheikh Nawaf Saud Al-Sabah said Kuwait is in talks with Saudi Arabia and the UAE to route its barrels through their export lines, the Saudi East-West pipeline to Yanbu on the Red Sea and the UAE's ADCOP link to Fujairah, so future crude can reach market without transiting the Strait of Hormuz. The structural hedge comes as Kuwait pushes production back toward 2 million barrels a day within a week of Hormuz reopening, the lesson of a war that idled most of its exports and exposed its dependence on a single chokepoint.

Kurdistan

June 22, 2026 · Energy

Kurdistan Region crude exports top 200,000 barrels a day as Baghdad confirms it will renew the interim oil-export deal

A senior Iraqi oil official confirmed Kurdistan Region crude exports have climbed past 200,000 barrels a day, and Baghdad said the interim agreement governing those flows, set to lapse this month, will be renewed so Kurdish oil keeps reaching Ceyhan. Officials signaled the next federal budget round would recalibrate export volumes toward the region's full capacity, a rare easing in the Erbil-Baghdad oil file that nonetheless sits under the shadow of the July 27 pipeline-treaty expiry now threatening the same route.

A delegation from Canada's all-party Parliamentary Friends of the Kurds visited Erbil on June 22 and met President Masoud Barzani, reaffirming Ottawa's support for the Kurdistan Region and signaling interest in expanding political, security, and civil-society ties.
