Syria

August 1, 2026 · Politics

Damascus says the SDF merger has reached its final stages

The presidential team said the deal to fold the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces into the national army has reached its final stages at the military and security levels, with officials signaling that a formal disbandment of the SDF and dissolution of the northeast Autonomous Administration may now be close after months of implementation. The step would bring the largest armed force outside state control under Damascus for the first time since Assad's fall, alongside efforts to ease returns of the displaced.

Al-Sharaa closes ranks with Riyadh after the strikes on Saudi oil sites and Gulf tankers

In a call with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, President Ahmed al-Sharaa reaffirmed Syria's support for Saudi Arabia and condemned the Iranian-linked drone attacks on the Kingdom's oil facilities and the strikes on Saudi and Qatari tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. The alignment pulls Damascus firmly toward the Gulf as the US-Iran war chokes regional energy and drives Baghdad's scramble for export routes. Translated from SANA.

Iraq

August 1, 2026 · Energy High impact

Iraq and Turkey sign a one-year deal to pump 750,000 barrels a day to Ceyhan

Baghdad and Ankara signed a one-year agreement on Saturday setting a floor of 750,000 barrels a day through the 986-kilometer Kirkuk-Ceyhan line, struck between Turkey's BOTAS and Iraq's SOMO and NOC while the two sides finalize a broader oil, electricity and water framework. The pact hardens Iraq's main non-Hormuz outlet at a moment when southern exports remain bottled up behind the throttled strait, which normally carries the bulk of the country's crude.

Al Jazeera
August 1, 2026 · Oil

Baghdad bets it can still move most of its crude past Hormuz on non-US tankers

Iraqi energy economist Nabil al-Marsoumi argued that Iraq could still ship close to 70 percent of its crude through Hormuz on non-US-flagged tankers that sidestep the Revolutionary Guard's ban on vessels moving under US escort. The workaround is being floated as southern flows sit near 200,000 barrels a day and total output languishes around 1.9 million, roughly half of February's level, keeping the pressure on the Kurdish and Turkish routes to fill the gap.

Kuwait

July 31, 2026 · Security

Kuwait shoots down Iranian drones aimed at its air bases

Kuwait's military said it detected and destroyed hostile drones that crossed into its airspace at dawn on Friday, after Iran claimed kamikaze strikes on the Ali al-Salem and Ahmad al-Jaber air bases. Falling shrapnel caused material damage but no casualties, and the defense ministry condemned the continued aggression and vowed to protect the country's sovereignty. The bases host US forces, keeping Kuwait squarely in the war's line of fire.

Arab Times
August 1, 2026 · Aviation

Kuwait holds its skies shut to overflights as diversions pile up

Kuwait kept its airspace closed to overflights until at least August 4, leaving the Kuwait flight information region open only to approved arrivals and departures, as Kuwait Airways rescheduled most of its services and inbound traffic was diverted to Doha, Riyadh and Dubai. The restriction extends the disruption that began with the July 18 drone strikes on Kuwait International Airport and continues to snarl Gulf connectivity.

Kurdistan

July 31, 2026 · Energy

Erbil presses Baghdad to more than double its crude allocation as fuel lines bite

The KRG asked Baghdad to lift the Region's daily crude allocation from 50,000 to 126,700 barrels, the volume matching its 12.67 percent share of Iraq's population, and capped petrol prices to ease weeks of gasoline shortages and overnight fuel queues. Prime Minister Masrour Barzani tied the crunch to a shortfall in federal crude and to war-driven production cuts at the Khor Mor gas field, the Region's main domestic supply.

August 1, 2026 · Security High impact

A US-led coalition pulls its air defenses out of Erbil, leaving the Region exposed

American, British, French and German forces withdrew hundreds of troops and key air-defense systems from Erbil International Airport in a 72-hour pull-out, stripping the Kurdistan Region of protection as Iranian and militia drone and missile attacks intensify. The Region has absorbed more than 950 such strikes since late February, and with the coalition umbrella gone, most energy firms remain offline and Erbil is left leaning on Baghdad for both cover and crude.
