Syria

August 8, 2026 · Security

Women's units and the courts become the last knots in the SDF merger

After President Ahmad al-Sharaa's August 4 talks with SDF commander Mazloum Abdi, the hardest pieces of the January 29 integration deal are now the fate of the Kurdish women's protection units, which sought a place inside a Defense Ministry that has no women in its ranks, alongside unsettled education and judicial files. Four SDF brigades have already folded into the ministry, and officials still describe the military track as the most advanced part of the merger.

August 10, 2026 · Security

Syrian forces press an anti-ISIS sweep in the east as attacks persist

Interior Ministry internal security units are running a stepped-up campaign against ISIS in Deir ez-Zor province, dismantling cells and seizing explosive belts and weapons in a string of early-August raids, even as the group keeps hitting back with attacks on soldiers in the eastern countryside. The operations underline how the desert east remains the toughest security file for Damascus while it works to consolidate control retaken from the SDF.

Iraq

August 9, 2026 · Energy High impact

Iran says the Oman deal alone will not reopen Hormuz, and ties it to Washington

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said the near-final coordinates deal with Oman is a technical and legal arrangement that does not mean the Strait of Hormuz has reopened, and that a full reopening now hinges on the United States making amends for what Tehran calls a violation of the June memorandum of understanding. Iran is pressing for a transit fee of 5 to 7 percent of cargo value and says it would bar US and Israeli-linked vessels, dimming hopes of a quick restart for Iraq, which moves about 94 percent of its crude through the strait.

NBC News
August 8, 2026 · Energy

Oil minister says Iraqi crude exports have collapsed 75 percent

Oil Minister Hayan Abdul-Ghani said on August 8 that Iraq's crude exports have fallen 75 percent because of the Hormuz closure and the wider war, leaving the country shipping only about a quarter of the roughly 3.5 million barrels a day it sold before hostilities began on February 28. The shortfall has gutted the revenue that funds close to 90 percent of the federal budget.

Iraqi News
August 8, 2026 · Public Finance

Iraq's domestic debt climbs to a record 79 billion dollars

Iraqi domestic debt hit an all-time high of 103 trillion dinars, about 79 billion dollars, at the end of May, up from 90.5 trillion dinars at the close of 2025, as Baghdad borrows to cover salaries and services while Hormuz throttles oil income. The central bank holds the bulk of the claims, with a further 20.3 trillion dinars in loans and 10.9 trillion in bonds, and the trajectory points steeply upward.

Kuwait

August 8, 2026 · Sovereign Rating

Fitch keeps Kuwait at AA- as huge buffers absorb the war fallout

Fitch affirmed Kuwait's AA- sovereign rating, citing exceptionally strong fiscal and external balance sheets, with sovereign net foreign assets put at about 668 percent of GDP, the highest ratio among all Fitch-rated sovereigns. The high grade holds even as the Hormuz closure hammers the crude revenue that dominates state income, leaving Kuwait far better cushioned than its northern neighbors.

August 8, 2026 · Energy

Kuwaiti output rebounds toward 2 million barrels while exports route around the strait

Kuwait lifted crude production to about 1.97 million barrels a day in July, up from 1.65 million in June and just 580,000 in May, the largest monthly gain among OPEC+ producers as the group rolls back its voluntary cuts with a further 188,000-barrel raise for September. With no pipeline of its own to bypass Hormuz, Kuwait is still shipping heavily through the UAE's Fujairah terminal and ship-to-ship transfers to keep barrels moving while the strait stays contested. Kurdistan

August 7, 2026 · Energy

KRG accuses Khor Mor operators of a breach over gas sent to Baghdad

The Kurdistan Regional Government publicly rejected the account given by Dana Gas and Crescent Petroleum of the start of natural gas deliveries from the Khor Mor field to Iraq's federal Ministry of Electricity, calling the operators' unilateral move a breach of contract and an unwarranted interference in KRG-Baghdad arrangements. Erbil said it was told of the deliveries on August 4 only as the companies went public, and warned the step falls outside the framework governing the joint field.

August 8, 2026 · Energy

The Kirkuk-Ceyhan line carries Iraq's export lifeline north around Hormuz

With Hormuz still throttling southern shipments, Iraq is leaning on the Kurdistan pipeline to Turkey's Ceyhan port, even rerouting some Basra crude north, to keep a slice of exports flowing to the Mediterranean. SOMO moved about 7 million barrels through Ceyhan in July, and Baghdad has approved a plan to ramp the northern route from roughly 220,000 toward 770,000 barrels a day, making the KRG-hosted line the country's most secure export outlet.
