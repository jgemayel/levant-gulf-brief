Syria

August 13, 2026 · Politics

Baghdad and Damascus set up joint judicial committees over thousands of Syrian detainees

Iraq's Supreme Judicial Council chief Faiq Zidan met President Ahmed al-Sharaa and Justice Minister Mazhar al-Wais in Damascus on August 13, agreeing to form joint committees to handle the cases of Syrians held in Iraqi prisons and to draft an accord on transferring and prosecuting detainees. Rights groups put the number of Syrians in Iraqi custody at roughly 3,500, many moved from the northeast and held on ISIS-related files, with families still pressing for information and the return of those facing no substantiated charges. The talks mark a concrete step in normalizing Baghdad-Damascus legal ties after Assad's fall.

Enab Baladi
August 14, 2026 · Economy

Syria's recovery gathers pace with growth tipped near 10 percent

Syria's economy is projected to grow close to 10 percent in 2026, roughly double last year's pace, as eased sanctions, steadier security, and returning professionals lift activity, with the IMF pointing to sustained momentum in the rebound. Trade is turning quickly toward new partners even as reconstruction needs run into the hundreds of billions of dollars, a bill some estimates place above 200 billion. The gains sit against a still-fragile base, with power, banking, and industry only starting to reconnect to the wider region.

Iraq

August 14, 2026 · Security High impact

Iran-aligned militias warn of civil strife as Iraq's disarmament deadline nears

The Badr Organisation warned Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi's government that forcing Iran-aligned factions to disarm before a September 30 deadline could trigger internal strife, setting conditions that include a full US withdrawal and stronger national forces. Quds Force commander Esmail Qaani visited Baghdad this week seeking a formula that would let the groups keep their weapons, while al-Zaidi has said factions that refuse to disarm will face terrorism charges and insisted all foreign forces will leave by October 1. The standoff is the sharpest test yet of Baghdad's authority over the armed groups that have driven months of attacks across Iraq and the Kurdistan Region.

August 14, 2026 · Energy High impact

Iraq's crude exports rebound to 2 million barrels a day, the highest since the war began

Oil Minister Bassem Mohammed Khudair said on August 14 that Iraq's average daily crude exports have climbed to about 2 million barrels this month, the strongest rate since US and Israeli strikes on Iran on February 28 choked the Strait of Hormuz. To lock in the recovery and cut its reliance on the strait, Baghdad is advancing two new export routes, a Basra to Fishkhabur line feeding Turkey's Ceyhan terminal and a Haditha to Baniyas pipeline reaching the Mediterranean through Syria. Iraq pumped around 4 million barrels a day and shipped an average of 105 million barrels a month before the war, mostly out of Basra.

Kuwait

August 12, 2026 · Energy

Kuwait's oil output climbs to 1.8 million barrels a day as some crude slips past Hormuz

Kuwait lifted crude production to about 1.8 million barrels a day in July from 1.5 million in June, a third straight monthly gain as producers found ways to move limited volumes despite the blockade, though output stayed roughly 25 percent below year-earlier levels. Gulf supply is recovering unevenly, with millions of barrels a day still shut in and war-risk insurance and transponder-off shuttles shaping what actually reaches buyers. Kuwait, which sent more than 2 million barrels a day through the strait before the war and leans on crude sales for about 80 percent of its budget, remains far from pre-conflict flows.

Kurdistan

August 15, 2026 · Security

Baghdad and Erbil tighten border security as Aboudi leads a delegation to the Region

Iraq's national security adviser Qasim al-Aboudi led a high-level delegation to Erbil on August 15, received at the airport by KRG Interior Minister Rebar Ahmed, to review the rollout of a joint Iraq-Iran security arrangement covering border control, smuggling, and infiltration. Aboudi also met Kurdistan Democratic Party leader Masoud Barzani, with both sides stressing direct communication and joint coordination under directives from the armed forces commander-in-chief. The visit signals steadier Baghdad-Erbil security ties after months in which drones and missiles tied to Iran-aligned groups repeatedly struck the Region's territory and energy sites.

Kurdistan24
August 8, 2026 · Energy

The KRG accuses its own operators of diverting Khor Mor gas to Baghdad

The Kurdistan Regional Government says Dana Gas and Crescent Petroleum breached their contract by starting to pipe roughly 100 million cubic feet a day of Khor Mor gas to the federal Electricity Ministry's Taza plant in Kirkuk without its approval, learning of the one-year deal only when the operators announced it on August 4. Erbil calls the move an unwarranted interference in its arrangements with Baghdad and vows to take all appropriate measures, while the operators reject the breach claim, leaving the standoff unresolved. The fight cuts to who controls gas extracted on Kurdish soil at a moment when the Region is still short of the power that Khor Mor normally feeds.
