Syria

August 26, 2026 · Security

A day after the SDF dissolves, a unified Syrian army is still on paper

With the Syrian Democratic Forces formally wound down, attention has turned to how much of a single national army actually exists, and the answer so far is not much. Vetting of the SDF's divisions finished on July 31 and their units were renamed to match Syrian Arab Army nomenclature on August 1, but command lines, force deployment, and who controls the northeast on the ground remain unsettled, with Mazloum Abdi and Ilham Ahmed still holding talks in Damascus to close the gaps by month end. The dissolution was the easy part, and the integration it was meant to seal is still being negotiated.

A Latakia raid breaks up a Captagon and hashish network

The Interior Ministry's Drug Enforcement Department dismantled a trafficking network in Latakia province and arrested three of its members after a period of surveillance, seizing 45 kilograms of hashish, 25,000 Captagon pills, 400 grams of crystal methamphetamine, and two handguns. The bust is part of a widening campaign against the Captagon trade that flourished under the former regime and that the new government has made a priority for its ties with Gulf states. Iraq

August 26, 2026 · Economy High impact

Iraq's deficit triples toward 16 billion dollars as Hormuz chokes oil revenue

Iraq's budget shortfall has more than tripled in two months to around 16 billion dollars as disrupted exports through the Strait of Hormuz gut the crude sales that fund roughly 90 percent of the state, forcing Baghdad to lean on loans and central bank support to keep salaries paid. With no 2026 budget passed, the government is now drafting a 2027 bill that its financial adviser Mazhar Muhammad Salih calls one of the most complex yet, built on a cautious oil price of 50 to 60 dollars a barrel against the 70 dollars used last year and due at parliament by mid-September. The gap sets up a hard fight with Erbil and the provinces over a shrinking pool of money.

August 23, 2026 · Security

A new PMF law threatens to entrench the militias the state is trying to disarm

As the September 30 disarmament deadline nears, a draft law working through parliament would formalize the Popular Mobilization Forces rather than fold them into the regular military, locking in a 238,000-strong parallel force with its own 3.6 billion dollar budget and dozens of Iran-aligned factions. Analysts warn the bill would cement the status quo and make later reform less likely, undercutting Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi's pledge to place all weapons under state control. It hands the hardline factions a legal shield just as Washington presses Baghdad to deliver.

Kuwait

August 26, 2026 · Energy

Kuwait's power grid clears the summer despite Iranian strikes on its plants

As the season winds down, the Ministry of Electricity, Water and Renewable Energy says it kept the grid intact through the summer of 2026 even after Iranian attacks damaged power generation facilities during the war. Officials credited round-the-clock work at generation and desalination plants along with a public conservation drive to trim peak-hour demand. Holding the system together through wartime strikes and extreme heat keeps a core service running while the export economy stays under pressure.

Amir Sheikh Meshal Al-Ahmad Al-Jaber Al-Sabah sent written messages to Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi and South Korean President Lee Jae-myung on strengthening bilateral relations and on current developments in the region. Kurdistan

August 26, 2026 · Economy

The KRG approves an emergency fuel plan and stakes a 29 trillion dinar budget claim

The Council of Ministers signed off on an emergency strategy to ease the fuel shortages and power cuts hitting Erbil and Duhok, and reaffirmed the Region's constitutional entitlements as it presses its case in the federal spending plan. Erbil has sent Baghdad a 29 trillion dinar draft for 2027 seeking a 14.1 percent share of the national budget to cover salaries, investment, and permanent contracts, with the federal bill due at parliament by mid-September and disputes over oil, domestic revenue, and the oil and gas law still open. The move puts the Region's fiscal demands on the table before the budget fight begins.

Erbil sends fresh cash north to Baghdad under the salary deal

The KRG has transferred more than 38 million dollars of domestic non-oil revenue to Baghdad under the arrangement that ties Kurdistan's share of federal salary funding to handing over crude and local income. The payment shows Erbil keeping up its side of a deal that has repeatedly stalled on Baghdad's, even as public workers wait on delayed pay. It is a bid to keep the salary channel open while the wider budget and oil disputes stay unresolved.
