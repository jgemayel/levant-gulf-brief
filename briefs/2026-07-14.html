Syria

July 13, 2026 · Economy

Damascus hosts its first Syrian-American business forum as Washington eases the way back in

The inaugural Syrian-American Business Forum opened Monday at Damascus's Dama Rose Hotel, with Economy and Industry Minister Nidal al-Shaar telling investors that recent US decisions were opening a new chapter in trade, finance and investment after years of isolation. The Syrian Petroleum Company's chief pitched the oil and gas sector to international firms and a US State Department official framed Syria's opening as a chance to shore up stability, days after Secretary of State Rubio moved to rescind Syria's state sponsor of terrorism designation.

Arab News
July 12, 2026 · Politics

Al-Sharaa charges the new People's Assembly with drafting a permanent constitution

President Ahmad al-Sharaa addressed the formal convening of Syria's 210-seat People's Assembly on Sunday, setting its central task as writing a permanent constitution and laying the groundwork for elected government after five decades of Assad family rule. Two thirds of the members were picked last year by regional electoral colleges and al-Sharaa appointed the remaining 70 seats himself, a balance critics say tilts the chamber toward the presidency during the transition.

Iraq

July 13, 2026 · Security High impact

US and Iran trade a third straight night of strikes as Trump orders a renewed Hormuz blockade and a 20 percent toll

US forces launched a third consecutive night of strikes on Iran after hitting roughly 140 military targets over the weekend, and President Trump said he would reimpose a blockade of the Strait of Hormuz and charge foreign ships a 20 percent toll for safe passage, a plan his own Secretary of State had called unworkable. Iran said it disabled two supertankers and the UAE reported two of its tankers hit by Iranian missiles in Omani waters, killing one crew member, keeping the waterway that normally carries about 90 percent of Iraq's 3.4 million barrels a day of exports effectively shut and pulling Baghdad deeper into a war it has tried to sit out.

NPR
July 13, 2026 · Politics

Al-Zaidi lands in Washington to sign oil and gas deals built around escaping Hormuz

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi arrived in Washington on Monday for talks with President Trump and a set of memorandums bringing US firms deeper into Iraqi energy, including Chevron in Basra and a US operator at the Akkas gas field, alongside a plan to build export routes that lessen the blow of any Hormuz closure. Baghdad is chasing a jump in capacity toward 7 million barrels a day and floated a fund that would set aside about 500,000 barrels a day in exchange for US help rebuilding the power grid, casting the trip as a shift from crisis management to a lasting economic partnership.

Al Jazeera
July 13, 2026 · Energy

Baghdad and Ankara race to renew the Ceyhan pipeline pact before it lapses this month

With Gulf shipping stalled, Iraq is leaning harder on its northern lifeline through the Kirkuk to Ceyhan pipeline even as the underlying transit agreement heads toward expiry late in July following Ankara's earlier termination notice. Losing the route would strip out roughly 220,000 barrels a day of northern exports just as southern seaborne cargoes struggle to find tankers willing to enter the strait, so Baghdad is pressing Turkey for renewal while weighing a wider ramp-up plan for the line. Kuwait

July 13, 2026 · Security High impact

Kuwait's air defences fight off a fresh Iranian barrage that damages an offshore platform

Kuwait's army said its air defences engaged hostile aerial targets overnight into Monday as Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed it hit a US surface to surface missile base in the country, saying it set fire to two HIMARS launchers and munitions stores, part of a wider salvo that also struck Bahrain, Jordan, Oman and Qatar. Kuwait reported damage to an offshore drilling platform, the first direct hit on its energy infrastructure in weeks, keeping the emirate squarely inside the widening arc of the US-Iran war.

Al Jazeera
July 13, 2026 · Markets

Kuwait's bourse shed nearly 4 billion dollars in the first half as war nerves bite

Kuwait's main index lost close to 4 billion dollars of value in the first six months of 2026 with traded value down 22 percent year on year to about 9.8 billion dinars, weighed down by profit-taking, thin liquidity and the return of Gulf war risk. Crude climbed about 4 percent back above 74 dollars a barrel on Monday as the strikes resumed, a reminder that the same conflict rattling Kuwaiti equities is also propping up the oil revenue the budget leans on.

Kurdistan

July 13, 2026 · Fiscal

Baghdad wires Erbil June wages but docks 120 billion dinars over missed non-oil revenue

The federal Finance Ministry transferred 841 billion dinars, about 641 million dollars, to cover June salaries for KRG public workers but withheld 120 billion dinars, roughly 91 million dollars, because Erbil again failed to hand over the monthly non-oil revenue Baghdad requires under the August 2025 mechanism. The KRG says its local income has fallen sharply and it may have to take on local loans to close the gap, keeping the salary file fragile even with Kurdish crude flowing again.

Iraqi News
July 13, 2026 · Security

Iran renews drone and missile strikes on Kurdish opposition camps near Sulaymaniyah

Iran launched a fresh round of drone and missile strikes on bases of Iranian Kurdish dissident groups inside the Kurdistan Region on Monday, with Iraqi security sources reporting a drone attack on an opposition camp near Sulaymaniyah alongside continued pressure around Erbil, whose airport hosts US forces. The bombardment extends the campaign that has repeatedly closed Kurdish airspace, again forcing airlines to reroute and putting the Region's civilians in the path of a war being fought over their territory.
