Syria

August 20, 2026 · Diplomacy

Syria denies a Turkish buildup at the bombed Idlib airbase as Netanyahu vows to block one

Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani told Axios on Wednesday there was no plan to build a Turkish base or deploy Turkish forces at the Abu al-Duhur airbase Israel struck on Tuesday, saying the site was only being rehabilitated for the Syrian Air Force and that he had said as much to Israel before the eight strikes. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu countered in a Likud video that Israel will not tolerate a Turkish military buildup pushing south toward its territory and said Damascus had been warned. The exchange keeps the Israel-Turkey contest over post-Assad Syria at a boil even after US envoy Tom Barrack called the raid an unnecessary escalation.

August 19, 2026 · Diplomacy

Syria's top diplomat lands in Islamabad, the first such visit in 19 years

Al-Shaibani arrived in Pakistan on Wednesday heading a high-level delegation for a two-day visit, the first by a Syrian foreign minister in 19 years and the first since Bashar al-Assad's fall, at the invitation of counterpart Ishaq Dar and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif. Talks cover the full span of bilateral ties including trade and defense, part of Damascus's drive to rebuild relationships well beyond the immediate region as it hunts for investment and diplomatic standing.

Baghdad asks Iran for special status to keep its oil moving through Hormuz

Iraq's parliament speaker used talks with visiting Iranian counterpart Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf in Baghdad on Wednesday to request special consideration for Iraqi crude passing through the Strait of Hormuz, arguing Iraq is among the states hardest hit by the war throttling the waterway. The appeal is the latest in Baghdad's scramble to keep exports flowing, alongside steep discounts and leaning on rival Gulf producers, with August shipments running near 2 million barrels a day against the roughly 4 million pumped before the conflict. The UAE severs all trade with Iran after a missile hits its waters and Trump declares an economic D-Day

The United Arab Emirates halted all trade, commercial exchange, and financial transactions with Iran effective immediately after two ballistic missiles were fired at the country and one landed in its territorial waters, cutting off a partner that trails only China and a top re-export route through Jebel Ali. President Donald Trump framed the rupture as an economic D-Day against Tehran as strikes on Gulf tankers ground on. The break tightens the vise on the whole lower Gulf, Kuwait included, which has no export outlet but the same contested waters. See the Iraq section.

Kuwait condemns the strikes on the UAE and presses for de-escalation

Kuwait condemned the continued Iranian attacks on the UAE and other Gulf states as a dangerous escalation and a breach of sovereignty, and urged a return to diplomacy to contain the crisis. Kurdistan

August 19, 2026 · Politics

The KDP and PUK close in on a new cabinet and a reactivated parliament

The two main Kurdish parties are near a deal to form the next Kurdistan Regional Government and revive the long-dormant regional parliament, an official said, with roughly 90 percent of the four-year cabinet program now drafted. The blueprint sets out ministerial powers, revenue distribution to the provinces, and crisis management, and the talks are also converging on a unified Kurdish candidate for the Iraqi presidency, nearly two years after the October 2024 vote left the KDP with 39 seats and the PUK with 23.
