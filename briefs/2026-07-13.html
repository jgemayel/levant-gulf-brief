Syria

July 12, 2026 · Politics

Syria's first post-Assad parliament elects a speaker as it holds its inaugural session

The 210-member People's Assembly held its first working session in Damascus on Sunday, took the constitutional oath and elected Abdul Hamid al-Awak, a former Justice Ministry judge from Hasakah, as speaker, 19 months after rebels led by President Ahmad al-Sharaa toppled Bashar al-Assad. Two thirds of the seats were filled through electoral colleges and a third appointed by al-Sharaa, who told members the country was moving to consolidate the state, while tight security kept the session's date under wraps until it convened.

PBS NewsHour
July 12, 2026 · Diplomacy

Damascus says the US terrorism delisting clears a path to reconstruction and investment

Syrian officials framed Washington's move to rescind the country's state-sponsor-of-terrorism designation, now in a 45-day congressional review, as the opening of a path to economic engagement, with the Foreign Ministry preparing a visit to Washington the week of July 13 to press reconstruction and investment ties. The delisting builds on the State Department's July 8 revocation of the HTS foreign-terrorist listing and comes as US companies signal interest in a market shut for nearly half a century.

Iraq

July 12, 2026 · Security High impact

US runs a third round of strikes on Iran as Iraq is pulled deeper into the war

US Central Command launched a third round of strikes on Iran within a week, following a Saturday wave that hit about 140 targets and pushed the week's tally past 300, saying the strikes aimed to degrade Iran's ability to attack shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. CENTCOM pushed back on Tehran's claim that the strait is closed even as the standoff kept Iraqi crude largely stranded, and pro-Iran factions kept up pressure on US positions, dragging Baghdad further into a war it has tried to sit out.

Iran International
July 12, 2026 · Energy

Baghdad races to triple northern crude exports to 770,000 barrels a day as Hormuz stays shut

With the Gulf route choked, Iraq is pushing to lift flows through the Kirkuk to Ceyhan pipeline from around 220,000 barrels a day toward 770,000, a plan the cabinet approved to route more crude north to the Mediterranean and away from Hormuz. The drive runs against the clock, with the Iraq to Turkey pipeline agreement set to expire on July 27, and it leans on Kurdistan's northern infrastructure to carry the additional barrels. Kuwait

July 12, 2026 · Security High impact

Iran strikes Kuwait's Ali Al Salem air base and the Emir condemns an attack from a friend

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it struck US infrastructure at Kuwait's Ali Al Salem air base, claiming to have destroyed fuel storage tanks and Patriot air-defence systems in a barrage that also hit Bahrain, Jordan and Qatar, while US Central Command said no American personnel were hurt. Emir Mishal al-Ahmad al-Jaber al-Sabah called it an unprovoked attack from a neighbouring Muslim country Kuwait considers a friend and had never allowed to use its land or airspace, a rare public rebuke of Tehran.

Al Jazeera
July 12, 2026 · Security

A drone hits a Kuwait Oil Company offshore platform and three northern border posts

Kuwait's Defence Ministry said a hostile drone struck an offshore drilling platform owned by the Kuwait Oil Company, causing material damage and wounding one worker, while three border posts in the north were also hit and damaged in the same wave. The ministry did not name who was responsible, and the United Arab Emirates and other Gulf states condemned the strikes on Kuwaiti sovereignty as the war's front edge pressed onto Kuwaiti oil infrastructure.

Kurdistan

July 12, 2026 · Energy

The KRG's Ceyhan pipeline becomes Iraq's main workaround as the Gulf route stays closed

Kurdistan's northern pipeline to Turkey's Ceyhan port is carrying the weight of Iraq's export bypass, with Baghdad aiming to lift throughput from about 220,000 barrels a day toward 770,000 to move crude that Hormuz can no longer carry. The route hands the Region strategic leverage even as the underlying Iraq to Turkey pipeline pact expires on July 27, leaving Erbil, Baghdad and Ankara to strike terms before the deadline. July 11, 2026 · Fiscal

Erbil rejects Baghdad's transfer claims and says it met every legal obligation

The KRG Finance Ministry rejected federal claims over budget transfers, saying the Region had fulfilled all its legal obligations and kept remitting Baghdad's share of non-oil revenue from January through May, with March and April declining sharply because of wartime conditions and the still-unimplemented ASYCUDA customs system at border crossings. The pushback keeps the salary and budget dispute live even as the oil route north gains value as a Hormuz bypass.
