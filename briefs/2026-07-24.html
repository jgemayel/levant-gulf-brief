Syria

July 19, 2026 · Politics

Al-Sharaa overhauls the security services, handing Khattab the national security file

President Ahmed al-Sharaa named Interior Minister Anas Khattab to also head the National Security Bureau, the body that oversees Syria's intelligence and policing agencies, while appointing Abdul Qader Tahan to run the General Intelligence Service and installing Hussein al-Salamah and Mulham al-Shantout in deputy security posts. Damascus framed the reshuffle as a move to formalize command lines and clear obstacles to reconstruction, folding the overlapping security roles Khattab held in the old Idlib administration into a single formal structure.

Al Jazeera
July 23, 2026 · Economy

Syria and the IMF map a medium-term fiscal framework as Article IV talks near

Finance Minister Mohammad Yisr Barnieh and a visiting IMF mission led by Ron van Rooden reviewed Syria's economic and financial priorities in Damascus, working through a medium-term macroeconomic framework, public debt sustainability, tax administration and financial sector reform, and floating sovereign sukuk as a new financing tool. The engagement is meant to rebuild the statistics and policy footing needed to resume Article IV consultations, a step that would mark Syria's return to the global financial system a year and a half after Assad's fall.

Iraq

July 23, 2026 · Economy High impact

Brent tops 100 dollars as Houthi strikes open a Red Sea front and Iraq's crude stays trapped

Brent rallied 6.45 percent to 101.10 dollars and touched 102 intraday, its first move above 100 since May, after Houthi fighters struck the Saudi tankers Encelia and Layla in the Red Sea and five more Saudi ships reversed course under a declared maritime embargo. The attacks close off the Red Sea workaround just as the US finishes a 13th straight night of strikes on Iran and the Hormuz standoff keeps Iraq's southern exports, normally about 90 percent of Baghdad's flow, effectively halted, with Goldman Sachs now warning Brent could reach 120 dollars by the fourth quarter.

The National
July 23, 2026 · Diplomacy

Iran rejects the ceasefire al-Zaidi carried to Tehran, leaving Hormuz unresolved

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi flew to Tehran with a US ceasefire proposal and met President Masoud Pezeshkian, top negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, only for Iran to reject it as a temporary deal that fails to settle who controls the Strait of Hormuz. The mission, coming days after al-Zaidi's White House visit, casts Baghdad as the war's go-between and puts its professed neutrality under strain as strikes continue across the region. The Jerusalem Post
July 21, 2026 · Energy

Baghdad puts its US energy package at 200 billion dollars across the biggest fields

Oil Minister Bassem Mohammed Khudair valued the agreements his ministry signed with US firms during al-Zaidi's Washington trip at about 200 billion dollars, the energy core of a wider set of 48 deals worth more than 60 billion across energy, health and technology. The line-up pulls ExxonMobil, Chevron, Halliburton, HKN, GE Vernova and Shell into the Hamrin, West Qurna-2, Nasiriyah, Balad, Bin Omar and Sindbad fields and pairs with a deal to build the Haditha to Baniyas pipeline toward the Syrian coast.

Arab News
July 22, 2026 · Energy

Iraq pushes July exports to 1.5 million barrels a day and asks Turkey to quadruple Ceyhan capacity

Combined shipments through Hormuz and the Kirkuk to Ceyhan line averaged about 1.5 million barrels a day in July before the latest escalation, roughly three times June's flow, as Baghdad unveiled a formal strategy to route crude around the strait. Iraq has asked Ankara to lift Kirkuk to Ceyhan capacity nearly fourfold toward 2.5 million barrels a day and extend it to Basra, part of a diversification drive that leans on the northern pipeline running through Kurdistan. Kuwait

July 21, 2026 · Security High impact

Iran hits Kuwait's power and desalination plants for a fourth straight day

Iranian strikes hit Kuwaiti power stations and water desalination plants for a fourth consecutive day, sparking fires and knocking several electricity-generating units offline, with a second plant struck within 24 hours of the first. The Ministry of Electricity, Water and Renewable Energy said the barrage, which state agency KUNA called heinous Iranian aggression, threatens supply in a country that draws about 90 percent of its drinking water from desalination.

Asharq Al-Awsat
July 24, 2026 · Security

Sirens sound again as Kuwait's air defenses confront a fresh missile and drone wave

Kuwait activated air defenses and sounded sirens nationwide overnight as the army reported confronting a fresh volley of hostile missiles and drones, with explosions heard across parts of the country. The wave landed as Iran pressed its retaliation against US bases and facilities in the Gulf, extending a campaign that has kept Kuwaiti skies contested for more than a week.

Kurdistan

July 23, 2026 · Security

Five drones downed over Erbil as the airport suspends flights

Coalition and Kurdish air defenses shot down five explosive-laden drones over Erbil, and Erbil International Airport briefly halted operations as interceptors engaged the incoming craft. More than 30 drones have been launched toward the city since US-Iran hostilities resumed, drawing the Kurdistan Region, a declared noncombatant in the war, deeper into the crossfire.

Kurdistan24
July 22, 2026 · Economy

Hormuz shutdown hands Erbil rare leverage even as Baghdad underpays its budget

With the strait shut and southern exports stalled, Baghdad now depends on the Kurdish-controlled line to Ceyhan to move Iraqi crude, reversing the balance of power that has long favored the federal government. Yet Erbil says Baghdad has transferred only about 41 percent of the Region's owed budget since 2023 and still owes foreign oil firms more than 1 billion dollars, leaving civil-servant salaries exposed even as the northern route becomes the country's main working artery.
