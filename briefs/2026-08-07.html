Syria's electricity authority signs about a billion dollars in Saudi solar deals

Syria's electricity authority signed three agreements with Saudi Arabia's Al-Harafi Contracting to build solar generation projects worth up to 1 billion dollars, deepening a Gulf-led push to rebuild a grid shattered by war. The contracts add to the wave of energy commitments Damascus has drawn since sanctions were lifted, as it races to restore steady power and court foreign capital. Translated from Enab Baladi.

August 6, 2026 · Security

Presidential team maps the final SDF merger around new army brigades

Syria's presidential team said the January 29 integration accord with the SDF has reached its final stages at all levels, with the plan now built around forming three army brigades in Hasakah and one in Ain al-Arab that fold the Kurdish-led forces into the Defense Ministry. Officials cast the structure as dismantling the group's separate command and weapons control, while SDF commander Mazloum Abdi flagged mine clearance near Ras al-Ain as the main obstacle to returning displaced residents.

Iraq

August 6, 2026 · Energy High impact

Trump threatens to hit Iran hard as the Hormuz shipping deal stays unsigned

President Trump said the Strait of Hormuz would reopen soon and warned the United States would hit Iran really hard if it did not, even as Tehran's deputy foreign minister said the Iran-Oman commercial shipping framework was still only close to being finalized and Iran's lead negotiator mocked Trump's repeated reversals. The standoff keeps the strait effectively shut on its 158th day, a blockade that has choked roughly 94 percent of Iraq's crude exports and the near 90 percent of the federal budget they fund, with an eighth Saudi tanker struck since the closure began.

August 6, 2026 · Public Finance

Baghdad weighs a 10 trillion dinar borrowing law to keep paying salaries

Iraq is moving toward a borrowing law worth about 10 trillion dinars, near 7.6 billion dollars, to cover public-sector wages through the rest of 2026 as the Hormuz-driven oil slump guts revenue. The state spends roughly 6.5 billion dollars a month on salaries, pensions and welfare, while monthly oil income has fallen to about 2.5 trillion dinars against the 10.8 trillion needed, leaving July pay still undelivered across several ministries.

Kuwait

August 5, 2026 · Energy

Kuwait rebuilds crude output to near 2 million barrels a day in July

Kuwait lifted average crude output to 1.971 million barrels a day in July, up from about 1.65 million in June and a May low near 580,000, a Reuters source said, as Gulf producers claw back volumes even with the Strait of Hormuz standoff unresolved. The rebound, run in part on tankers sailing with their tracking signals switched off to dodge Iranian targeting, still trails the 2.5 million barrels Kuwait pumped before the strait was shut in late February.

Kurdistan

August 6, 2026 · Oil & Gas

SOMO says Kurdistan's oil exports have collapsed to 20,000 barrels a day

A senior SOMO official said attacks on oil fields and the wider regional crisis have driven Kurdistan Region output and exports down from 230,000 barrels a day to just 20,000, with the Hormuz closure hitting flows across both Erbil and Baghdad. Iraq sold only 42 million barrels of crude in July, including 7 million shipped through the Kurdistan pipeline to Ceyhan, far below the roughly 105 million a month it moved before the crisis.

Kurdistan Region needs 748.5 million dollars to cover August salaries

The Kurdistan Region needs 748.5 million dollars to pay its August public-sector salaries, a source told Shafaq News, as the Erbil-Baghdad transfer mechanism strains under the collapse in oil income. The figure sharpens the pay dispute that runs through the oil-for-salaries deal, with the Region handing over crude and domestic revenue while Baghdad's own coffers run dry.
