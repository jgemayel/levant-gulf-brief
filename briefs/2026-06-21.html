Syria

June 19, 2026 · Security

Israeli forces push at least 1.5 km into Syria's Quneitra countryside, destroying around 15 homes and farmland in the deepest incursion of the week

Israeli forces advanced at least 1.5 kilometers into several towns in Syria's Quneitra countryside on June 19, destroying around 15 homes along with farmland and orchards, after an overnight raid in which nine military vehicles entered the village of Al-Asbah and searched houses before pulling back. Damascus called the operation another violation of the 1974 Disengagement Agreement that set up the UN-monitored buffer zone along the occupied Golan, and the near-daily incursions have become a steady pressure point on the al-Sharaa government in the south.

Arab News
June 20, 2026 · Security

Islamic State kills two Syrian soldiers near Manbij, extending a wave of attacks on the al-Sharaa government across the northeastern Aleppo corridor

Islamic State claimed an attack near Manbij in northeastern Aleppo province on June 20 that the Syrian Defence Ministry said killed two of its soldiers, the latest in a run of assaults the group has staged since announcing a new operational phase against President Ahmed al-Sharaa's forces in February. Reuters could not independently verify the claim, which landed on the group's Telegram channel, but the pace of IS ambushes in the Manbij belt points to an active insurgency the new state has yet to contain.

Jerusalem Post
June 20, 2026 · Politics

Syria opens the first citizenship interviews for stateless Kurds under Decree 13, the first concrete rollout of a landmark minority-rights reform

The Hasakah and Raqqa governorates published the first batch of interview schedules on Saturday for Kurds applying for Syrian nationality under Presidential Decree No. 13 of 2026, which scraps the exceptional measures tied to the 1962 Hasakah census and grants citizenship to Kurds long left stateless. The Interior Ministry says 2,892 family applications covering 10,516 individuals have come in since the process opened on April 6, marking the first on-the-ground step of one of the al-Sharaa government's signature reforms and a piece of the wider SDF and Damascus integration track.

June 20, 2026 · Economy

Syria cuts the subsidized bread bundle from ten loaves to eight, deepening a 16-month squeeze on in-kind support amid widespread food insecurity

Syria's Ministry of Economy and Industry cut the subsidized bread bundle from 10 loaves to 8 effective Saturday, June 20, holding the price fixed at 40 new pounds, about 28 cents, the latest step in a 16-month run of reductions that has taken the bundle from 1,500 grams down to 1,000. The squeeze lands on a population where roughly 9.1 million people face food insecurity and the UN World Food Programme has already halved emergency aid, sharpening the cost-of-living pressure the al-Sharaa government is managing.

June 18, 2026 · Rights

Detention of British-Syrian activist Hassan Akkad in Damascus sparks an outcry over free expression under the new government

Security forces detained British-Syrian activist Hassan Akkad at a Damascus cafe late on June 17 over a complaint tied to his social-media criticism of a pro-government presenter, holding him before a public prosecutor confirmed the case was later dropped. Akkad, a BAFTA and Emmy winner who runs a campaign chasing pledged reconstruction funds, drew an immediate wave of protest that turned his arrest into an early test of how far free expression extends under President al-Sharaa.

Iraq

June 19, 2026 · Finance High impact

FATF adds Iraq to its grey list for the first time since 2018, putting the banking sector back under increased monitoring

The Financial Action Task Force added Iraq to its grey list of jurisdictions under increased monitoring at its June 19 plenary, Iraq's first return to the list since it exited in 2018, citing weak money-laundering and terror-financing investigations, heavy reliance on cash, and underuse of financial intelligence. Baghdad has committed to an action plan to close the gaps, but the listing raises compliance costs and correspondent-banking friction for Iraqi banks, with the IMF estimating that grey-listing can cut a country's foreign capital inflows by around 7.6 percent of GDP.

June 20, 2026 · Energy

Iraq orders its five biggest southern oil fields back to full capacity, targeting 3 million barrels a day as the US-Iran deal reopens Hormuz

Basra Oil Company ordered operators of the Rumaila, West Qurna-1, West Qurna-2, Zubair, and Artawi fields to lift output to maximum capacity in a document dated June 19, aiming to return above 3 million barrels a day after the US-Iran agreement reopened the Strait of Hormuz. Southern production is already recovering to about 1.5 million barrels a day from roughly 900,000 a month ago, though the Oil Ministry says the full ramp will be gradual over one to two months and depends on buyers arranging tankers while war-risk shipping costs stay elevated.

June 19, 2026 · Security

Reuters reports the IRGC set up covert Iraqi cells that launched the drone attacks on Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE during the war

Reuters reported that the IRGC set up three or four covert cells of around 10 elite Iraqi fighters each, operating outside the established militia command, that launched at least seven drone attacks from desert sites near Basra and Samawa against Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE between April 20 and May 17. Targets included Kuwait's Ali al-Salem air base and the military terminal at Kuwait's international airport, with the structure built to give Iran plausible deniability and ease US pressure to disarm the main factions; Iraqi officials and the Iran-aligned groups rejected the account.

Kuwait

June 19, 2026 · Energy High impact

Kuwait lifts all wartime oil force majeure and races output back toward 2 million barrels a day as the Strait of Hormuz reopens

Kuwait Petroleum Corporation lifted all force majeure notices issued during the war with immediate effect and said it would push output back above 2 million barrels a day within about a week, after production had collapsed to roughly 576,000 barrels in May, down about 78 percent, when the Strait of Hormuz was choked off. CEO Sheikh Nawaf Saud Al-Sabah said repairs are far enough along to ramp quickly and KPC has already floated a July export tender, making the restart the single biggest economic event of Kuwait's post-war week given that oil funds the bulk of state revenue.

The National
June 18, 2026 · Aviation

International airlines return to Kuwait International Airport via Terminal 4, two weeks after the deadly June 3 drone strike

International carriers including Emirates, flydubai, and Air Arabia resumed flights at Kuwait International Airport on June 18, operating through Terminal 4 more than two weeks after a June 3 Iranian drone strike on Terminal 1 killed one person and damaged the building. Only national carriers Kuwait Airways and Jazeera Airways had been flying since the attack, so the return of foreign airlines marks the effective reopening of the country's main international gateway as the wider Gulf recovers from the conflict.

June 20, 2026 · Diplomacy

Kuwait airlifts 334 citizens home from Iran in its first evacuation flight, busing them through Turkmenistan

A Kuwait Airways evacuation flight repatriated 334 citizens stranded in Iran on Saturday, June 20, the first such airlift, busing nationals from Mashhad across the border into Turkmenistan before a roughly 25-hour journey into Terminal 4. The operation, run under the Foreign Ministry's emergency plan, closes one of the last loose ends of the war for Kuwait as oil, aviation, and now citizens make their way back home.

Times Kuwait
June 19, 2026 · Markets

Boursa Kuwait posts its highest liquidity of 2026 as trading jumps to 220 million dinars on index rebalancing

Boursa Kuwait liquidity jumped 40.3 percent to 220 million dinars on Thursday, June 19, its highest of 2026, with volume up 23.1 percent to 811.6 million shares as portfolios rebalanced around the periodic S&P and FTSE Russell index reviews. Banking stocks took about 42 percent of the turnover, a sign of how quickly Kuwaiti markets steadied once the ceasefire and the Hormuz reopening lifted the war-risk overhang.

Kurdistan

June 17, 2026 · Fiscal

KRG cabinet presses Baghdad on unpaid pensions, equal wheat payments for Kurdish farmers, and the region's share of federal spending

The KRG Council of Ministers, chaired by Prime Minister Masrour Barzani, met on June 17 and directed ministries to coordinate with Baghdad on starting pension payments for retirees still going unpaid, pressed for Kurdish farmers to be treated equally under Iraq's national wheat-procurement program, and reasserted the region's claim to a fair share of federal sovereign and food-security spending. The readout is the clearest current statement of the KRG's financial demands on Baghdad as the salary and budget-transfer disputes drag on.

June 20, 2026 · Politics

Masoud Barzani backs a proposed all-Iraq governors' conference in Erbil, positioning the region as a center-region convening hub

KDP leader Masoud Barzani publicly backed Prime Minister Masrour Barzani's proposal to convene all of Iraq's provincial governors in Erbil, calling it a positive step for national development, after Baghdad Governor Atwan al-Atwani spent several days meeting KRG leaders on infrastructure and federal cooperation. The endorsement, issued Saturday, casts Erbil as a coordination hub between the center and the provinces even as the tenth KRG cabinet remains unformed since the October 2024 election.

Kurdistan24
June 21, 2026 · Energy

July 27 expiry of the Iraq-Turkey pipeline treaty hangs over a Kurdish export route only just recovering from the war

The 1973 agreement governing crude exports through the Iraq-Turkey pipeline to Ceyhan expires on July 27, 2026, after Ankara triggered its one-year exit clause, and Turkish and Iraqi officials say talks on a replacement are still unresolved with weeks to go. The deadline hangs over a Kurdish export route only just recovering from the war, when drone strikes cut KRG flows to as little as 20,000 to 40,000 barrels a day, leaving the region's primary oil lifeline legally uncertain just as Baghdad pushes nationwide output back up.
