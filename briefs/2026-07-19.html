Syria

July 18, 2026 · Economy

Syria becomes the Middle East's fuel lifeline as Iraqi oil pours overland around Hormuz

Thousands of trucks are now hauling Iraqi fuel oil across Syria to its Mediterranean ports, and in a matter of months the country has gone from shipping none of the product to making up more than a quarter of Middle Eastern export volumes as the closed Strait of Hormuz forces flows onto land. The loads take about four days to reach Baniyas and Tartus, handing post-Assad Damascus a central role in the region's energy map exactly as it courts Gulf and Western investment. Bloomberg
July 17, 2026 · Economy

Damascus sets a September reconstruction fair as it presses for investment through the war

Syria will host the second International Reconstruction Exhibition from September 13 to 16 at the Damascus Fairgrounds, drawing more than 270 companies from 23 countries, with Finance Minister Mohammed Yisr Barnieh pitching promising openings across sectors and a rewrite of the legislative framework to convert interest into projects. The plan lands as the regional war batters supply lines, underscoring the government's bet that reconstruction demand can pull in capital even amid the fighting.

Iraq

July 18, 2026 · Security High impact

The US-Iran war turns deadlier as an Iranian strike kills two US troops at a Jordan base

An Iranian ballistic missile attack on the Muwaffaq Salti air base in Jordan killed two US service members and left one missing on July 18, CENTCOM said, with four more evacuated to hospital as Washington pressed an eighth consecutive night of strikes on Iran and the war's US death toll climbed to 16. The escalation tightens the vise on Iraq, which hosts the last US forces due to leave by September 30 and is straining to hold the neutral line Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi carried to Washington last week.

CNN
July 18, 2026 · Energy

Iraq's grid nears collapse as gas supply craters and southern exports stay choked

Baghdad is warning of a fresh 1,400 megawatt loss after Dana Gas suspended its Kurdistan operations over credible security threats, piling onto gas imports that have fallen to roughly 5 million cubic meters a day against the 30 million the grid needs at peak summer. With Hormuz shut, southern seaborne crude exports remain down more than 90 percent from pre-war levels, cutting off the hard currency Iraq would use to buy alternative fuel and leaving the power sector exposed as demand peaks.

Kuwait

July 18, 2026 · Security

Kuwait's water and power take a direct hit as an Iranian barrage damages a desalination plant

Kuwaiti air defenses engaged a fresh wave of Iranian missiles and drones early on July 18, but a strike on a combined power generation and water desalination plant sparked a fire and knocked out several generating units, the first hit on the country's critical water infrastructure in the war. Airport operations were suspended during the attack and several Land Force personnel were wounded, exposing how thin the margin has grown for a desert state that leans on desalination for its drinking water.

Kurdistan

July 18, 2026 · Security High impact

An IRGC missile strike kills nine Komala fighters at their camps in the Kurdistan Region

Six Iranian ballistic missiles slammed into camps of the exiled Iranian-Kurdish opposition group Komala in the Kurdistan Region around dawn on July 18, killing nine peshmerga, with a second strike on a nearby base wounding two more, the group said. The Kurdistan Regional Government condemned what it called Iran's unjustified attacks and urged an end to the escalation, as Tehran folded its long campaign against Kurdish opposition bases into the wider war raging across the region.

Arab News
July 18, 2026 · Economy

Baghdad sends Erbil 841 billion dinars for June pay but withholds part over the revenue split

The federal Finance Ministry transferred 841 billion dinars, about 641.7 million dollars, to the KRG to cover June public sector wages, but held back 120 billion dinars because Erbil again fell short of the non-oil revenue it owes Baghdad each month. With local income sliding, KRG officials say the Region may have to borrow to close the payroll gap, keeping salaries for more than a million civil servants hostage to a revenue-sharing deal that grows harder to honor as the war squeezes oil flows.
