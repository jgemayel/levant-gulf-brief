Syria

August 13, 2026 · Diplomacy

Damascus takes back Russian-run civilian sites on the coast

Syria has regained control of civilian facilities on its coast that had been operated by Russia, even as a Rossotrudnichestvo delegation visited on August 13 and agreed that the Russian House cultural center in Damascus would resume operations. The handover narrows Moscow's civilian footprint while keeping channels open, part of al-Sharaa's effort to recalibrate the Assad-era relationship into something narrower and on Syrian terms. It lands as Damascus widens its Arab and Western reintegration and keeps up security talks with Israel.

Cairo backs Syria's stability and calls the relationship normal

Egypt publicly threw its weight behind Syria's stability and described relations with Damascus as normal, a warming signal from Cairo as al-Sharaa's government presses its return to the Arab fold. Iraq

August 14, 2026 · Security High impact

Washington vows to hold the Iran blockade indefinitely as the ceasefire collapses

US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said on August 13 the Navy can keep its blockade of Iranian ports in place "indefinitely" by rotating ships in and out, as a tentative June ceasefire fell apart and Tehran kept the Strait of Hormuz effectively shut. Iran's Revolutionary Guard countered that no tanker crosses without its permission, and an adviser warned Iran could prolong the war until President Trump leaves office, leaving Iraq's Gulf export lifeline throttled with Brent slipping below 88 dollars a barrel. About a fifth of the world's oil and LNG moved through the strait before the fighting began in late February.

RTE
August 13, 2026 · Energy

A lawmaker accuses SOMO of refusing to ship crude through Hormuz

Hiyam al-Yasiri, a lawmaker in the Reconstruction and Development bloc, accused the Oil Ministry and marketer SOMO of refusing to move crude through the Strait of Hormuz on Iraqi tankers despite what she called American and Iranian security approvals, bleeding revenue as salary transfers slip. The ministry denied blocking any exports, saying cargoes sail when buyers accept the passage risk, with output near 2.7 million barrels a day and exports running far below capacity. The clash turns the closure into a domestic political fight over who is to blame for the throttled flow.

Kuwait

August 13, 2026 · Economy

Kuwait's downturn deepens as the blocked strait chokes oil income

With the Strait of Hormuz still shut, Kuwait's economy contracted 4.6 percent year on year in the first quarter as oil-sector output collapsed 12.5 percent, and the finance ministry projects a 2026/27 budget deficit near 9.8 billion dinars, up almost 55 percent on the prior year. Officials call it the biggest crisis the oil sector has faced, with a breakeven around 90 dollars towering over crude assumed at 68 and nearly all exports hostage to the blocked waterway. Kuwait has leaned on borrowing and spending restraint to absorb the shock while output claws back month by month.

Kurdistan

August 13, 2026 · Energy

166 MPs press Baghdad for a bigger crude share to break the fuel crisis

A petition signed by 166 members of Iraq's parliament, led by KDP lawmaker Sipan Sherwani, urges Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi's government to lift the Kurdistan Region's domestic crude allocation from 50,000 barrels a day toward the roughly 126,700 barrels its 12.67 percent population share would justify, or to hand over an equivalent volume of gasoline. Kurdish MPs argue the larger allocation would expand subsidized fuel output and cool benzine prices that have spiked to record highs despite the price caps the KRG imposed in July.

Kurdistan24
August 13, 2026 · Economy

The KRG issues new pricing directives as the gasoline squeeze holds

The Kurdistan Regional Government rolled out fresh pricing directives to steady a fuel market where super-grade petrol has pushed past 2,000 dinars a liter, around 1.32 dollars, in Erbil and Duhok. The move follows July price caps that failed to clear the shortages, leaving drivers in long queues as the war-driven import squeeze and a thin domestic crude allocation keep pumps starved. Erbil is pairing the retail measures with its push in Baghdad for more subsidized crude.

August 12, 2026 · Security

Iranian drones fall near Erbil homes as the barrage grinds on

Renewed blasts and drone activity struck Erbil province on the night of August 12, with Iranian drones reported crashing near civilian homes across the governorate. The incidents extend a months-long run of missiles and drones launched from Iran or by Iran-aligned militias inside Iraq, keeping residents on edge and reinforcing Prime Minister Masrour Barzani's warning that the Region is being dragged into a war it never sought. The strikes have battered oil fields, power plants, and border crossings, compounding the fuel and fiscal squeeze.
