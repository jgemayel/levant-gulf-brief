Syria

August 27, 2026 · Politics

Al-Sharaa names Mazloum Abdi a presidential adviser, days after the SDF stood down

President Ahmed al-Sharaa issued Decree No 164 appointing former Syrian Democratic Forces commander Mazloum Abdi as an adviser to the presidency, giving the Kurdish leader a formal seat in Damascus barely two days after he announced the SDF's dissolution. A second decree is expected to name Autonomous Administration co-chair Ilham Ahmed to Syria's People's Assembly, folding the two most senior northeast figures into state institutions. The move turns the paper integration into personnel, even as command and deployment questions over the northeast stay open.

Al Jazeera
August 27, 2026 · Economy

Saudi firms sign about a billion dollars of energy deals at the Damascus fair

Saudi companies signed one agreement and six memoranda with Syria's Ministry of Energy at the Damascus International Fair, led by an ACWA Power deal to study up to 1,000 megawatts of solar and 1,500 megawatts of wind and to assess and rehabilitate existing power plants and the national grid. The Syrian side put the solar package at roughly a billion dollars, with the Saudi Electricity Company, TAQA, ADES, Arabian Drilling, and ARGAS adding MoUs across generation, oilfield development, drilling, and geophysical surveys. It is one of the largest reconstruction bets on Syria since sanctions relief and deepens Riyadh's footprint in the new government's energy sector.

Iraq

August 27, 2026 · Energy

Baghdad presses a Qatar and Chevron consortium to speed pipelines that skirt Hormuz

Oil Minister Basim Mohammed Khudair met a delegation from Qatar's UCC on the proposed Basra to Haditha to Fishkhabur and Haditha to Baniyas export lines, urging faster contracting and the removal of obstacles as the two sides move to weekly meetings on the economic model and route. UCC leads a consortium including Chevron, TI Capital, and TotalEnergies on the project, whose new lines are estimated at around 15 billion dollars and are meant to give Iraq export outlets that do not depend on the Strait of Hormuz. With crude flows through the strait still throttled, the pipeline push is Baghdad's clearest hedge against the chokepoint.

Iraq Business News
August 27, 2026 · Security

Baghdad and the factions edge toward a weapons-handover compromise before September 30

With the September 30 deadline closing in, Iran-aligned factions have signaled they will begin handing weapons to the state and negotiators report the two sides are nearing a compromise on the mechanics, a shift from weeks of flat refusal. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi says all political forces now agree on bringing arms under state control, but Kataib Hezbollah still ties any deal to a full withdrawal of US forces from Iraqi soil and airspace and says the July strikes on the Popular Mobilization Forces are grounds to keep its weapons. The gap between a public consensus and the hardliners' conditions is what still has to be bridged.

Kuwait

August 27, 2026 · Energy High impact

Kuwait and Qatar claw oil exports back to 70 percent of pre-war levels through Hormuz

Traders say Kuwait and Qatar have restored crude exports to about 70 percent of pre-war levels, from a combined 2 million barrels a day before the Iran war, by running a shuttle trade that moves oil through the strait and transfers cargoes onto other ships outside it. Kuwait is leaning on its own fleet of 11 supertankers, most of which have gone dark with transponders off for more than two months to mask the risky crossings. The rebound eases a total export halt that had paralyzed the sector, though it still leaves roughly a third of Kuwaiti barrels stranded.

Kuwait reviews soaring shipping costs as Hormuz disruption reroutes cargo

The Ministry of Commerce and Industry met shipping companies to weigh how higher transport, freight, and logistics costs are feeding into the price of imported goods, with carriers now using alternative routes and ports to keep supplies flowing. Officials framed the session around the Strait of Hormuz and its drag on maritime traffic serving Kuwait. The review points to growing worry about import inflation as the war reshapes the trade lanes the country depends on.

Kurdistan

August 27, 2026 · Fiscal

Erbil and Baghdad open six-file talks as the federal PM pledges equal salary funding

KRG and federal delegations met in Baghdad on a six-file agenda covering civil servant salaries, non-oil internal revenues, oil export mechanisms, the return of foreign oil companies, dam projects, and the ASYCUDA customs rollout at border crossings. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi committed to funding Kurdistan Region salaries on equal terms with the rest of Iraq and to guarantees on oil, the clearest federal pledge in a payroll dispute that has run for years. Delivery on those promises, repeatedly the sticking point, is what will decide whether the talks hold.

Shafaq News
August 27, 2026 · Energy

The KRG pushes to more than double its crude allocation to ease the fuel crisis

Erbil has asked Baghdad to raise the Region's daily crude allocation from 50,000 to 126,700 barrels, a figure tied to Kurdistan's 12.67 percent population share, to lift local refining and relieve the gasoline shortages emptying pumps in Erbil and Duhok. Iraq's oil minister called the increase straightforward and said talks are underway, with initial signals of support from the federal Oil Ministry. A bigger allocation would let the KRG feed its own refineries rather than ration a supply meeting a fraction of demand.
