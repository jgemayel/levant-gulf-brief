Syria

August 13, 2026 · Diplomacy

Cairo calls its ties with Damascus normal and backs Syria's stability

Egyptian Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty said relations between Cairo and Damascus are normal and progressing, calling Syria's security and stability an Egyptian and Arab interest and backing the unity of its institutions. He pressed for an inclusive political process that excludes no faction and for counterterrorism steps that keep Syrian soil from becoming a threat to neighbors, and said he intends to visit Damascus without setting a date. The comments mark another step in Cairo's cautious reengagement with President Ahmed al-Sharaa's government after long wariness of its Islamist roots. Translated from Arabic.

August 13, 2026 · Politics

Damascus says the SDF merger has reached its final stages

The Syrian presidency's team says the January 29 integration accord with the Syrian Democratic Forces has reached its final stages at all levels, with the military and security tracks furthest along and the remaining work focused on folding SDF units and administrative bodies into state structures. Government and SDF sources now suggest a formal dissolution of both the SDF and the Autonomous Administration could be announced before long, a shift that would end the parallel authority governing the northeast. The claim of near-completion sits alongside earlier warnings that the justice, education, and civil files still lag the disarmament timetable. Translated from Arabic.

Iraq

August 14, 2026 · Energy High impact

Iraq's daily oil exports climb to 2 million barrels, the most since the war began

Oil Minister Bassem Mohammed Khudair said Iraq's exports have averaged 2 million barrels per day since the start of August, about 26 million barrels in all, the first time daily flows have hit that rate since the crisis began on February 28. The level is still roughly half the pre-war average of around 105 million barrels a month, and Baghdad is leaning on the Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline to Turkey and overland trucking to Syria and Jordan while the Strait of Hormuz stays throttled. The rebound eases, without ending, a revenue squeeze that has left the government struggling to cover a public wage bill of more than 6 billion dollars a month.

August 14, 2026 · Security

Iran strikes two more ADNOC tankers in Hormuz as the UAE cries piracy

Two vessels operated by Abu Dhabi National Oil Company were attacked while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on the evening of Thursday, August 13, with no injuries reported and the situation brought under control. The UAE foreign ministry blamed Iran for the strike and called Tehran's attempts to weaponize the waterway an act of piracy, the second hit on ADNOC ships in under a week. The attacks keep the same passage that carries the bulk of Iraq's Basra crude under fire, reinforcing why Baghdad is racing to shift barrels onto northern and overland routes. Kuwait

August 14, 2026 · Energy

KPC pushes offshore megaprojects and a 4 million barrel capacity goal

Kuwait Petroleum Corporation said its strategic oil and gas projects are advancing, with Kuwait Oil Company hitting its production target of 2.72 million barrels per day for a second straight year and the offshore Jazah and Nokhitha fields set to open to international partners. Work on the shared Durra field is expected to move to tenders this year for a development holding roughly 20 trillion cubic feet of gas and 310 million barrels of oil, targeting 1 billion cubic feet of gas per day by 2029. The corporation is holding to a goal of lifting total capacity to 4 million barrels per day by 2035 even as the war chokes its Gulf export routes.

Reuters
August 14, 2026 · Security

A Sunday deadline looms as the US and Iran each claim Hormuz

A Sunday deadline for a memorandum meant to govern passage through the Strait of Hormuz is closing in with Washington and Tehran both asserting control of the waterway and each applying economic pressure to make the other blink. Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority insists no vessel may transit without its permit, while the United States maintains it holds the passage, leaving traffic near record lows as importers draw down stockpiles. No Gulf producer is more exposed than Kuwait, whose crude and refined exports move almost entirely through the strait and remain largely paralyzed until a corridor reopens.

Kurdistan

August 13, 2026 · Economy

Baghdad and Erbil call ties their highest ever as budget talks turn to 2027

A financial adviser to Iraqi Prime Minister Mohammed Ali al-Zaidi told Rudaw that Baghdad's relationship with the Kurdistan Region is at its highest levels, with unprecedented coordination now shaping the 2027 budget despite the oil-export shocks battering federal revenue. He said that once the Region's exports reach 400,000 barrels per day the salary problem would disappear, and that Baghdad has released May salaries while June and July payments remain pending. The Region has been delivering an average of about 220,000 barrels per day to federal marketer SOMO under the tripartite export deal, with pay still tied to exports, non-oil revenue transfers, and salary nationalization.

August 14, 2026 · Security

Three bomb-laden drones intercepted over Erbil airport

Coalition air defenses shot down three bomb-laden drones over Erbil in the early hours of Friday, August 14, with the region's counterterrorism directorate reporting the intercepts between 2:17 and 2:25 am and no casualties or damage. The drones targeted US troop positions near Erbil International Airport, the latest in a run of strikes that has hit the Kurdistan Region hundreds of times since the US-Iran confrontation escalated. The barrage keeps Erbil on edge even as its government presses Baghdad on salaries and oil, wary of being pulled deeper into a war it says it never sought.
