Syria

August 18, 2026 · Security High impact

Israeli warplanes crater a Syrian airbase runway in Idlib to block a Turkish deployment

Eight Israeli strikes hit the Abu al-Duhur airbase in Idlib's eastern countryside early on Tuesday, damaging the runway but causing no casualties, Syrian state media said, shortly after a Turkish military delegation reportedly toured the base to assess its rehabilitation. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office said Israel acted because Damascus was on the verge of breaching an agreed security status quo by letting Turkish troops deploy near Aleppo, while Turkey denied any presence and Syria's foreign ministry condemned an unjustified attack. US envoy for Syria Tom Barrack called the raids an unnecessary escalation that does not advance regional stability, a rare public rebuke of an Israeli operation.

Iraq

August 18, 2026 · Energy

Iraq plans a tanker-fleet overhaul to push crude past the closed strait

Iraq's oil ministry unveiled a strategic overhaul of the state Iraqi Oil Tanker Company, using partnerships with tanker owners, deferred-payment purchases, and leasing to build out a modern fleet capable of moving oil beyond the Strait of Hormuz. The company currently owns just six aging vessels of limited capacity, and the ministry wants large carriers of up to 2 million barrels each to reduce its dependence on Gulf terminals throttled by the blockade. The push comes as Baghdad claws August exports back toward 2 million barrels a day, still well short of the roughly 4 million it pumped before the war. See the Kuwait section.

Kuwait

August 18, 2026 · Energy High impact

The Hormuz standoff hardens as Iran ties any reopening to sanctions relief

A day after the 60-day US-Iran window lapsed, Tehran's lead negotiator said on Tuesday the Strait of Hormuz will stay closed to all shipping until Washington lifts its port blockade and oil sanctions, extinguishing near-term hopes of a reopening. Britain's UK Maritime Trade Operations flagged a vessel struck by an unknown projectile while trying to exit the Gulf the same day, with damage and one crew casualty, and Kpler counted just six commodity ships crossing the strait against a 10-day average of 11. Kuwait, whose barrels have no outlet but the Gulf, stays bottled behind the standoff with no alternate export route to fall back on. See the Iraq and Kurdistan sections.

Kurdistan

August 18, 2026 · Economy

The Region needs 748 million dollars for August salaries as Baghdad's transfer stalls

A source told Shafaq News the Kurdistan Region requires about 748.5 million dollars to cover August public-sector salaries, still unpaid as Baghdad withholds the monthly payroll transfer over the long-running oil and revenue dispute. Erbil says it has met its side of the tripartite deal by delivering crude to SOMO and remitting non-oil revenue, yet the federal government has released only a fraction of the Region's entitlements, leaving more than a million civil servants exposed. The squeeze deepens as the war throttles trade and drone strikes keep hammering the Region's power and energy infrastructure. See the Iraq section.

Shafaq News
August 18, 2026 · Security

World capitals condemn the drone strike on Barzani's office as Tehran denies it

The United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union, Saudi Arabia, and several regional governments condemned the August 17 drone attack on Prime Minister Masrour Barzani's private office and the intelligence chief's home, as Baghdad pressed Tehran for answers and Iran denied responsibility. A local tally counted 16 drones and three missiles fired at the Kurdistan Region in the first two weeks of August alone, part of a bombardment that has run past 950 launches since the war began. Erbil says the strikes will not drag it into a war it did not choose, even as the direct targeting of the head of its government marks the sharpest escalation yet.
