Syria

August 25, 2026 · Security High impact

The Kurdish-led SDF formally dissolves as its merger into the Syrian army completes

Commander Mazloum Abdi went to the People's Palace in Damascus and, alongside President Ahmed al-Sharaa, announced that the Syrian Democratic Forces have ended their mission and dissolved as an independent military body after completing integration into the national army. It closes roughly a decade of Kurdish-led autonomous command in the northeast, months after the force lost most of its territory to a government offensive in January and then signed the March merger deal. The step turns the earlier declaration of integration into a formal wind-down of the SDF as a standing force.

August 26, 2026 · Economy

Damascus opens its first international fair since sanctions relief

The 63rd Damascus International Fair opened today and runs to September 4, drawing more than 1,000 companies from about 60 countries alongside over 40 Syrian ministries and public bodies, under President al-Sharaa's patronage. It is the first edition since Washington removed Syria from the terrorism list and lifted the last major barrier to investment, and Damascus is pitching it as a platform to accelerate recovery and attract capital across industry, construction, food, and technology. August 24, 2026 · Justice

A Damascus court jails Assad's former grand mufti for life

The Fourth Criminal Court in Damascus sentenced Ahmad Badreddin Hassoun, grand mufti under Bashar al-Assad from 2005 until the post was abolished in 2021, to life in prison for inciting civil war and sectarian strife, with added terms of 20 years for incitement to killing and 10 years for participation in killing. The court found that the mufti's office had provided religious cover for regime military operations, including the use of barrel bombs against populated areas. It is one of the most senior Assad-era convictions handed down so far.

Iraq

August 25, 2026 · Security

Baghdad pushes back its September 30 disarmament deadline as Iran floats a weapons freeze

A senior Iraqi official pushed back the September 30 date for pro-Iran factions to hand over their weapons without setting a new one, as talks converge on a face-saving formula rather than a full handover to the state. Iranian parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, on his Baghdad visit, floated freezing the factions' heavy weapons and possibly moving them to Iran until a political settlement is reached. Kataib Hezbollah and other hardliners still refuse, with Quds Force commander Esmail Qaani reported to have urged them to keep their arms.

Asharq Al-Awsat
August 24, 2026 · Oil & Gas High impact

A Gulf shadow fleet keeps Iraqi crude flowing as Iran loses its grip on Hormuz

Vessel-tracking data show Iraq and its Gulf neighbours now moving well over 4 million barrels a day through a covert network of transponder-dark tankers that run the Strait of Hormuz and transfer cargoes ship to ship off Oman, with roughly 150 vessels gathered there against about 40 in January. Iraq still exported around 35.5 million barrels from the south in July, far below the 105 million barrels a month it shipped before the shipping crisis, but the workaround has blunted Iran's leverage over the waterway. The reframing turns an earlier plan to route around a closed strait into a system running close to full tilt.

Kuwait

August 24, 2026 · Oil & Gas

Kuwait charters dark tankers to keep its exports moving through a contested strait

Kuwait is chartering very large crude carriers to sail out of the Gulf with their transponders switched off, then transferring oil to customers' tankers in the Gulf of Oman, part of the same Gulf-wide shadow system now carrying more than 4 million barrels a day past Hormuz (see Iraq). The scramble protects the export revenue that underpins a budget already projecting a deficit near 9.8 billion dinars for 2026-27, up almost 55 percent, with oil still supplying about 79 percent of state income. Keeping barrels on the water is now central to Kuwait's fiscal footing as the regional war grinds on.

Kurdistan

August 25, 2026 · Energy

A fuel crisis empties Erbil and Duhok pumps as the KRG demands a bigger crude share

Most private stations in Erbil and Duhok have run dry, with drivers queuing overnight, after Pearl Petroleum cut output at the Khor Mor gas field following mid-July drone strikes on energy infrastructure. The KRG has formally asked Baghdad to lift its daily crude allocation to 126,700 barrels from 50,000, a rise of more than 150 percent, citing Kurdistan's 12.67 percent population share under the federal budget law. Price caps set regular petrol near 850 dinars a litre, but shortages have persisted and pushed super grade well above that.

August 25, 2026 · Politics

Nechirvan Barzani renews the compensation demand on the Badinan Anfal anniversary

Marking the 38th anniversary of the Anfal campaign against Badinan, which ran from August 25 into September 1988, Region President Nechirvan Barzani renewed his call for the federal government to deliver full compensation to victims' families and to treat the recognised genocide as an outstanding legal obligation. He tied the demand to broader appeals for resolving disputes with Baghdad and protecting stability across Iraq and the Region. The statement lands as Erbil and Baghdad remain locked over budget transfers and crude.
